# Round 1 — after C1–C3, D1–D4 and N2 were answered (2026-09-28)

**The answers that opened these questions:** C1, C2, C3 confirmed · D1 **B** (a warning) · D2 **B** (a message to the Pariwar Admin) ·
D3 **A** (wait for the fraud register) · D4 **B** (keep the true nominee's new claim separate) · N2 **only the correction**.

**FQ1 — D1: should an approved correction also get the warning?**
When two staff approve a correction after the death, the corrected entry **keeps the date of the entry it corrects**, even though the
correction itself was made after the death. The warning needs a rule for these.

| | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| A | No warning on an approved correction | The two approvals are the only check (the risk already in the 6.20 notes) |
| **B** | **A different, plain label: "corrected after the death — approved by [District Admin] and [Pariwar Admin]"** | None beyond the label |
| C | The same suspicion warning as any other late change | Every genuine correction looks suspicious |

*Our suggestion: **B**.*

**FQ2 — D1: can a claim be approved while the warning shows?**
Today a written reason for approving is optional. The system already requires a reason in one similar case: approving a claim that
carries a concealment flag.

| | Choice |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes, but the District Admin must choose a reason and write a note** |
| B | Yes, with no extra step |

*Our suggestion: **A**.*

**FQ3 — D2: how does the message reach the Pariwar Admin, and what does it say?**
For staff, the system holds only an **email address**, with no phone number. Messaging staff has to be built first; until then, the
list is the only way they find out.

| | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **An email that says "a claim was refused on suspicion of a nominee change — open the list". It carries no names and no note; those stay inside the console** | One extra step to read the details |
| B | An email that includes the District Admin's note and reason | Private details leave the system by email |
| C | A notice inside the admin console only, with no email | They see it only when they log in |

It goes to every Pariwar Admin of that Pariwar. *Our suggestion: **A**.*

**FQ4 — D3: can the service go live before a wrongly locked living member can be unlocked?**
With D3 = A, nothing can unlock them until the fraud register is built. That work waits on legal advice, so no date can be given.

| | Choice |
|---|---|
| A | Go-live waits until an unlock exists |
| **B** | **Go-live may go ahead. A living member wrongly locked stays locked until the fraud register is built** |

*Our suggestion: **B**. A lock only happens after a false death claim against a living member, which should be rare.*

**FQ5 — D4: two claims for one death. Which one gets paid?**
With D4 = B, the true nominee's new claim runs while the refused claim is under appeal. Until now, merging the claims meant only one
claim ran per death. Each approved claim starts **its own collection from members**, and we found no check that stops both being
approved.

| | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The new claim goes through every check but waits at final approval until the appeal is decided. If the appeal is refused, it is approved; if the appeal succeeds, the new claim is closed** | The true nominee waits, but only at the last step |
| B | The new claim may be approved, and approving it ends the appeal | Takes away the appeal the Panel granted (`-239` c) |
| C | Whichever claim is approved first is paid, and the other is closed | A race between staff |

*Our suggestion: **A**.*

# Round 2 — the scenario the Panel asked for, and two gaps it showed (2026-09-28)

**The Panel asked:** *"we want to know the exact steps and scenario, when a person changed the nominee name deliberately after death of
member and filed the claim."* As given (traced through the code on `main`; **NEW** = ruled that day, not built):

**The people (made up):** Ramesh is the member. His wife **Sunita** is his nominee. His nephew **Vikas** has Ramesh's phone.

| # | When | What happens | What the system does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Years before | Ramesh names Sunita as nominee in the app | Records **version 1**, dated by the server's clock |
| 2 | **10 March** | Ramesh dies | Nothing. The system doesn't know yet, so nothing is locked. The declaration locks only when the first claim is filed (`-234` V) |
| 3 | **12 March** | Vikas opens the app on Ramesh's phone and changes the nominee to himself, with **his own mobile number** | A nominee change needs a code sent to **Ramesh's registered mobile**, and Vikas is holding that phone. The system records **version 2, dated 12 March** (the server sets the date) and doesn't stop him |
| 4 | **13 March** | Vikas files the claim in the app and uploads the death certificate | The filing code goes to the **latest** nominee's mobile — **Vikas's own phone**. Once filed, **the declaration is locked for good** |
| 5 | Checking | The District Admin reviews the certificate and records the date of death: **10 March** | The 6.21a certificate-date step |
| 6 | Checking | The District Admin sees **both versions with their dates**. **NEW (D1 B):** version 2 carries a **warning** | The system accepts only one marking: **version 1 stands, version 2 is discarded**. The claim now pays **Sunita**, and the bank check compares the account holder with **Sunita's name** — **Vikas cannot be paid through this claim** |
| 7 | Decision | The District Admin **refuses** with the reason "nominee changed after the death" and a note | The system never refuses by itself. **NEW (FQ2 A):** approving instead, while the warning shows, needs a reason and a note |
| 8 | Same day | **NEW (D2 B, FQ3 A):** every Pariwar Admin of that Pariwar gets an **email** pointing to the list | Today there is only the list |
| 9 | After | Vikas may **appeal once** | The District Admin who refused can't hear it |
| 10 | After | **Sunita starts again** with a **fresh original death certificate** | The **ground inspection carries over**; everything else is given again (C3). **NEW (D4 B, FQ5 A):** her claim is **kept separate**, goes through every check, and **waits at final approval** until Vikas's appeal is decided |

**Gap 1:** the filing code goes to the phone of whoever was named **last** (Vikas), and stays so after the refusal — so **Sunita cannot
file her new claim in the app** (only the helpline works for her) and **Vikas can keep filing new claims in the app.**

**FQ6 — after a refusal on suspicion, where does the app send the filing code?**

| | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| A | No change: Sunita files through the helpline, and staff refuse any further claims from Vikas | Sunita has to know to call the helpline; Vikas can keep filing |
| **B** | **Send the code to the nominee the District Admin found in place at the death (Sunita), never to a discarded one** | New work |

*Our suggestion: **B**.*

**Gap 2:** nobody tells Sunita. The system sends **no message to anyone** when a claim is refused, and FQ3 only emails the Pariwar Admin.

**FQ7 — who tells the true nominee to start again?**

| | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| A | The Pariwar Admin contacts her after the email | Depends on one person remembering |
| **B** | **The system texts the nominee on record at the death, at the mobile from her declaration: "A claim for [member] could not go ahead. Please call the helpline." It accuses no one** | A new text that must be registered first (DLT); her old number may be out of date |

*Our suggestion: **B**.*

**The Panel then asked:** *"Let's say Ramesh died on 10th of March, [Vikas] opened the app on 12th of March and changed the nominee to
himself. He gets a fake certificate dated 13th of March, or he mutilated the death certificate dated 10th of March to 13th of March. What
happens in that scenario?"*

**As answered:** the fraud works. With a certificate dated 13 March, **both versions stand** (both are before the 13th) and the **latest
wins — Vikas**; there is **no warning** (nothing is on or after the 13th); the bank check **passes** (the account is in Vikas's name); and
**members pay straight into Vikas's account**, so nothing can be taken back inside the system. What might still catch it — none
automatic: the District Admin's eye; the ground inspection (free-text notes only, **no date-of-death field**); the check with neighbours
(it asks *that*, ⛔ not *when*); Sunita herself (**nobody tells her** she was replaced); and `-238` after the fact. ⚠ And a change made
**before** the death (e.g. in hospital) is treated as the member's own.

**FQ8 — what should guard against a false date? (more than one may be chosen)**

| | Control | What it catches | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Warn on a recent change:** any nominee change made within **30 days before the claim was filed** gets a warning, whatever the certificate says | Vikas's change, even with a forged date. It **doesn't depend on the certificate** | It also flags genuine last-minute changes by a dying member |
| **B** | **Check the certificate with the government:** the District Admin checks the certificate number on the government's online death register (CRS) and records that they did | Forged and altered certificates, where the register can be searched | A manual step. Not confirmed that every state's certificates can be checked online |
| **C** | **The inspector records the date of death** the family gives, and the system shows any difference from the certificate to the District Admin | The case where the real family is present at the inspection | One extra field on the inspection |
| D | **Text the removed nominee** whenever a nominee is changed | Every change, early, before any claim | A member's private choice becomes known to the person removed |

*Our suggestion: **A and B.** D is a question of values for the Panel; we don't recommend it by default.*

**The Panel then asked:** *"we also had system built where we message five colleagues, what are we asking from them? or is it not
built?"* **As answered:** only half built. The five nearest members are **chosen**; nothing **sends** them anything (the message has no
wording), there is **no way to reply**, and staff can't see replies. Every claim ends its 72 hours with zero replies and falls back to
the ground inspection. It would ask *"do you know this person and do you believe they have died?"* — ⛔ not when. **No sprint row owns
finishing it.** Added to FQ8:

| | Control | What it catches | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E** | **Also ask the five: "If you know, on what date did they die?"** The District Admin is shown where the answers differ from the certificate date by more than a day | Vikas's forged 13 March: neighbours who went to the funeral know it was the 10th | One extra question; answers may be rough, so the system only shows a difference |

# Round 3 — the Panel asked whether a claim waits for the ground inspection (2026-09-28)

**The Panel asked:** *"Do system process the claim without ground inspection or wait for the ground inspection report."*

**As answered:** No. Today a claim can be approved without any ground inspection — approval needs only an accepted certificate, the
marked nominee versions, and the two accounts with a passing name check. The PRD (§4.6, FR-40) says *"both peer mesh confirm AND ground
confirm must be `pass` for the claim to advance to State Trustee approval."* FQ8 C and E only help if they happen **before** approval.

**FQ9 — must the ground inspection be finished before a claim is approved?**

| | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes, for every claim.** The District Admin can't approve until a completed inspection is recorded. **A refusal never waits for it.** If the visit can't happen, the claim **waits** and is not refused for that reason | Every claim waits for a visit |
| B | Only when something is flagged | A fraud that looks clean skips the visit |
| C | Never required; the District Admin decides (as today) | FQ8 C gives no protection |

*Our suggestion: **A**.*

**FQ10 — what does the check with neighbours count for?**

| | Choice | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A signal, not a gate** (as built). Fewer than 3 replies in 72 hours makes the inspection the main check. A reply of "they have not died", or a differing date (FQ8 E), is shown to the District Admin as a warning | The neighbours can never block a claim on their own |
| B | A gate, as the PRD says: approval needs 3 or more confirming replies | **Every claim is blocked** until sending and replies are built |

*Our suggestion: **A**.*

**FQ11 — at the inspection, must the inspector see the original certificate?**

| | Choice |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes. The inspector sees the original certificate, photographs it, and records whether it matches the uploaded copy** |
| B | No; the uploaded copy and the online check are enough |

*Our suggestion: **A**.* `-239` already asks the refiling nominee for an original certificate.

# Round 4 — two points the rulings left open (2026-09-28)

**FQ12 — Does FQ2's rule (a reason and a note to approve) apply to the other warnings too?** That means the 90-day warning, a differing
date of death, and a neighbour saying the member hasn't died. *Our suggestion: yes, one rule for every warning.*

**FQ13 — When the true nominee files again, they bring a fresh original certificate, but the ground inspection they inherit saw the
refused claim's certificate** (in the fraud case, the forged one). **Must an inspector also see the fresh original?** *Our suggestion:
yes, a short visit or an office check limited to the certificate.*
