# For your information first (⛔ not a question)

In August you ruled that the details members add to the directory (a school, a designation) are **for display only** — they may ⛔ not
decide anything on their own — and that a school can ⛔ never be a unit of staff authority. In October you ruled that the people asked
about a death are the member's **workplace** colleagues. We read October's ruling as your own decision to use the workplace for exactly
that, and nothing else: the workplace is recorded as part of a member's **posting** (like the district), it is ⛔ never shown in the
directory or on any public page, it is ⛔ never a unit of staff authority, and ⛔ no Pariwar Admin can set or create a member's workplace —
an admin may only **suggest** one, which the member must confirm. ⭐ If you meant the August rulings to need a separate step first, please
say so.

# SC1 — the date of death is ⛔ not known when the five are chosen

**In one sentence:** because we know only the day a claim is filed — ⛔ not the day the member died — may we count the 90-day transfer
rule back from the filing day, and accept that a family holding the member's phone could change the member's workplace or the people
they named before filing, with the limits below?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** both points narrow your CF4 A ruling at an edge.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ When the five are chosen, the only date of the death the system has is the **filing date**. The certificate's date is checked by a
District Admin later, and the app does ⛔ not read the date off the certificate (⛔ no reading service is connected yet). Many families
file from the member's own phone.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Count from the filing day, with two limits** (our suggestion): colleagues recorded at the school for 90 days or more are asked first, and staff are told when the member's workplace or the people they named changed in the 90 days before the claim. | The five are chosen at once, as today. | A family that files late can miss the previous school's colleagues; a family that plans ahead can still change who is asked — staff see the warning, and the ground inspection always runs. |
| **B** | **Ignore every change to the member's own workplace or named people made in the 90 days before filing.** | Changes made shortly before a claim never count. | A member who dies days after a real transfer loses the new school's colleagues; a member who joined less than 90 days before has ⛔ nobody asked. |
| **C** | **Choose the five only after a District Admin has accepted the certificate's date.** | The rule runs on the real date; ⛔ no change after the death counts. | The neighbours are asked days later, and every claim must start its checks without them — a change to how all claims begin. |

**Our reading:** A — **because** it keeps the neighbours' check early and complete for genuine transfers, and the two limits make a
family's change visible to the staff who decide; the ground inspection is required for every claim in any case.

# In plain English

When someone dies, we ask a few members who worked with them whether it is true. You ruled that these are the member's own colleagues,
and the previous school's colleagues too if the member moved less than 90 days before dying. The difficulty is that when we pick them,
we do ⛔ not yet know the day of death — only the day the claim was filed — and the family may be using the member's own phone. We suggest
counting the 90 days from the filing day, asking the long-standing colleagues first, and telling staff whenever the member's workplace or
named people changed shortly before the claim. A careful family could still influence the choice, but staff would see it, and every claim
still has its ground inspection. We also suggest one small wording change so that office colleagues have an answer that fits them.
