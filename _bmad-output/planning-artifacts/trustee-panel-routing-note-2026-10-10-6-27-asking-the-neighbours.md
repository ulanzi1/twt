# Question 1 — may the Trust text the five neighbours, naming the member?

**In one sentence:** To ask the five nearest members about a death, may the Trust send each of them a text message that names the
member and says the Trust has been told they have died, asking them to answer in the app or by calling the helpline?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it tells five people — before anything is checked — that a named member is reported dead,
and it texts members, which the approved plan does ⛔ not do (members get app notices and WhatsApp, ⛔ never ordinary texts). Whom
the Trust writes to, what it says about a person, and by what means, are yours.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **The app cannot alert anyone today.** It never registers a phone for notifications, and members have ⛔ no inbox. So without a
text, the five learn they were asked only if they happen to open the app and see the request — in practice, almost never. That is
what happens today: since the check with neighbours was built, **every claim has ended its 72 hours with zero answers**.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A text naming the member.** *"TWT: We have been told that [member] has died. If you knew them, please answer two short questions: [link to the app] — or call the helpline [number]."* ⭐ **Plus one WhatsApp reminder after two days** (48 hours), with the same words, to those of the five who have ⛔ not answered **and** have chosen WhatsApp messages from the Trust. | The five learn of the reported death the day the claim is checked, and can answer in a minute — the link opens the questions. Those who missed the text get one reminder, in time for their answer to count in the 72 hours. | Five people hear of a death **before it is checked** — if the claim is false, five members were told a living member died. A cost per text and a new DLT template — ⚠ in Hindi, a name, a link and a number do ⛔ not fit in one SMS, so each text is likely sent as about three parts and costs about three times a short one (five texts per claim). A text to members, which the plan did ⛔ not have. Counsel must confirm the legal basis before go-live (as for your FQ7 text). The reminder: a second message about the same death, sent only to members who agreed to WhatsApp from the Trust; Meta must approve its wording, and may refuse. |
| **B** | **A text that does ⛔ not name anyone.** *"TWT: There is a question for you about a member of your area: [link to the app] — or call the helpline [number]."* | The name is seen only inside the app, after logging in. | Many people ignore a vague text; fewer answers. The same cost per text, DLT template and counsel check as A. The name is still shown to the five — only later. |
| **C** | **⛔ No text — the helpline calls the five.** | A person from the Trust phones each neighbour and records the answer. | Up to five calls per claim, by staff; slower; the call says the same as A's text, so the disclosure is the same. ⛔ No new message type. |
| **D** | **⛔ No text — the request waits in the app.** | Nothing changes from today for most claims. | Almost ⛔ no answers, as today. FQ8 E (*"the five neighbours are also asked"*) and FQ10's warnings would exist on paper only. |

**Our reading: A, with the reminder** — **because** the Panel has already decided the five are asked (FQ8 E, FQ10 A), and asking them means telling
them who; A is the only option that actually reaches them without staff calls. ⚠ It is a widening: members would get an ordinary
text for the first time, and five people hear of a death before it is checked. ⭐ What A's text **leaves out**: ⛔ no mention of a
claim, money, the family, the nominee or who reported it; ⛔ no deadline. ⭐ If you want the text but ⛔ not the reminder, answer
*"A without the reminder"*.

# Question 2 — a neighbour's answer that comes after the District Admin has approved

**In one sentence:** If a neighbour answers *"they have not died"* (or gives a date of death more than a day from the certificate's)
**after** the District Admin has already approved the claim, must the final approval wait for the District Admin's reason and note —
as it does for every other warning that comes late?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** your one rule (`-277` Q3 B) says yes for **any** late warning. Your FQ10 A says the
neighbours *"can ⛔ never block a claim on their own"*. A late neighbour's answer is the one case where both apply, and the
Panel was told late warnings *"should be rare"* (a certificate re-checked after approval) — neighbours' answers were ⛔ not in
front of you then.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **A text reaches real people, who answer when they see it — days later is normal.** The District Admin can approve before
the 72 hours are over (once the inspection is complete), so a late answer is ⛔ not rare: it is expected.
⭐ And **the wait is never a refusal, and the neighbour cannot hold it**: the District Admin's chosen reason and note release it at
once, as for every warning.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **One rule for every warning.** The answer is kept and shown; the final approval waits until the District Admin records a reason and a note for it. | A family whose claim a neighbour disputes after approval waits until the District Admin looks at that answer — usually a day or two. | One person's late answer can hold a family's payment until the District Admin acts. ⚠ Read strictly, that is a neighbour holding a claim *"on their own"* — we read FQ10 A as *"a neighbour's answer can ⛔ never refuse a claim or stand in for the inspection"*, and the District Admin's reason releases it. |
| **B** | **Shown, but ⛔ nothing waits.** The answer is kept and shown; each later approver (State Trustee, R9 panel) must give a reason and a note for it, but the claim does ⛔ not wait for the District Admin. | The family's claim never waits on a neighbour. | ⚠ A second rule for one kind of warning. The District Admin — the person who knows the area and can phone the neighbour — may ⛔ never see an answer that came after their approval. |
| **C** | **Answers are taken only until the District Admin decides.** After that, the app and the helpline say *"thank you — this is no longer needed"*. | Nothing a neighbour says after approval reaches anyone. | ⚠ Narrows FQ10 A's *"is shown to the District Admin as a warning"*: a true *"they have not died"* that arrives an hour after approval is thrown away. |

**Our reading: A** — **because** FQ12 (`-264`) asked for one rule for every warning, naming *"a neighbour saying the member has not
died"* among them; the wait is released by the District Admin's own reason and note, so a neighbour can ⛔ never refuse a claim or keep
it waiting once the District Admin has looked. ⭐ Under every option, ⛔ no answer is taken once the final approval has been given
(after that, nothing would read it).

# In plain English

Story 6.6 picks the five members nearest to a member who has died and writes them down — but it never asks them anything, so ⛔ no
one has ever answered. To make the check with neighbours real, the Trust has to reach those five. The app cannot notify them, so the
only practical way is a text. A text has to say who it is about, so it tells five people that the Trust has been told a member has
died — before anyone has checked. We suggest it does, and says nothing else: ⛔ no claim, ⛔ no money, ⛔ no family names, ⛔ no deadline.
Those who have not answered after two days, and who chose WhatsApp messages from the Trust, get one WhatsApp reminder with the
same words.

Once the five can answer, some will answer after the District Admin has approved — that is normal for texts. Your one rule says any
warning that comes late makes the final approval wait for the District Admin to look at it and write why they still approve. We
suggest that applies here too: the District Admin's note releases the claim at once, so a neighbour can ⛔ never refuse a claim or hold
it once the District Admin has looked.
