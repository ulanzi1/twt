# Confirm 1 — the true nominee files in the app only if she has the member's account

**In one sentence:** After a refusal on suspicion, the filing code now goes to the true nominee's phone (your FQ6) — but the code is
**requested from the member's own account in the app**, so she can file in the app only if she can open that account; otherwise she files
through the helpline, as before. Do you accept that?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** you ruled FQ6 to close the gap *"Sunita cannot file her new claim in the app"*. It closes the
**other** half of that gap (Vikas can ⛔ no longer file in the app) but only partly closes this half. You should know that before it goes live.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **The app has one way to start a claim: from inside the member's own account.** That was true before FQ6 and FQ6 did ⛔ not change it —
it changed only **where the code is sent**. In your own scenario Vikas holds Ramesh's phone, so Sunita usually cannot open Ramesh's account
and files through the helpline. And if Vikas opens the app and asks for the code, it goes to Sunita (good — he cannot file) — but his
screen shows **the last four digits of Sunita's number**, as the app shows every time a code is sent.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Accept it as built.** The code goes to the nominee in place at the death; she files in the app if she can open the member's account, otherwise through the helpline. | Vikas can ⛔ no longer file in the app. Sunita files in the app only with access to Ramesh's account — usually through the helpline. Whoever holds Ramesh's phone sees four digits of her number when asking for the code. | Sunita still has to know to call the helpline — but your FQ7 text tells her exactly that (*"Please call the helpline"*). Four digits of her number are shown to the person who made the change (⚠ inference: a relative who may well know it already). |
| **B** | **Build a second way to start a claim**, from the true nominee's own phone, without the member's account. | Sunita could file in the app herself. | A new way into the claims system for someone who is ⛔ not the account holder — a new identity check to design, the very kind of path the false-nominee fraud abuses. A new story, and a new question for you about who may start a claim. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** the helpline already works for her, your FQ7 text sends her there, and B opens a new door at the exact
spot the fraud you ruled on came through. ⛔ No 6.24b work waits on this.

# Confirm 2 — the cases where a text you ratified is not sent

**In one sentence:** In two kinds of case the system does ⛔ not send a text you ruled must be sent: **(a)** the refused person is ⛔ not
sent the text *"The claim for [member] could not go ahead. It can be appealed until [date]. …"* — ⛔ any of it — once they have already
appealed; and **(b)** the true nominee is ⛔ not texted *"Your claim
for [member] has been closed"* about a closed claim that was recorded against a nominee entry saved after the death — which most often
means the claim was filed by the person who made the change, but in two cases can be her own claim. Do you confirm both?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** both narrow **when** a ratified text goes. That is a change to what the Trust tells a person,
so only you can accept it.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **(a): the text would be false.** A person who has already appealed would be told they *can* appeal until a date.
⭐ **(b): the system can tell which nominee entry a claim was filed under, ⛔ not who filed it.** A claim filed under an entry saved
after the death is most often the changer's claim — telling Sunita *"Your claim … has been closed"* about Vikas's claim would
confuse her about a claim she never made. But two kinds of claim that really are hers are also filed under such an entry:
- **her own entry saved again after the death** — her new number was entered, **or** the change that added the other person kept her
  on the list: every save of the nominee list re-saves every entry on it, hers included; and
- **a claim recorded while only the newest entries could be chosen** — always in the app, and through the helpline before the District
  Admin's finding; if she picks her own place on the list and that entry was saved after the death, her claim is recorded against it.

Each skipped closure text (b) raises an **alarm to staff** (claim ids only), so ⛔ none is lost silently — staff can call her. (a) raises
⛔ no alarm: the person has already acted on the refusal, so there is nothing for staff to do.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Confirm both, as built.** (a) ⛔ no appeal-date text once an appeal is filed; (b) ⛔ no closure text about a claim filed under an entry saved after the death; each (b) skip alarms staff. | A person who appealed gets ⛔ no false text. Sunita is ⛔ not told about a claim Vikas filed **as the nominee**. In the two own-claim cases above, she is ⛔ not texted about her own closed claim; staff are alerted. | In the own-claim cases she learns of the closure only if staff act on the alarm, or when she calls — and nothing assigns the alarm to anyone yet. ⚠ A does ⛔ not remove B's cost entirely: she IS still texted about a claim Vikas filed as a family member who is ⛔ not a nominee, and the first nominee is texted about a claim the second nominee filed (both recorded; rarer). |
| **B** | **Send in every case** — (a) even after an appeal is filed; (b) for every closed claim. | Everyone who could be texted is texted. | (a) tells an appellant something false. (b) tells Sunita her claim was closed when it was Vikas's — in the likeliest case, a confusing and possibly alarming text about a claim she never made. |
| **C** | **Confirm (a); for (b), send in her two own-claim cases** by checking whether the name on the skipped claim's entry matches hers. | Sunita is texted about her own claim in those two cases too. | New work: a name match on sensitive data, which can be wrong both ways — a misspelling skips her, a shared family name texts her about someone else's claim. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** (a)'s text would be untrue, (b)'s main case is a text about the wrong person's claim, the two own-claim
cases are alarmed rather than silent, and C replaces those misses with a name check that can fail in either direction. ⚠ How often the
own-claim cases happen is unknown (below) — if you expect them to be common, C or B deserves more weight.

# In plain English

**First:** you ruled that after a refusal on suspicion, the code that lets a family file a claim in the app goes to the true nominee, ⛔ not
to the person who changed the nominee. We built that. But the app only lets a claim be started from inside the member's own account — so
the true nominee can file in the app only if she can open that account. In the case you were shown, the person who made the change holds
the member's phone, so she would usually file through the helpline — and the text you ruled tells her to call the helpline. And when
anyone asks for the code from that account, the screen shows the last four digits of the phone it went to. We suggest accepting this:
building a separate way in for her would open a new door exactly where this fraud came in.

**Second:** in some cases we do ⛔ not send a text you ruled. We do ⛔ not tell someone "you can appeal until this date" after they have
already appealed — it would be untrue. And we do ⛔ not tell the true nominee "your claim has been closed" about a claim filed under the
nominee entry saved after the death — because most often that is the other person's claim, ⛔ not hers. In two kinds of case it is
actually hers, and we do ⛔ not know how often; then staff get an alarm instead, so they can call her. We suggest confirming both. If you would rather the texts go in those
cases, tell us, and we will change it before the texts go live.
