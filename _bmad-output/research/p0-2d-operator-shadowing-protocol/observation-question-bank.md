# Observation Question Bank — P0-2d Operator Shadowing

> **Note-prompt cue list for the RESEARCHER, NOT a script presented to operator or caller.** Per ethics-protocol §3.6 + README §4 invariant.
>
> Each dimension carries ≥5 observation prompts (observer-side note-prompts, NOT operator-prompts). The researcher uses these prompts as **observation-orientation cues** — what to attend to during a call, what to note in per-shift note.
>
> **Anti-leading carve-out per ethics-protocol §3.6:**
> - §3 + §7 prompts MUST NOT solicit caller-personal-content
> - §5 + §6 prompts MUST NOT prescribe or correct operator workflow

## §1 — Call category distribution (AC dimension 1)

**What to observe per call:**
- What category of call is this? (claim-related / contribution-related / KYC / technical / complaint / other)
- What sub-category emerges within the broad category? (e.g., claim-related → claim-status-inquiry / claim-document-submission / claim-decision-appeal)
- Does the call category match the host-helpline's existing routing-policy categories OR diverge?
- If divergent, what does the operator do? (best-fit-routing / supervisor-escalation / improvised-routing)

**Per-shift aggregate (NOT a per-call note):** at shift-end, tally total calls per category across all calls with `caller-consent-given`. This is a shift-end summary activity — do not attempt to maintain a running count mid-call.

**5 observation prompts:**

1. **Category identification — at call-open:** what is the operator's first signal of the category? (caller's stated reason / inferred from caller's words / inferred from caller demographic context)
2. **Sub-category emergence — during call:** does the call resolve to a more specific sub-category as it unfolds? what triggers the sub-category determination?
3. **Cross-category overlap:** does the call touch multiple categories? if so, how does the operator prioritize?
4. **Outlier categories:** does the call fall outside the host-helpline's existing categories? what does the operator do?
5. **Category-distribution skew:** at end-of-shift, what is the rough distribution across categories? is one category dominant (>50%)? are some categories absent?

## §2 — Average handling time (AC dimension 2)

**What to observe per call:**
- Total call duration (operator's clock or researcher's clock)
- Within the call, time spent on: member-lookup / category-selection / body-capture / escalation-handover / closing
- Time-to-first-substantive-response (how long after call-open does the operator give actionable information?)
- Operator's apparent time budget per call
- Hold time / transfer time / wait time

**5 observation prompts:**

1. **Total duration capture:** record call start + end timestamps in per-shift note
2. **Time-to-first-substantive-response:** how long after call-open does the operator give the caller actionable information (a category determination, a lookup result, an escalation acknowledgment)?
3. **Member-lookup time:** how long does the operator spend on member-lookup-with-disambiguation? does the operator use multiple criteria (name + mobile + Aadhaar-masked + Pariwar-ID)?
4. **Body-capture time:** how long does the operator spend capturing the call body (the substantive issue)?
5. **Closing time:** how long does closing take? does the operator follow a standard closing or improvise?

## §3 — Common caller pain points (AC dimension 3)

**What to observe per call (CRITICAL: NO caller-personal-content — categorical observations only):**
- What category of pain point is the caller experiencing? (lookup-failure / status-confusion / payment-failure / document-confusion / escalation-frustration / other)
- How does the operator respond to the pain point?
- Does the pain point pattern repeat across calls in the shift?
- Per-shift tally: most common pain-point categories

**5 observation prompts:**

1. **Pain-point category:** what broad category of pain point is surfacing? (CRITICAL: category-level only — do NOT capture specific case details)
2. **Pain-point cadence:** how does the operator pace the response? does the operator slow down, repeat, restate?
3. **Pain-point escalation trigger:** does the pain point escalate to supervisor-handover, or does the operator close in-shift?
4. **Pain-point recurrence:** is the same pain-point category recurring across calls in the shift?
5. **Pain-point resolution clarity:** at call-end, is the pain point apparently resolved OR pending OR escalated?

**Anti-leading carve-out:** prompts MUST NOT solicit specific caller story, specific case details, specific names, specific identifiers. Categorical observation only.

**Synthesis routing note:** §3 observations feed synthesis §3.3 (common caller pain points) as narrative only. No worksheet verdict rows are associated with this dimension — the operator-workflow-call-pattern observation worksheet has no §3 pain-point section.

## §4 — Escalation patterns (AC dimension 4)

**What to observe per call:**
- Does this call escalate to supervisor-handover OR remain in-operator-scope?
- If escalation: what triggers it? (caller request / operator-recognized non-standard scenario / policy boundary / technical limitation)
- What handover protocol does the operator follow? (warm transfer / cold transfer / case-held-with-intake-pending / case-routed-to-supervisor-queue)
- How long does the escalation handover take?
- What happens to the caller during handover? (held / called back / dropped)

**5 observation prompts:**

1. **Escalation trigger identification:** what specifically triggered the escalation in this call?
2. **Handover protocol:** what protocol does the operator use? warm transfer (introduces supervisor before transferring) vs cold transfer vs case-held vs queue-routing?
3. **Caller-experience during handover:** is the caller held on line, called back, or dropped?
4. **Operator-attribution at handover:** does the operator's attribution (Operator-1 name) persist after handover OR is it replaced by supervisor attribution?
5. **Escalation-distribution at shift-end:** what % of calls escalated? what % remained in-operator-scope?

## §5 — Operator-wishlist: what the operator wishes their tooling did (AC dimension 5)

**What to observe per call + between calls:**
- Does the operator voluntarily mention a tooling-wish during a call OR between calls?
- What category of wish is it? (member-lookup-improvement / category-selection-improvement / read-back-card-improvement / escalation-handover-improvement / SLA-tracking-improvement / other)
- Is the wish concrete (specific tooling change) OR aspirational (general improvement)?

**Anti-leading carve-out:** prompts MUST NOT prescribe or correct operator workflow. Researcher does NOT suggest workflow improvements. Researcher does NOT solicit tooling wishes — only notes what the operator voluntarily mentions.

**5 observation prompts:**

1. **Voluntary tooling-wish mention:** does the operator mention a tooling-wish unprompted?
2. **Wish category:** what category does the wish fall into? (capture for later cross-shift pattern analysis)
3. **Wish-concreteness:** is the wish concrete or aspirational?
4. **Wish-context:** what triggered the wish-mention? (a specific call frustration / a between-call reflection / a general observation)
5. **Wish-recurrence:** does the same wish-category recur across shifts?

## §6 — Operator-improvisation: where the operator improvises around process gaps (AC dimension 6)

**What to observe per call:**
- Does the operator improvise around a process gap during this call?
- What category of improvisation? (workflow-step-skip / workflow-step-reorder / informal-routing / informal-escalation-shortcut / informal-data-capture-format / informal-closing-pattern / other)
- Is the improvisation a one-time response to this caller OR a routine pattern?
- Does the operator narrate the improvisation (between calls) OR is it silent?

**Anti-leading carve-out:** prompts MUST NOT prescribe or correct operator workflow. Researcher does NOT suggest workflow improvements. Researcher does NOT critique the improvisation.

**5 observation prompts:**

1. **Improvisation identification:** what specifically did the operator do that diverges from the apparent standard workflow?
2. **Improvisation category:** what category of improvisation? (capture for cross-shift pattern analysis)
3. **Improvisation triggers:** what triggered the improvisation? (caller's specific need / tool limitation / time pressure / supervisor unavailability)
4. **Improvisation reproducibility:** is the improvisation a routine pattern the operator uses repeatedly, OR a one-time response?
5. **Improvisation outcome:** did the improvisation resolve the issue OR lead to escalation?

## §7 — Cross-cutting operator-caller register grammar (7th dimension — implicit per AC)

**What to observe per call:**
- Call-opening salutation: what Hindi register does the operator use? (formal / informal / Hindi-Bhojpuri blend / English-Hindi blend)
- Closing patterns: how does the operator close the call?
- Pacing under emotional caller load: how does the operator pace speech when caller is in distress?
- Pattern 4 operator-facing precise-technical-wording (UX-DR55 carve-out) validation: does the operator use precise-technical wording (e.g., "claim ID", "reconciliation status") OR informal equivalents?
- Operator's apparent comfort with Hindi register switching

**5 observation prompts:**

1. **Call-opening register:** what salutation register does the operator use? does it shift based on caller context?
2. **Closing patterns:** does the operator have a standard closing? does it shift based on call outcome?
3. **Emotional-load pacing:** when caller is in distress, does the operator slow down, repeat, or use special register?
4. **UX-DR55 operator-facing precise-technical-register validation:** does the operator use TWT-internal-technical terms OR informal equivalents? does the precise-technical register serve operator workflow OR impose jargon overhead?
5. **Hindi register switching:** does the operator shift register (formal/informal/dialect) based on caller context? smoothly OR awkwardly?

**Anti-leading carve-out:** prompts MUST NOT solicit caller-personal-content. Register observation is about the operator's language patterns, NOT the caller's words.

**Synthesis routing note:** Only §7 prompt 4 (UX-DR55 operator-facing precise-technical-register validation) has worksheet infrastructure in operator-workflow-call-pattern observation worksheet §10. Prompts 1–3 and 5 (salutation register, closing patterns, emotional-load pacing, Hindi register code-switching) feed synthesis §9 (register grammar findings) as narrative only — no worksheet verdict rows.

## §8 — Closing observation prompts (per-call)

These are observer-side note-prompts for the researcher's per-shift note structure at end-of-call:

1. **Call duration captured?** (start + end timestamps)
2. **Call category captured?** (primary + sub-category)
3. **Operator workflow-steps captured?** (per-call observation row)
4. **Escalation noted?** (yes/no + target if applicable)
5. **Operator-improvisation noted?** (yes/no + category if applicable)
6. **Operator-wishlist mention noted?** (yes/no + category if applicable)
7. **Register-grammar observation noted?** (any notable register pattern)
8. **Per-call divergence flag?** (does this call's observation contradict an `assumption-inventory.md` row?)
9. **Caller-consent status?** (`caller-consent-given` / `caller-consent-declined` / `caller-consent-no-response` / `caller-consent-revoked-mid-call` / `caller-ended-call-abruptly` / `emotional-overload-observer-discretion-end`)
10. **Pseudonymization check?** (no operator real identity + no caller identity + no caller-personal-content) (per-call: awareness check only — ensure no caller identity or caller-personal-content was captured; formal batch verification: post-shift within 24h per shadowing-protocol §7)
