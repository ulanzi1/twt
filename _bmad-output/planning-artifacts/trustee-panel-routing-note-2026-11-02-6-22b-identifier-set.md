# The question

**In one sentence:** on 21 September you ruled that the Trust keeps a register of people found to have faked a death claim, barred for
life by their PAN or an Aadhaar-based number collected on the claim form — and five things that ruling does not say must be decided
before the form asks anyone for a number.

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** each answer decides whether a family can claim, how long it waits, what the Trust keeps about
it, or who can be barred for life. Building any of them our own way would decide it for you.

## The one fact that decides most of it

**Not everyone has a PAN, and a PAN check does not always recognise the right person.** By count, roughly three in four adults hold a
PAN, and poorer families are likely to hold one less often. Commercial PAN checkers say names on PAN records often do not match how
people write them. So a register keyed on PAN either turns some genuine families away or lets some people through without a check.
Q1 and Q2 decide which; Q3 decides how long we keep what they gave us.

---

# Q1 — A family member who has no PAN: may they still file, and be paid?

You ruled that PAN and Aadhaar are collected on the claim form, and that only an exact number may bar someone. Neither ruling says what
happens when the person filing, or the person to be paid, has no PAN.

**The one fact:** by count, roughly three in four adults hold a PAN — an upper estimate; there is no figure for poor or rural families,
who are likely below it. So some genuine families filing a claim will have none.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **PAN required** to file and to be paid. | A person with no PAN cannot file, or cannot be paid. | A genuine family with no PAN cannot claim at all. |
| **B** | **PAN asked; with none, a signed declaration**, and the claim goes to the person who checks name matches (our suggestion). | A family with no PAN signs a statement that they have none, and their claim is checked by a person. | Someone who wants to avoid the register can simply say "no PAN"; the register catches only those who give one. |
| **C** | **PAN optional**, no declaration. | As B, without the signed statement. | As B, with less on record. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** a mutual-aid trust that turns away the families least likely to hold a PAN fails the people it
exists for. The question written for counsel asks whether a PAN may be required at all and whether a declaration is acceptable; if counsel narrows what is
lawful, this comes back to you.

# Q2 — When a PAN cannot be confirmed — it does not match the name, or the checking service is down — does the claim wait?

Our own record of your ruling noted it already: waiting stops a grieving family; not waiting admits a number nobody checked.

**The one fact:** commercial PAN checkers report that names on PAN records often differ from how people write them — initials, order,
spelling. One vendor claims up to 18% of checks are rejected on that alone (a vendor's claim, not a measured rate). So some "did not
match" results will be wrong about a genuine family.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The claim waits** until the PAN matches. | A family waits on a vendor, or on a spelling. | A genuine family can be held up by a name written differently. |
| **B** | **The claim goes on with a warning** the approver must answer with a reason — the same warning you chose for other doubts on 28 September (our suggestion). | The claim is not held; the approver sees the warning and must say why they approve. | A claim with an unconfirmed PAN can be paid. |
| **C** | **The claim goes on silently.** | Nothing changes for the family. | The check protects nothing. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** it keeps the family moving and still puts the doubt in front of a person who must answer it.

---

# Q3 — When does a claim "close with no finding of fraud", so the family's identity details are deleted?

You ruled that identity details are deleted when a claim closes with no finding of fraud. Our own reading, written down when you ruled
and never ratified, was *"after the appeal path is exhausted or its window lapses"*.

**The one fact:** a claim refused for the first time, and never appealed, can be appealed at **any** time — it never becomes final. Only
three refusals ever become final: one after an appeal, one closed because the family never answered, and a post-death refusal after
your 90 days. So under our reading the commonest refused claim's details would never be deleted. And a **paid** claim may need the
payee's details kept by other law (the question written for counsel asks).

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Delete only when a claim is closed** — never for a refused or a paid claim. | A refused family's and a paid person's PAN are kept for ever. | The opposite of "only the guilty are kept". |
| **B** | **Give every refusal an appeal window** (90 days, as you set for post-death refusals) and delete when it lapses; delete a paid claim's details after any period counsel sets. | A refused family must appeal within 90 days. | A family loses the right to appeal late, which today they have. |
| **C** | **Delete a closed claim's details at once and a paid claim's after counsel's period; for a refused claim, delete after a fixed period counsel sets, without closing the appeal** (our suggestion). | A refused family can still appeal later. | A family who appeals late and wins is asked for their documents again. |

**Our reading:** **C** — **because** it deletes without taking away an appeal you have not limited.

**Also asked:** when an investigation finds a member **innocent**, the staff's written notes about it — do they fall in this deletion?

# Q4 — When the member really died but the person who filed lied: who raises it, and can that person be found guilty?

Two kinds of fraud happen after a real death: the nominations are changed after the member died, or a death certificate is forged. You
ruled that a post-death nomination change is refused at once, and that a lifetime bar follows **only** after the same investigation
required for a faked death.

**The one fact:** the chain you ruled starts with *"operator flags"*, but in these cases it is the **District Admin** who sees the fraud —
in the nomination history or the certificate check. And the only findings you ever ruled are about the **member** — "innocent" or
"guilty" — which here would brand a dead member. The note that recorded your 21 September ruling summarises the chain as *"helpline
operator or District Admin flags"*; that line was the note author's summary — neither the option you chose nor the decision says so.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The helpline operator raises it, on the District Admin's word** — your words kept. | — | A hand-off that adds nothing: the District Admin has to tell the operator what the District Admin found. |
| **B** | **The helpline operator or the District Admin raises it; a different person finds** (our suggestion). | The person who saw the fraud can raise it; someone else decides. | It widens your ruled chain — the summary line is not a ruling, so this is new. |
| **C** | **Only the District Admin raises it, and a person one level up finds.** | — | A new chain of people to set up. |

And the finding: *"the person who filed took part / did not take part"* — is that a separate investigation of the person who filed,
**not** a third outcome added to your *"innocent / guilty"* finding about the member?

**Our reading:** **B, and yes** — **because** the person who sees the fraud should be able to raise it, someone else should decide it,
and a finding about the person who filed must never be recorded against the member who died.

---

# Q5 — If counsel confirms the Trust cannot lawfully hold an Aadhaar-based number, is Aadhaar dropped from the claim form?

You ruled that *"PAN and Aadhaar of nominee and claimant are to be collected on the filing form"*. Dropping Aadhaar narrows that ruling,
so it is yours.

**The one fact:** our identity provider gives us only the last four digits of an Aadhaar number, and the Aadhaar authority issues its
token only to licensed authentication agencies — our understanding, which the question written for counsel asks it to confirm. Without a token, an Aadhaar
number can be used to recognise someone only by keeping the number itself, which the Aadhaar authority's rules tightly limit.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Drop Aadhaar; PAN is the only exact number** (our suggestion — the record we wrote of your 21 September ruling already noted it: *"If no UID token is available, PAN is the only exact identifier"*). | No one is asked for an Aadhaar number. | A person with no PAN is caught only by the person checking name matches. |
| **B** | **Collect the last four digits only**, as a supporting detail, never to bar anyone. | Families give four digits they did not have to give before. | More kept about people who are not members, for a weak signal. |
| **C** | **Keep your words and wait for a lawful route.** | — | The register cannot be built until one exists. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** a number the Trust may not lawfully hold protects no one, and asking for it costs families trust.
⛔ This question does not need answering before counsel replies: your answer can be conditional on counsel confirming there is no route.

# In plain English

You decided that people who fake a death claim should be barred for life, and that the Trust would recognise them by an identity
number collected when they file. Before we ask grieving families for that number, five things need your decision. A family with no PAN
should still be able to claim, after signing that they have none (Q1). A number that does not check out should raise a warning, not
stop the claim (Q2). Families' details should be deleted after a set time, without taking away their right to appeal (Q3). The District
Admin who spots a fraud after a real death should be able to raise it, with someone else deciding, and that finding should be about the
person who filed, never the member who died (Q4). And if the lawyers confirm the Trust cannot lawfully hold an Aadhaar-based number,
Aadhaar should be dropped (Q5). Nothing is built until counsel has answered too.

## E3 — What the code actually does (traced at `b7b43c47`, Story 6.22b G2–G13)

- **No PAN, father's name or full Aadhaar exists** in any schema or contract. `pan` appears only as a FORBIDDEN custom-field key.
- **DigiLocker** returns the member's Aadhaar masked to the last four digits at the provider boundary (`mapEaadhaarToKycProfile` →
  `maskAadhaar`); only `aadhaarMaskedId` (last-4) is stored. No token or reference key survives.
- **Intake carries no claimant identity**; the claimant's name / mobile / address are written AFTER the claim is created
  (`writeClaimContact` → `claim_contacts`). The filer is trusted by possession of the nominee's phone (handover OTP), ⛔ by identity.
- **A refusal becomes final in exactly three cases** (`assertAppealInitiable`): an earlier appeal journey
  (`AppealAlreadyExhaustedError`), a no-response closure (`AppealClosedNoResponseError`), a post-death refusal older than 90 days
  (`AppealTimeLimitPassedError`). Every other first refusal has *"NO elapsed-time gate"*.
- **The 6.22 investigation** records only `flagged | found_innocent | found_guilty` against the MEMBER reported dead; a guilty finding
  freezes that member's account. Used on a real death it would brand the dead member ⇒ Q4's "a separate investigation".
- **Nothing deletes or scrubs anything when a claim closes**; no retention job exists.
- **A death certificate's record** has no guard against deletion today (only the absence of a delete grant); Story 6.22b adds one.
