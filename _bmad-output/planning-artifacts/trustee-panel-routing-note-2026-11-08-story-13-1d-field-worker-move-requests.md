# Question 1 — four permissions: do you confirm them?

**In one sentence:** do you confirm four new permissions — **ask** another District Admin to take on or release a worker, **answer** such a
request, **settle** a request that has gone to the Pariwar Admin, and **read** the requests — held as below?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** your earlier decision says permissions for field workers are confirmed by you once the feature
that uses them is built. This is that feature.

## The one fact that decides it

**You have already said who does each of these; the permissions turn it into switches.** You ruled that the District Admin of the district a
worker is leaving or joining asks, the other answers, and the Pariwar Admin settles what they cannot. Every act is written to the request's
history with the officer and the permission that allowed it. Moving a worker directly stays the Pariwar Admin's alone, as you confirmed on
2026-11-07.

## What you are choosing between (Question 1)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Confirm all four, as built.** Ask: a District Admin of either end. Answer: a District Admin of the other end, never the one who asked. Settle: the Pariwar Admin. Read: the District Admins of either end, and the Pariwar Admin. *Our suggestion.* | Two District Admins settle a move between themselves; the Pariwar Admin sees only what they cannot settle. | A District Admin who holds both districts cannot both ask and answer — the request waits for another officer or goes to the Pariwar Admin. |
| **B** | **As A, and the Pariwar Admin may also ask and answer.** | The Pariwar Admin can step into any request. | More work lands on the Pariwar Admin, against BigDev's wish not to load them (2026-11-03); and the Pariwar Admin can already move a worker directly, so asking adds little. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it is your ruling turned into permissions, and the Pariwar Admin keeps the direct move for anything the
District Admins cannot settle.

# Question 2 — the officer at the other end: do they see the worker's name?

**In one sentence:** when a District Admin is asked to take on — or release — a worker, does the system show that officer the worker's
**name**?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it is what the Trust discloses about a person, and to whom. Today a District Admin sees only the
workers in their own district; the officer asked to take a worker on is, by definition, not yet that worker's officer.

## The one fact that decides it

**Your ruling asks the officer to agree to take a person on; they cannot agree to someone they cannot identify.** Without a name, the two
officers would have to identify the worker to each other by phone, outside any record.

## What you are choosing between (Question 2)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The name only — not the phone number — and every time a list of requests is shown, the system records which officer saw which names.** *Our suggestion.* | The worker's name is seen by the District Admins of both districts on the request. | The name stays on the request after it is answered — including for an officer who refused — for as long as the request is listed. |
| **B** | **The name and the phone number.** | The officer asked can call the worker before deciding. | A worker's number reaches an officer who may refuse them and never manage them; the asking officer can pass the number on if a call is needed. |
| **C** | **Neither.** The request shows only the two districts; the officers identify the worker between themselves. | Nothing new is shown. | The officer agrees to a person the system has not named; a mistake (the wrong worker) is found only after the move. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** the name is what the decision needs, the phone number is not, and every look is recorded.

# Question 3 — who tells the worker the answer?

**In one sentence:** when a request is accepted or refused, should the system tell the worker, or does the officer who asked tell them?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it is who is told of a decision about a person. Your ruling named the District Admins as the
people told; it did not mention the worker.

## The one fact that decides it

**The system has no way to reach a field worker today, and it cannot see that the worker asked.** Field workers have no app login yet; a
text message needs a wording approved by the telecom regulator first (the code text is still waiting for that approval); and you accepted,
as a cost of your ruling, that "at the worker's request" is the officer's word.

## What you are choosing between (Question 3)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The officer who asked tells the worker.** The system tells that officer the answer and reminds them: "Tell the worker." *Our suggestion.* | The worker hears from the officer who took their request. | A worker may not hear if the officer forgets; the system cannot check that they were told. |
| **B** | **The system sends the worker a text on every answer.** | The worker hears even if the officer forgets. | A new text wording to approve before it can be sent; the text would carry a refusal to a worker who may not know who refused or why. |
| **C** | **The worker sees it in their own app, when it exists** — and the officer tells them until then. | As A now; later, the worker can look. | Nothing for the worker until the app is built; then a second decision about what the worker may see (the reason for a refusal, the officer's name). |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** the worker asked an officer, so that officer is the natural one to answer them, and nothing else can
reach the worker today. C can follow when the worker's app is built.

# In plain English

We are building the way two District Admins move a field worker between them: one asks, the other accepts or refuses with a reason, and a
request nobody answers for a week goes to the Pariwar Admin. Your ruling already says all of that. Three things are left for you. First,
please confirm the four permissions that put your ruling into the system — ask, answer, settle and read — held as you described the roles.
Second, the officer asked to take a worker on does not manage that worker yet, so today they could not see the worker's name: we suggest
showing the name (never the phone number) and recording every time it is seen, because nobody can agree to take on someone they cannot
identify. Third, the system has no way to reach a field worker today, so we suggest the officer who took the worker's request tells them the
answer, and the system reminds that officer to. We suggest A for all three; each can be changed before anything goes live.
