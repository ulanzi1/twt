# Story 6.16: 3-Stage Claim-Denial Appeal Flow + Reversed-Denial → Sahyog Vivran Publish Hook (FR-43A)

Status: done

<!-- Note: Validation is optional. Run validate-create-story for quality check before dev-story. -->

## Story

As a claimant whose claim was denied,
I want a structured 3-stage internal appeal process — **Stage 1** (a District Admin reviewer who is **not** the original verifier/decider) → **Stage 2** (a State Trustee panel vote) → **Stage 3** (Trustee discretion, final) — with a reversed denial emitting a publish hook for Sahyog Vivran,
so that procedural fairness is enforced end-to-end and reversed claims become publicly transparent.

**Story type:** `[SURFACE]` (FR-43A `[v1-M]`). This is the **last story of Epic 6** and its most cross-cutting surface. It wires **write-paths + routes + admin/member surfaces** on top of the appeal state machine **Story 6.1 already committed** — it does **NOT** modify the frozen reducer, the appeal lifecycle states, or the six appeal events (all authored in 6.1). It adds the appeal **decision-metadata** stores, the Stage-2 **panel** (the 6.14 R9 pattern, minus the clause registry), the reviewer-conflict enforcement, the `claim.reversed` **publish-hook event** Epic 11b consumes, and the legal-counsel-review go-live gate.

- **Reducer** (`packages/domain/src/claim/state.ts:267-294`): `denied → appeal_stage1_initiated → appeal_stage_1`; the three `appeal_stageN_reviewed` events branch on `payload.decision` (`advance → next stage | reversed → reversed | upheld → denied`); the `stage2/3_initiated` events are identity **formal-filing markers** (state already entered by the prior stage's `advance`). Stage 3 has **no** `advance` (Trustee discretion is final).
- **Events + payload schemas** (`packages/domain/src/claim/events.ts:383-474`): `ClaimAppealStage1InitiatedPayloadSchema`, `…Stage1ReviewedPayloadSchema` (carries `decision: appealReviewDecisionSchema`), `…Stage2InitiatedPayloadSchema` (identity), `…Stage2ReviewedPayloadSchema`, `…Stage3InitiatedPayloadSchema` (identity), `…Stage3ReviewedPayloadSchema` (carries `decision: appealFinalDecisionSchema` — reversed|upheld only). **Therefore: do NOT touch `state.ts` reducer logic or the six appeal-event schemas.** This story EMITS these existing events through new write-paths, exactly as 6.13/6.14/6.15 emit their events through `projectClaimState`. ### Ratified design decisions (D-A–D-H; D-A/D-C/D-D/D-E/D-G validated + revised, D-H added, by BigDev's 2026-07-15 story-validation pass; all binding on this story)
- **D-A — RATIFIED (validation-reconciled) — `claim.reversed` is a NEW dedicated identity-annotation event (the 31st claim event) — Epic 11b's SOLE reversed-denial publication hook, carrying a public NON-PII disposition tag.** A successful appeal reversal writes the applicable `claim.appeal_stageN_reviewed { decision: 'reversed' }` lifecycle transition and **then** `claim.reversed { reversed_at_stage, disposition_category }` **in the SAME transaction**. The publish-hook event is valid ONLY after the claim has reached `reversed`, has `from_state === to_state === 'reversed'` (a `requireIdentityTransition`), **changes no lifecycle state**, and **does not unfreeze** the deceased member's account. It is the ONE clean subscription point Epic 11b consumes (epics.md:3831-3834 names it by that exact type). Mirrors the owner-story-adds-a-timeline-annotation discipline (`peer_mesh_responded` 6.6, `verifier_escalated`/`verifier_decision_revised` 6.11, `shepherd_assigned` 6.12, `concealment_assessed` 6.15). (Option B — no new event, Epic 11b filters the three reviewed events by `decision==='reversed'` — was considered and REJECTED.)
  **Reconciling PRD §4.6's public-accountability requirement (legal/privacy decision):** the PRD calls for the reversal's decision + rationale + reviewer identity to be public on Sahyog Vivran. This is reconciled, NOT implemented verbatim — `claim.reversed` NEVER carries the Tier-1 rationale ciphertext or an individually-identifying reviewer name (publishing either leaks PII/encrypted content and exposes reviewers to targeting). Instead the reviewer selects `disposition_category` at decision time — a bounded, NON-PII enum tag (e.g. `new_evidence_presented` | `procedural_correction` | `reconsideration_on_merits`; the exact taxonomy + its public wording template is a D-G legal-counsel sign-off item, Task 10) stored on the `claim_appeal_decisions` row and copied into the `claim.reversed` payload. Accountability is conveyed via **stage + body**, never a named individual: "Reversed at Stage {N} ({District Admin reviewer | State Trustee panel | Trustee}) — {disposition_category}". Counsel confirms before go-live (D-G) that no category/stage/timing combination is linkable to an individual reviewer.
- **D-B — Stage 1 = single reviewer; Stage 2 = panel; Stage 3 = single trustee.** Stage 1 and Stage 3 are single-decider decisions (one `claim_appeal_decisions` row each). Stage 2 is a State Trustee **panel** (the 6.14 R9 sessions+votes pattern, **minus the niyamavali clause registry** — an appeal panel votes on the appeal, not on a clause). Its finalize writes a `claim_appeal_decisions` row (stage=2, uniform audit) alongside the panel session-outcome row. **Panel minimum size is 2** (PRD-mandated — stricter than R9's ≥1-derived floor; do not copy that floor verbatim, Task 1).
- **D-C — RATIFIED — Stage-1 and Stage-2 negative outcomes are NON-TERMINAL and AUTOMATICALLY advance the appeal.** Stage 1 maps "do not reverse" to `decision: 'advance'` → `appeal_stage_2`; Stage 2 maps a non-reversing panel outcome to `decision: 'advance'` → `appeal_stage_3`. Only **Stage 3** may finally uphold the denial via `decision: 'upheld'` → `denied` (final, binding). **In v1, Stages 1 & 2 NEVER emit `upheld`** — `reversed` or `advance` only. Intermediate surfaces MUST communicate the next-stage consequence explicitly and MUST NOT present the action as a final denial. **No separate claimant-escalation action and no intermediate escalation window are introduced** — advancement is automatic on a non-reversing outcome.
  **Explicit scope cut (v1, deliberate — not an oversight):** PRD §4.6 additionally describes a Stage-1 "request additional evidence / escalate" outcome and a Stage-2 "partial uphold under a 75/25 nominee-split" outcome. BOTH are explicitly DEFERRED out of v1 — resolved via explicit deferral, not silently dropped ([[feedback_closure_language_precision]]). Rationale: the binary reverse/advance model keeps the appeal ladder mechanically simple and TOTAL (AC9's reducer-stays-total discipline); an evidence-request state would require a NEW non-terminal appeal sub-state — a reducer change, out of scope per this story's "6.1 owns the machine" boundary — and the 75/25 partial-uphold has no existing disbursement primitive ([[project_nominee_bank_disbursement_channel]] — the existing dual-account mechanism is explicitly NOT this split). Both are candidate follow-up-story scope if a future epic revisits FR-43A's full outcome taxonomy; not built here.
- **D-D — Stage-1 reviewer-conflict is enforced server-side at BOTH the API and domain layers.** `reviewer_actor_id ∉ (original_verifier_ids ∪ original_state_trustee_decider_ids)`, derived from `claim_verifier_decisions` (all actors, live+superseded) and `claim_state_trustee_decisions` (all decider actors) + `claim_r9_votes` (panel voters, if R9-denied). A typed `AppealReviewerConflictError → 409`. The AC mandates this ONLY for Stage 1; Stages 2/3 conflict-exclusion is a documented open procedural-fairness question for legal counsel (D-G), not enforced in v1.
  **Note (intentional extension, confirmed — not scope creep):** including `claim_r9_votes` panel voters in the exclusion set deliberately strengthens separation-of-duties beyond epics.md's literal two-party list (original verifier + original State Trustee decider) — an R9 panel voter who already adjudicated this claim's special-case vote is, in substance, an original decider too.
- **D-E — RATIFIED (REVISED) — no claimant-facing appeal-initiation deadline; the trust-side SLA (D-H) governs pacing instead.** Initiation requires ONLY `current_state === 'denied'` AND the appeal ladder not already exhausted (D-F) — there is NO elapsed-time gate on the claimant's right to initiate. `AppealWindowExpiredError` and the 60-day hard-expiry design (epics.md L2581's "e.g., 60 days") are REMOVED from v1: they contradicted the PRD's explicit "no formal time limit on the family's right to appeal — grief-aware" language. `denial_occurred_at` is still snapshotted on the `claim_appeals` anchor for audit/SLA context (informational only — never a gate).
- **D-F — EXACTLY ONE appeal journey per claim; Stage 3 is exhaustive.** A claim has at most ONE appeal journey, ever — enforced by an **unconditional unique constraint on `claim_appeals.claim_case_id`** (NOT a partial `WHERE status='open'` index, which would permit a fresh journey after the first became `reversed`/`upheld_final`). After `appeal_stage3_reviewed(upheld) → denied`, the claim is NOT re-appealable; a Stage-1/2 `advance` is the only path deeper into the ladder.
- **D-G — legal-counsel procedural-fairness review is tracked separately + gates go-live (Story 0.13 pending-review pattern) — scope EXPANDED to cover external-remedy disclosure + the D-A disposition taxonomy.** The appeal procedural-fairness spec carries a structurally-visible "PENDING LEGAL REVIEW PER STORY 0.13" marker; a tracked config flag (`appeal_flow_legal_review_status`) gates the appeal flow from going LIVE per Pariwar until counsel returns; any counsel-flagged issue is addressed before the flag flips. Counsel's review MUST additionally cover, before go-live: **(1) the CPA-2019 no-judicial-challenge-mitigation disclosure** — the member surface (AC7) and the Stage-3-uphold outcome copy must state that exhausting the internal appeal does NOT waive the claimant's right to external legal/consumer-forum recourse (district/state consumer commission, civil court — architecture's regulatory cross-reference table + launch-gate row name this against FR-43A specifically); **(2) the D-A `disposition_category` taxonomy + its public wording template** — confirming no category/stage/timing combination could re-identify a reviewer or leak rationale. This is a documented tracking + go-live gate, NOT a code workflow (mirrors Story 0.4's per-surface pending-review marker discipline).
- **D-H — RATIFIED (NEW) — trust-side, internal-only, per-stage SLA tracking (Sprint Change Proposal Item 8 + architecture Cross-Cutting Concern #11).** Each stage carries a configurable, Pariwar-scoped SLA duration, measured from the claim's entry into that stage (the transition event's `occurred_at` — derived from event replay, no new event needed for the clock start). Elapsed-vs-SLA is computed AT READ TIME (not a cron/new event — the validity-cache fail-open discipline: derive-on-read, never a blocking write). A breach surfaces as: a flag on the AC6 audit query, an "overdue appeals" indicator on the admin stage surfaces (Task 8), and context fed to Story 0.7's fallback-handler/response-time-SLA framework (cross-referenced, NOT re-implemented — 0.7 owns escalation/paging). **This SLA is internal/trust-side ONLY and never blocks, expires, or gates the claimant's right to initiate or continue an appeal** — that would reintroduce the D-E claimant-facing deadline this decision explicitly removed.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **(AC1 — initiate the appeal from `denied`, no claimant-facing deadline)** A denied claimant (or the AR-61 helpline-mediated fallback path on their behalf) can initiate an appeal on a claim whose `current_state === 'denied'`, AT ANY TIME (D-E — no formal filing deadline, per PRD). Initiation emits `claim.appeal_stage1_initiated` via `projectClaimState` → `appeal_stage_1`, records an appeal anchor + audit line, and is idempotent/guarded: initiating on a non-`denied` claim, or on a claim whose appeal ladder was already terminally exhausted (D-F), throws a typed error mapped to a stable 4xx. Initiation records who initiated (claimant actor or operator-on-behalf) with attribution.

2. **(AC2 — Stage 1: District Admin reviewer ≠ original)** A District Admin reviewer, scope-gated on the new `claim.appeal_review` permission (district dimension), reviews the case at `appeal_stage_1`. The system enforces `reviewer_actor_id ∉ (original_verifier_ids ∪ original_state_trustee_decider_ids)` at the **API layer AND the domain write-path** (D-D; `AppealReviewerConflictError → 409`). Stage-1 outcome is **reverse** (`decision='reversed'` → `reversed`, fires the D-A publish hook) OR **deny** (`decision='advance'` → `appeal_stage_2`, per D-C). The decision writes a `claim_appeal_decisions` (stage=1) metadata row with reviewer identity + decision + a mandatory Tier-1-encrypted rationale + the R5 decision-time `actor_display` snapshot, in the SAME scope-tx as the lifecycle event.

3. **(AC3 — Stage 2: State Trustee panel vote)** When the claim is at `appeal_stage_2`, a State Trustee panel (the 6.14 R9 pattern minus the clause registry, D-B) votes: `open` captures the immutable panel roster (minimum 2 members, D-B; each member holds the new `claim.appeal_vote` key @ pariwar) + snapshots quorum + emits the `claim.appeal_stage2_initiated` marker (identity — the panel formally convenes); `vote` casts/revises a member's `reverse|deny` vote with a mandatory Tier-1 rationale (**no lifecycle event**); `cancel` supersedes an unfinalized session for correction (**no lifecycle event**); `finalize` (step-up-gated) tallies against the panel size and writes — in ONE scope-tx — the panel session outcome + a `claim_appeal_decisions` (stage=2) row + the lifecycle event. **Explicit tie rule (the burden of persuasion — FROZEN):** the appeal is reversed ONLY on a **strict majority of REVERSE votes over the panel size** (`reverse_count > panel_size / 2`, i.e. `reverse_count ≥ ⌊panel_size/2⌋ + 1`); a **tie advances**, and any non-reversing tally (once quorum is met) → `appeal_stage2_reviewed(decision='advance')` → `appeal_stage_3`. A strict reverse-majority → `appeal_stage2_reviewed(decision='reversed')` → `reversed` (fires the D-A hook). Abstention is expressed as a non-vote (an absent panelist counts against reversal — the R9 panel-size-denominator discipline). The tally helper's semantics are frozen and tested (odd/even panel sizes, quorum-only participation, abstention-by-non-voting, and ties — Task 11).

4. **(AC4 — Stage 3: Trustee discretion, final)** At `appeal_stage_3`, a Trustee (scope-gated on the new `claim.appeal_final` key, **Pariwar dimension in v1** — resolved, Task 7), step-up-gated, records a final discretion decision: **reverse** (`appeal_stage3_reviewed(decision='reversed')` → `reversed`, fires the D-A hook) OR **uphold** (`appeal_stage3_reviewed(decision='upheld')` → `denied`, final/binding within the internal appeal system). A `claim_appeal_decisions` (stage=3) row + the lifecycle event are written in ONE scope-tx. The stage-3 payload uses `appealFinalDecisionSchema` (no `advance` — an accidental advance is a Zod rejection, per 6.1). On an **uphold**, the write-path ALSO emits `claim.denied_no_appeal` (carrying `deceased_member_id`) in the same tx — the appeal ladder is exhausted, so this is the freeze-clearing terminal (the account-frozen overlay unfreezes on `denied_no_appeal`; without it a terminally-denied deceased member's account would stay frozen forever). The uphold outcome surface MUST present the D-G external-remedy disclosure (internal-appeal exhaustion does not waive external legal/consumer-forum recourse). After a stage-3 uphold the claim is NOT re-appealable (D-F unconditional uniqueness + a live guard).

5. **(AC5 — reversal fires the Sahyog Vivran publish hook)** Any stage reaching `reversed` emits, **in the SAME scope-tx as the `appeal_stageN_reviewed(reversed)` transition**, the NEW `claim.reversed` identity-annotation event (the 31st claim event, D-A) carrying the audit shape + `reversed_at_stage` (1|2|3) + `disposition_category` (D-A — a bounded NON-PII enum tag, never rationale text or an individual reviewer identity). The canonical `claim_case_id` (the event stream id, per the Story 6.4 immutability invariant) identifies the claim for Epic 11b's publish-queue consumer. The reversed claim naturally re-enters the approval pipeline via the EXISTING `reversed → claim.state_trustee_frozen → state_trustee_freeze` edge (6.13 already treats `reversed` as freezable/votable — no new work). `claim.reversed` does NOT unfreeze the account (it is absent from `ACCOUNT_UNFREEZE_EVENT_TYPES` — the deceased member's freeze persists until `settled`/`denied_no_appeal`).

6. **(AC6 — every stage is audit-logged + queryable by reviewer)** Each appeal stage writes an audit-sink line with reviewer identity, decision, and rationale reference (rationale text itself is Tier-1, never on the audit line/event). A trustee-side audit read answers "show me all appeal **stage 1** decisions where reviewer = X" (the 6.11 `actor + reason + time_range` audit-query pattern) — bounded, clamped, scope-respecting, indexed on the non-PII `(actor_id, stage, decided_at)` columns.

7. **(AC7 — member-facing appeal surface + AR-61 staff fallback + external-remedy disclosure)** A denied claimant sees appeal eligibility (no deadline, D-E) + a "file appeal" affordance on the mobile claim-status surface, and can see the appeal's stage/outcome as it progresses. The surface (and the Stage-3-uphold outcome view specifically) states, in the D-G-approved disclosure copy, that pursuing/exhausting the internal appeal does NOT waive the claimant's right to external legal/consumer-forum recourse (district/state consumer commission, civil court). The AR-61 helpline-mediated fallback path lets an operator initiate/track an appeal on the claimant's behalf (per Story 0.7's fallback-handler ledger, referenced not re-implemented). Copy is bilingual (i18n) + tone-guide compliant (Story 2.2), in the dignified "fursat" register.

8. **(AC8 — legal-counsel procedural-fairness gate, D-G — expanded scope)** The appeal procedural-fairness specification carries a structurally-visible "PENDING LEGAL REVIEW PER STORY 0.13" marker; the appeal flow is gated from going LIVE per Pariwar by a tracked `appeal_flow_legal_review_status` config until counsel returns; counsel-flagged issues are addressed before the gate flips. Counsel's review scope explicitly includes (D-G): the CPA-2019 no-judicial-challenge-mitigation disclosure copy (AC7) and the D-A `disposition_category` taxonomy + public wording template. The legal-review status is tracked separately from implementation completion ([[feedback_closure_language_precision]] — "resolved via deferral" vs "closed by edit" vs "not addressed" kept distinct).

9. **(AC9 — concurrency + two-authority-write invariants, inherited)** Every lifecycle-changing appeal verb takes a per-claim transaction-scoped advisory lock on a DISTINCT namespace prefix (`appeal:`) from the verifier/cycle-freeze/r9 locks; writes BOTH the lifecycle event (via `projectClaimState` — the sole `claims.current_state` writer) AND the decision-metadata row in ONE scope-tx so they can never diverge; the `events_log (stream_id, event_version)` unique index + the appeal tables' partial-uniques are the structural backstops. Claim STATE is ALWAYS derived from event replay, never from the appeal tables. The reducer stays TOTAL — every "is this appealable/reviewable/finalizable" guard lives in the write-path, not `state.ts`.

10. **(AC10 — new adjudication routes are covered by the human-actor CI gate)** Every new **administrative appeal-adjudication route** (stage 1 review; stage 2 open/vote/finalize/cancel; stage 3 decision) requires an authenticated HUMAN actor and is added to the `scripts/claim-adjudication-human-actor-invariant/check.ts` coverage set (the 6.11/6.13/6.14/6.15 gate-extension discipline). The gate covers these because they are high-stakes adjudication operations — **NOT** because every one emits a lifecycle event: within Stage 2, only `open` (the `claim.appeal_stage2_initiated` identity marker) and `finalize` (the reviewed transition + possibly `claim.reversed`) emit claim events; `vote` and `cancel` emit **no** claim lifecycle event (metadata-only, the 6.14 R9 precedent). Stage 1 review and stage 3 decision emit their reviewed transitions. The reverse/deny-committing writes (stage-2 `finalize`, stage-3 decision) are additionally step-up-gated (the 6.14 finalize / 6.13 commit precedent), given the ₹50L stakes.

11. **(AC11 — trust-side per-stage SLA tracking, D-H)** Each appeal stage carries a configurable, Pariwar-scoped SLA duration measured from the claim's entry into that stage (the transition event's `occurred_at`, derived from event replay — no new event required for the clock start). Elapsed-time-vs-SLA is computed at read time (never a cron/new event) and surfaces as a breach flag on the AC6 audit query and an "overdue appeals" indicator on the admin stage surfaces (Task 8); breach context is exposed for Story 0.7's fallback-handler/response-time-SLA framework to consume (cross-referenced, not re-implemented). This SLA is internal/trust-side ONLY and never blocks, expires, or gates the claimant's right to initiate or continue an appeal (D-E).

### The decision→target mapping (the ONE place semantics could go wrong) — D-C RATIFIED, WITH AN EXPLICIT V1 SCOPE CUT
`resolveAppealReview` in `state.ts:90-105` is the reducer authority: `advance → advanceTo`, `reversed → 'reversed'`, `upheld → 'denied'`. Per the RATIFIED D-C, the surfaces emit:
- **Stage 1 & 2 (non-terminal, auto-advance):** *Reverse* → `decision:'reversed'`; *do-not-reverse* → `decision:'advance'` (the appeal automatically advances to the next stage). **In v1 Stages 1 & 2 NEVER emit `upheld`** — the contract decision enum for stages 1/2 is `'reversed'|'advance'` ONLY, so the reducer's `upheld` branch is unreachable from these surfaces. There is no separate claimant-escalation action and no intermediate escalation window. Intermediate surfaces must state the next-stage consequence and must NOT present a non-reversal as a final denial.
- **Stage 3 (final):** *Reverse* → `'reversed'`; *Uphold* → `'upheld'` (→ `denied`, final) + `claim.denied_no_appeal` in the same tx (the freeze-clearing terminal). Stage-3 payload schema is `appealFinalDecisionSchema` (no `advance` — a Zod rejection).

**Do not "improve" this into the PRD's fuller outcome taxonomy.** PRD §4.6 separately describes a Stage-1 "request additional evidence/escalate" outcome and a Stage-2 75/25-split partial-uphold outcome — BOTH are explicitly deferred (D-C's scope-cut note). If you find yourself adding a fourth decision value anywhere in this story's write-paths or contracts, stop — that's out of v1 scope, not a bug to fix.

`claim.denied_no_appeal` (existing 6.1 event) is emitted in exactly ONE place in this story's write-paths: a **stage-3 uphold** (the appeal is exhausted — unfreeze). (D-E removed the appeal-window concept entirely, so there is no window-lapse path to this event either — do not build one.) Reversal never emits it (a reversed claim re-enters approval — freeze persists).

### `claim.reversed` — the publish hook (D-A), reconciled with the reducer AND the PRD's public-accountability requirement
The reducer reaches `reversed` via `appeal_stageN_reviewed(reversed)`. `claim.reversed` is a SECOND event appended in the SAME tx, when the claim is already `reversed` — a `requireIdentityTransition` (`from_state===to_state==='reversed'`), reducer identity. It exists so Epic 11b has ONE clean subscription point (epics.md:3831-3834: "When Story 6.16 emits `claim.reversed` event … the publish hook consumer routes the claim to Sahyog Vivran publication queue"). Two events per reversal is intentional and idiomatic: the `appeal_stageN_reviewed(reversed)` event is the DECISION (carries reviewer attribution + Tier-1 rationale in the metadata row, never public); `claim.reversed` is the downstream PUBLISH SIGNAL (carries `reversed_at_stage` + `disposition_category` for the "Reversed by appeal" narrative Epic 11b renders). `disposition_category` is the reconciliation of the PRD's "rationale + reviewer identity public" ask — a bounded NON-PII tag chosen by the reviewer at decision time, NEVER the rationale ciphertext or an individual's name (D-A; taxonomy + wording is a D-G legal sign-off item). Do NOT wire `claim.reversed` into the account-frozen overlay — a reversed claim re-enters approval, and the deceased-member freeze must persist until `settled` (`member/overlay.ts:34` `ACCOUNT_UNFREEZE_EVENT_TYPES` stays `['claim.settled','claim.denied_no_appeal']`).

### SLA tracking (D-H) is read-time-derived, not a new write-path
Architecture's Cross-Cutting Concern #11 and the Sprint Change Proposal both name "SLA tracking" as part of FR-43A — this is satisfied by `computeStageSlaStatus` (Task 3): a pure read comparing the stage-entry event's `occurred_at` against a Pariwar-scoped config duration, exposed on the AC6 audit query and the admin "overdue" indicator. It is entirely internal/trust-side (never a claimant-facing gate — that's D-E's job to NOT do) and entirely derived-at-read (never a cron, never a new event, mirroring [[project_validity_cache_failopen_pattern]]'s hit≡recompute discipline). If you find yourself writing a scheduled job or a new lifecycle event for SLA breaches, stop — that's over-building this decision.

### Reversal re-enters the approval pipeline for free
`state.ts:324` already has `{ from: 'reversed', event: 'claim.state_trustee_frozen', to: 'state_trustee_freeze' }`, and `TRUSTEE_VOTABLE_STATES` (state-trustee-decision-persist.ts:72) already includes `'reversed'`.
