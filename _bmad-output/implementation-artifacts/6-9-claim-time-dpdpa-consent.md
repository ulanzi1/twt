# Story 6.9: Claim-Time DPDPA Consent (Consumer of Story 2.7)

Status: done

<!-- Note: Validation is optional. Run validate-create-story for quality check before dev-story. -->

## Story

As a **claim filer** (nominee/family in Ravi-mode on the deceased's phone, or a helpline operator recording during intake),
I want to capture **explicit, granular DPDPA consent at claim-time** — three independent opt-ins covering (a) the trust's processing of deceased + claimant + nominee PII, (b) contributor-list + verifier-name publication on the deceased's Sahyog Vivran page (Epic 11b), and (c) In Memoriam appearance,
so that the public-transparency surfaces (Epic 11b) can publish **lawfully**, each consent is recorded with **full provenance** (locale + checkbox text + timestamp + audit line) via the Story 2.7 primitive, and a family that wants **no public memorial** can decline (b)/(c) **without compromising disbursement**.

## Story Classification

`[CONSUMER]` — this story wires the **Story 2.7 consent-registry primitive** (`recordConsent` / `revokeConsent` / `consentExists`, `consent_records` table + RLS) into two running claim-filing surfaces. It records REAL consent for the first time under the `claim_time_dpdpa` / `sahyog_vivran_publication` / `in_memoriam_listing` types (Story 2.7 shipped the primitive and recorded nothing). It **owns + first-emits** a required NEW annotation claim event `claim.dpdpa_consent_recorded` (identity transition, the 6.8 `claim.nominee_bank_recorded` precedent), **extends the `consent_type` pgEnum** with the two publication values via an additive `ALTER TYPE … ADD VALUE` migration (the 0040/0048 precedent), and adds the reserved `(claim)/consent.tsx` wizard step (the slot is **already pre-cut** in `apps/mobile/lib/claim-steps.ts`). It **does NOT** build a new PII table, any envelope-encryption (consent payload is non-Tier-1 internal jsonb per Story 2.7), any new port, the Sahyog Vivran / In Memoriam **rendering** (Epic 11b), or the member-facing "take down my memorial" management UI (Epic 11b / a settings surface). Per the Epic 6 dependency-boundary note (epics.md:2270), Epic 6 must not depend on Epic 11b's publication surfaces — 6.9 only **captures + records** consent and ships the **revocation mechanism**; 11b gates rendering on `consentExists` and performs the takedown.

## Seam decisions — surfaced for confirmation

Like Story 6.7's D1–D6 and Story 6.8's D1–D6, these touch a cross-story consumer contract (Epic 11b), a shared frozen primitive (Story 2.7), and a hard product invariant (private-processing-without-compromising-disbursement). Recommendations are given; **confirm or override before/at dev-time.** **D1 (subject identity) is correctness-and-contract-shaping** — get it wrong and Epic 11b queries `consentExists` on the wrong key and either publishes without valid consent or never publishes at all.

- **Decision D1 (consent subject identity) — ✅ APPROVED (BigDev); canonical subject-key semantics clarified + artifact-scoped lookup verified below.** `subject_id` = the DECEASED member's id (`claims.deceased_member_id`); `consent_artifact_ref` = the `claim_case_id`. Story 2.7's `consent_records.subject_id` is "the member-or-pre-member-applicant id" — semantically **the DPDPA data subject** whose PII/case/memorial is processed and published. For all three claim-time consents that subject is the **deceased member** (the person whose case is adjudicated and whose Sahyog Vivran / In Memoriam surfaces render), **NOT** the claim filer / nominee (they are the *grantor* acting on the family's behalf, not the data subject) and **NOT** the `claim_case_id` (that is the artifact/context, not the subject). `deceased_member_id` is available on the claim row (the `claims.nominee-bank.handlers.ts:169,325` ownership-assertion precedent reads `claimRow.deceasedMemberId`). **`subject_id` takes NO brand** (Story 2.7 kept it polymorphic-no-FK on purpose) — pass the raw uuid string.

  - **D1a — CANONICAL SUBJECT-KEY SEMANTICS (the query grain Epic 11b MUST use).** The canonical lookup key is **`(pariwar_id, deceased_member_id, consent_type)`** — it is **member-scoped, NOT claim-scoped**. This is the *correct* grain for a memorial: a family consents to **the member's** Sahyog Vivran / In Memoriam appearance, not to a claim-instance. Epic 11b, rendering a claim's memorial, resolves the key via `claim.deceasedMemberId → consentExists(pariwarId, deceasedMemberId, 'sahyog_vivran_publication')`. Because one death produces exactly one *canonical* claim (post-ICP convergence, Story 6.4), member-scoped and per-death are the same set in practice — but the **member** is the durable key, and a re-grant after revoke is a new row that `consentExists` resolves at the query instant (Story 2.7 grant→revoke→re-grant semantics).
  - **D1b — ARTIFACT-SCOPED LOOKUP: VERIFIED ABSENT — `consent_artifact_ref` is PROVENANCE-ONLY, never a query dimension.** Confirmed against `packages/domain/src/consent/read.ts`: `consentExists(pariwarId, subjectId, consentType, validAt?)` and `listConsents(pariwarId, subjectId, opts?)` expose **NO `artifact_ref` filter** — there is no artifact-scoped query in the 2.7 primitive, and `claims.deceased_member_id` carries only a **non-unique** index (`claims_deceased_member_id_idx`; the PK is `claim_case_id`). Consequence for the consumer contract: **Epic 11b MUST query by `(pariwar_id, deceased_member_id, consent_type)`, NOT by `claim_case_id`.** The `claim_case_id` in `consent_artifact_ref` (+ echoed in `consent_payload`) is a back-link for audit/provenance only — do NOT design 11b (or any consumer) to look up consent by it, and do NOT add a `claim_case_id` filter/column to the consent primitive (Story 2.7's `consent_artifact_ref` free-form-text-ref is exactly this provenance role, mirroring `tc_acceptance → tc_version_id`). If a future requirement genuinely needs per-claim (not per-member) consent resolution, that is a Story 2.7 primitive change (a new artifact-scoped accessor), NOT something 6.9 bolts on. Record the member-scoped-key convention in the ADR (Task 7) so Epic 11b reads the same grain.

- **Decision D2 (enum extension — 6.9 owns adding the two publication values) — ✅ APPROVED (BigDev): add `sahyog_vivran_publication` + `in_memoriam_listing` to the `consent_type` pgEnum via an additive `ALTER TYPE … ADD VALUE IF NOT EXISTS` migration (0058), + the lockstep contracts `z.enum` update.** The epic AC (epics.md:2461) names three checkbox types; only `claim_time_dpdpa` currently exists in the enum (Story 2.7 seeded the 7 AC1 values). Story 2.7's Dev Notes *tentatively* assigned `sahyog_vivran_publication`/`in_memoriam_listing` to "Epic 11b" — **that assignment is superseded by where the CONSENT is actually captured.** Consent is captured at **claim-time (6.9)** and only *consumed* at publication-time (11b); the enum value must exist when the first writer records it, so **6.9 — the first writer — owns the additive extension** (exactly as Story 5.4 owned `whatsapp_opt_in` / migration 0040 and Story 5.5 owned `telegram_opt_in` / migration 0048, even though the "consumers" live elsewhere). Update `@twt/contracts` `ConsentTypeSchema` (the `z.enum`) with the two values **in the same PR** so the Story 2.7 `packages/contracts/tests/consent.test.ts` dual-lockstep (`[...ConsentTypeSchema.options].sort()` deep-equals `[...schema.consentTypeEnum.enumValues].sort()`) stays green — a missed lockstep update is the predictable failure here.

- **Decision D3 (mandatory-vs-optional + the no-disbursement-block invariant) — ✅ APPROVED (BigDev) for the optional/publication posture; the (a)-required rule is GATED on a Story 0.13 legal determination (see D3a).** UX-DR2 / UX §12 Stance #1 (ux-design-specification.md:79, :592): "**Default opt-in is unacceptable; explicit consent + opt-out path required.** A bereaved family that wants no public memorial must be able to choose private processing of the claim **without compromising disbursement**." So (APPROVED, firm): all three boxes render **UNCHECKED** (explicit opt-in, never pre-ticked); `sahyog_vivran_publication` (b) + `in_memoriam_listing` (c) are the **public-transparency opt-ins** and declining either/both **MUST NOT block** claim progression, verification, approval, or disbursement — the step is completable with **neither (b) nor (c)** and the claim proceeds normally.

  - **D3a — the (a) `claim_time_dpdpa` required-to-proceed rule is UN-ATTESTED-PENDING a Story 0.13 legal determination — implement the recommended default, but GATE it (do not silently harden).** Recommended default: (a) — the trust-processing consent that permits adjudication + disbursement of the claim's PII — is a **precondition of advancing** the wizard to `document` (you cannot file a claim while forbidding processing of the claim's own data). **BUT this is a legal question, not an engineering one.** Story 0.13 (`done`) signed the legal-counsel engagement whose scope **explicitly includes "DPDPA consent flow design review"** — it did **not** itself produce a determination on whether processing-consent is mandatory-to-file or how it must be worded. So per [[feedback_record_unattested_no_backfill]] (un-gated action items decay → give the re-commitment a gate): build (a)-required as the default, but **flag it in the Dev Agent Record as pending Story 0.13 counsel confirmation**, and structure the code so the answer is a **copy + one guard-flag change**, not a structural one — if counsel says (a) must also be optional (or must be split/re-worded, e.g. a separate "necessary-processing notice" that is *informed* not *consented*), flipping the gate is trivial. **Gate:** the dev MUST either cite an attested 0.13 counsel answer on this specific rule, or record (a)-required as un-attested-pending and carry it as an open risk into review — never present it as legally settled.

- **Decision D4 (`granted_via_actor`) — ✅ APPROVED (BigDev): member-app path → `'member_self'`; helpline path → `'staff_assisted'`. NOT `'inherited'`.** The `consent_granted_via` enum is `member_self | staff_assisted | inherited` (Story 2.7). Ravi-mode captures consent through the member app (the family operating the deceased's phone under a handover-trust session) → `member_self`, mirroring the medical (3.5) and WA-opt-in (5.4) `member_self` usage. The helpline operator recording with read-back → `staff_assisted`. `inherited` is reserved for a genuinely different successor-consent-carryover case and is **not** this flow (the filer gives *fresh* consent, they do not inherit an existing one).

- **Decision D5 (dual surface — the slot is pre-reserved) — ✅ APPROVED (BigDev): two routes → one shared core; permission semantics resolved in D5a.** Member-app: a NEW `apps/mobile/app/(claim)/consent.tsx` wizard step, activated by **uncommenting `'consent'` in `apps/mobile/lib/claim-steps.ts`** — the Story 6.2 author **pre-cut the slot** (AFTER `relationship`, BEFORE `document`) with a header note: "inserting `'consent'` here later is a one-line change that reshuffles NO existing typed-route literals." Helpline: a consent read-back step in the operator intake console (`claims.helpline.*`, the Priya-path). The two surfaces are **two separately authorized routes → one shared handler / core write path** (the 6.2/6.3/6.8 "two thin surfaces, one core" precedent) — each route carries its own auth chain (member session vs admin scope + permission), and both delegate to the SAME handler/writer. **Do NOT collapse into a single route** and **do NOT invent a new wizard slot** — use the reserved one.

  - **D5a — PERMISSION SEMANTICS (BigDev: "verify permissions semantically") — RECORD path reuses `claim.file`; REVOKE path needs its own semantic call.** Verified against `apps/api/src/modules/claims/claims.helpline.routes.ts`: the intake gate is `[requireAdminSession, scopeResolutionHook, requirePermissionHook('claim.file'), requireStepUp('claim_file')]` (catalog v7), and the 6.8 review found `claim.file` reuse a **"semantic-scope mismatch"** for *management* actions, minting `claim.manage_nominee_bank` / `claim.correct_nominee_bank`. Applying that lesson here:
    - **RECORD consent (helpline, at claim-time):** consent capture happens **during intake, by the filing operator, once** — it is semantically **part of filing a claim** (more intrinsic to filing than nominee-bank, which is separately correctable later). → **Reuse `claim.file`** + `requireStepUp('claim_file')` (match the intake chain for consistency); **no catalog bump.** Member path: `requireMemberSession` only (the member's own session — no permission key, no step-up; consent is not a financial action, unlike 6.8 bank entry).
    - **REVOKE consent (helpline) — SETTLED: a dedicated `claim.manage_dpdpa_consent` permission (NOT `claim.file`).** Revocation occurs **much later** (a post-settlement takedown request, D7), by an operator who is **not** filing anything — semantically a *withdrawal/management* action, not filing. Reusing `claim.file` would knowingly reproduce the exact permission-scope problem the 6.8 review corrected when it replaced `claim.file` reuse with `claim.manage_nominee_bank`. So mint **`claim.manage_dpdpa_consent`**, bump `PERMISSION_CATALOG_VERSION`, and grant it to **`helpline_operator` + `pariwar_admin`** (mirroring the `claim.manage_nominee_bank` grant shape). This is settled; the only dev-time step is a **catalog-version lookup before editing** (verify the current `PERMISSION_CATALOG_VERSION` + the exact `claim.manage_nominee_bank` role grants to mirror) — not a remaining product decision.

- **Decision D6 (claim annotation event) — ✅ APPROVED (BigDev) AND REQUIRED: emit a NEW `claim.dpdpa_consent_recorded` identity annotation event (the 24th claim event), NON-PII payload only.** Mirror the 6.8 `claim.nominee_bank_recorded` posture exactly: a `from_state === to_state` identity transition via `projectClaimState` (the ONLY legal `claims.current_state` writer), so the claim timeline durably records "DPDPA consent captured at claim-time." Payload carries **only** the non-PII per-type outcome flags — `consent_types_granted: ('claim_time_dpdpa' | 'sahyog_vivran_publication' | 'in_memoriam_listing')[]` (the granted subset) — **NO** checkbox text, **NO** subject identity beyond the ambient `claim_case_id`. Add the identity reducer case (reducer STAYS total), register in `EVENT_TYPE_REGISTRY`, and update whichever events test currently owns the exact-count invariant (23 → 24; verify at dev-time — 6.8 left `nominee-bank-events.test.ts` owning it). **This is now a firm requirement, not optional** — the event is part of the claim's evidentiary timeline (a revoke later must be explainable against "consent was captured at claim-time"), so a `consent_records` row + audit line alone is insufficient. **Emit it ONLY when ≥1 grant row is written** — the payload's `consent_types_granted` is always non-empty, and an all-declined submission (only reachable if the D3a guard is later disabled) emits NO grant row and NO event ("consent recorded" must never mean "nothing was granted"). (Note: unlike medical 3.5, which emits a *member* projector event, this is a *claim* annotation event — the 6.8-consistent choice for a claim-time action.)

- **Decision D7 (revocation scope) — ✅ APPROVED (BigDev): ship the revocation MECHANISM here (domain `revokeConsent` + a member/helpline revoke route + the "honored" test); the member-facing "take down my memorial" management UI + the actual page takedown live in Epic 11b.** Epic AC3 (epics.md:2463): "revocation of any of these (`revokeConsent`) is honored — e.g., if a family later revokes Sahyog Vivran publication, **the page is taken down (Epic 11b consumer)**." The takedown is explicitly an **11b consumer** concern (11b renders only when `consentExists` is true; a revoke → the next render check fails → the page is down) and the Epic 6 dependency-boundary note forbids Epic 6 depending on 11b's publication surfaces. So 6.9 ships: (1) a revoke route (member + helpline) that calls `revokeConsent` under audit-or-throw for `sahyog_vivran_publication` / `in_memoriam_listing`, and (2) a test proving `consentExists(...)` flips to `false` at a current timestamp after revoke and stays `true` at a pre-revocation timestamp (the AC3 time-travel crux, already covered by Story 2.7's primitive tests — 6.9 asserts it through its own consumer path). Firmly: **Story 6.9 ships the member and helpline revoke routes plus the honored consumer-path test. Epic 11b owns the member-facing consent-management UI and the publication-takedown behavior.** 6.9 does NOT ship a polished member-facing consent-management screen.

## Acceptance Criteria

> Lifted from epics.md §Story 6.9 (lines 2451–2463) + UX-DR2 (epics.md:372, ux-design-specification.md:79/205/592) + FR-97 (epics.md:175). Consent-type / actor enum names are the Story 2.7 `consent_type` / `consent_granted_via` pgEnum values (the authoritative source). Recommendations in the Seam Decisions above are folded in.

**AC1 — Three granular, independent, explicit-opt-in consent checkboxes; enum extended (D1, D2, D3).**
**Given** UX-DR2 + the Story 2.7 consent registry (`consent_records` + `recordConsent`)
**When** the claim-time DPDPA consent flow renders
**Then** it presents **three independent** consent checkboxes mapped to three `consent_type` values — (a) `claim_time_dpdpa` (the trust's processing of deceased + claimant + nominee PII), (b) `sahyog_vivran_publication` (contributor-list + verifier-name publication on the deceased's Sahyog Vivran page), (c) `in_memoriam_listing` (In Memoriam appearance)
**And** the `consent_type` pgEnum is extended with `sahyog_vivran_publication` + `in_memoriam_listing` via an additive `ALTER TYPE … ADD VALUE IF NOT EXISTS` migration (the 0040/0048 precedent; `claim_time_dpdpa` already exists from Story 2.7), and the `@twt/contracts` `ConsentTypeSchema` `z.enum` is updated in lockstep (the Story 2.7 `consent.test.ts` dual-lockstep stays green)
**And** all three boxes render **UNCHECKED** by default (explicit opt-in — never pre-ticked, UX-DR2); declining (b) and/or (c) **never blocks** claim progression / verification / approval / disbursement (UX-DR2 "private processing must not compromise disbursement" — D3, firm); (a) is required to advance the step **per the D3a recommended default, which is un-attested-pending a Story 0.13 legal confirmation** (build it as a single guard-flag, flag it in the Dev Agent Record, do not present it as legally settled)

**AC2 — Each checked consent independently recorded via `recordConsent` under audit-or-throw, with full provenance (D1, D4, D6).**
**Given** the claim filer submits the consent step
**When** consent is recorded
**Then** for **each checked** box exactly one `recordConsent` grant row is inserted via the Story 2.7 domain accessor — `subject_id = deceasedMemberId` (D1, taken from the claim row re-read + **locked inside the scope-tx**, see AC5 — never a route-level pre-check), `consent_artifact_ref = claim_case_id` (D1), `consent_type` = the box's type, `granted_via_actor` = `member_self` (member app) / `staff_assisted` (helpline) (D4), and `consent_payload = { checkboxTextShown, locale }` where **`checkboxTextShown` is the SERVER-resolved canonical/versioned consent copy for that `locale`** (the client sends only the locale + which boxes, NOT the text — the medical `ackTextForLocale` precedent, so a tampered client cannot persist non-approved copy) and **`locale` is the constrained `['en','hi']` enum** (the `MedicalAckLocale` / `MemberTermsLocale` precedent, never an arbitrary string); `granted_at` is DB-authoritative `now()` — the timestamp requirement of epics.md:2462
**And** the recording runs inside the **audit-or-throw chain** (`audit.withCompensatingAudit`, ADR-0030 — the medical 3.5 consumer precedent): the audit intent line is written FIRST (`servicePool`, its own tx → `auditId`), each `recordConsent` threads that `auditId`, and the consent inserts + the claim event all run on ONE member/operator scope-tx so a throw rolls them back together while the intent line survives and a compensating `*_rolled_back` line settles the chain — a consent row is **never** written without its audit line (the Story 2.7 "consumer owns audit-or-throw" obligation)
**And** a `claim.dpdpa_consent_recorded` annotation event is emitted via `projectClaimState` as an **identity transition** (D6 — required whenever consent is recorded) **ONLY when at least one grant row was written**; its payload carries the non-PII `consent_types_granted` subset (**always non-empty**) — **NO PII**, no checkbox text, no subject id. If the D3a guard is later disabled and a filer continues with **zero** boxes checked, **no grant row and no `claim.dpdpa_consent_recorded` event are emitted** — "consent recorded" must never mean "no consent was granted" (this keeps the D3a legal-posture flip a pure guard/copy change, per D3a's promise)
**And** **NO PII** reaches any audit line or event payload (checkbox text + locale live only in `consent_payload`; the audit hash + event carry `claim_case_id` + the granted type flags + actor only — the 6.7/6.8 NO-PII-in-payload discipline)

**AC3 — Revocation is honored (the mechanism ships; 11b performs the takedown) (D7).**
**Given** a family later wishes to withdraw a publication consent
**When** `revokeConsent` is called for `sahyog_vivran_publication` (or `in_memoriam_listing`) via 6.9's revoke path
**Then** the Story 2.7 `revokeConsent` sets `revoked_at` + `revocation_reason` + `revoked_audit_id` (the row is **never deleted** — historical proof preserved), under the same audit-or-throw chain
**And** a downstream `consentExists(pariwarId, deceasedMemberId, 'sahyog_vivran_publication')` returns **false** at a current timestamp and **true** at a pre-revocation timestamp (the AC3 time-travel semantics the Story 2.7 primitive guarantees), so Epic 11b's render-time gate takes the page down on the next check
**And** the actual page **takedown** + the member-facing "manage my memorial consent" UI are **Epic 11b consumer** concerns (epics.md:2463, :2270) — 6.9 ships the revoke route + the honored-test, not the memorial-management screen

**AC4 — Dual surface: member-app (Ravi-mode) wizard step + helpline (operator) read-back; one shared core (D5).**
**Given** UX-DR2 consent is captured on both the Story 6.2 member-app claim flow and the Story 6.3 helpline console
**When** the consent surfaces are implemented
**Then** the member-app adds a NEW `(claim)/consent.tsx` wizard step (activate the **reserved** `'consent'` slot in `lib/claim-steps.ts`, positioned AFTER `relationship`, BEFORE `document`) rendering the three checkboxes with grief-register / Pattern-4 dignified copy (UX §7) + bilingual `claim` i18n keys, and the helpline console adds an operator consent read-back step; the two surfaces are **two separately authorized routes → one shared handler / core write path** (the 6.2/6.3/6.8 dual-surface-one-core precedent — each route carries its own auth chain, both delegate to the same handler/writer)
**And** the member-app step is reachable inside the existing post-handover-OTP wizard (no additional step-up — consent capture is not a financial action, unlike 6.8 bank entry) and honors the save-and-resume draft thread; the helpline route runs behind the operator's existing admin session + the intake permission (D5a below)

**AC5 — Tenant isolation, claim-ownership / permission gating, and a pre-adjudication recording guard.**
**Given** the multi-tenant substrate + the Story 2.7 tenant-isolated `consent_records` RLS
**When** any consent read/write/revoke runs
**Then** all `consent_records` access is RLS-isolated on `app.pariwar_id` (Story 2.7 posture, NOT cross-readable) with an explicit `pariwarId` predicate on every accessor call (defense-in-depth); a cross-tenant `claimCaseId` guess resolves to empty/`404`, never another Pariwar's data
**And** the member route asserts the claim's `deceased_member_id` matches the session member (the 6.8 claim-ownership precedent — a filer cannot record consent onto another member's claim); the helpline **RECORD** route runs the operator's admin scope + `claim.file` + `requireStepUp('claim_file')` (consent capture is part of claim intake, D5a), while the helpline **REVOKE** route requires the dedicated **`claim.manage_dpdpa_consent`** permission — revocation is a later consent-management action and is **not** authorized by `claim.file` (D5a settled, the 6.8 `claim.manage_nominee_bank` precedent)
**And** the shared writer **re-reads and LOCKS the claim inside the scope-tx** (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on `claims`, the 6.8 precedent); the ownership, tenant, state, and `subjectId = deceasedMemberId` values used for enforcement all come from **that locked row** — any route-level read is only a clean-error pre-check and is **NOT** the enforcement point (this closes the TOCTOU window on both the state guard and D1's subject binding — a claim cannot mutate between the pre-check and the write)
**And** consent **recording** is guarded to the pre-adjudication window (the claim is in an early state during intake — e.g. `intake_pending | intake_converged | documents_pending`; reject recording onto a terminal/settled claim, the 6.8 write-path-guard discipline), while **revocation** is allowed at any later state (the whole point of AC3 is a post-settlement takedown) — verify the exact allow-set against `schema/claims.ts` state names at dev-time (the literal `under_verification` does NOT exist, nor is `intake_initiated` a state — that string is the claim EVENT type `claim.intake_initiated`; the real initial state is `intake_pending`, and other enum states are `verification_in_progress` etc. — the 6.8 D2 note)

### The consumer precedent to mirror: the medical-disclosure handler (Story 3.5)
`apps/api/src/modules/medical/medical.handlers.ts` is "the FIRST consent-registry consumer in apps/api" and is the template for 6.9's write path. The ordering (medical.handlers.ts:196-264): resolve context → `audit.withCompensatingAudit(servicePool, { auditIntent, mutate })` → inside `mutate({ auditId })`: `consent.recordConsent(scopeTx.tx, { pariwarId, subjectId, consentType, consentArtifactRef, grantedViaActor: 'member_self', consentPayload: { checkboxTextShown, locale }, auditId })` → `projectXState(...)` event → `ok = true` → `emitAuthAudit(...)` fire-and-forget last. The WA-opt-in handler (`apps/api/src/modules/wa-opt-in/handlers.ts:222-250`) is the **revoke** precedent: `audit.withCompensatingAudit` → `mutate({ auditId })` → `consent.revokeConsent(scopeTx.tx, { pariwarId, consentId, reason, revokedAuditId: auditId })`. 6.9 differs only in recording **up to three** consents in one `mutate` (each an independent `recordConsent` threading the same `auditId`).

### Locked-read is the enforcement point (TOCTOU) — the 6.8 discipline
A route-level claim read (for a clean 404/409 UX pre-check) is fine, but it is **NOT authoritative**. The shared writer re-reads and **locks** the claim (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) **inside the scope-tx**, and the values that enforce anything — tenant, claim-ownership, the pre-adjudication state guard, and `subjectId = deceasedMemberId` (D1's subject binding) — are read from **that locked row**. Otherwise a dev could read the claim, validate ownership, THEN open the tx and write consent against a stale claim context (a claim that transitioned, or worse, a subject that drifted). This is exactly the 6.8 posture (`recordClaimNomineeBankAccounts` locks the `claims` row then guards). The lock protects both the state window and D1.

### Consent-copy integrity — the server owns the canonical copy (the medical precedent)
`checkboxTextShown` is **evidence** of what the family agreed to, so it must be the trust's **approved, versioned** consent copy — not whatever a client sends. The client submits only the `locale` (a constrained `['en','hi']` enum, the `MedicalAckLocale`/`MemberTermsLocale`/`LifeEventsLocale` precedent) + which boxes; the server resolves the canonical copy for that locale (mirror `medicalDomain.ackTextForLocale(...)` — medical.handlers.ts:175) and writes THAT into `consent_payload.checkboxTextShown`. A tampered client therefore cannot persist text different from the approved copy. (Story 2.7's `ConsentPayloadSchema` is permissive `z.string()` at the primitive level — that is the primitive being consumer-agnostic; 6.9 as the consumer tightens it to server-resolved copy, exactly as medical did.) If a future need for a formal consent-copy *version id* on the row emerges, that is a small additive follow-up — record it as a consent-copy-integrity note, do not expand this story.

### The ALTER TYPE migration gotcha (D2)
`ALTER TYPE … ADD VALUE` **cannot run inside a transaction block** and a newly-added value **cannot be USED in the same transaction it was added** (0048 header). So the enum extension is its OWN migration (0058), separate from any code that inserts a row with the new value (none does at migration time — consent rows are written at RUNTIME by the 6.9 routes, well after the DDL commits). `IF NOT EXISTS` makes it re-apply-safe. This is exactly the 0040 (`whatsapp_opt_in`) / 0048 (`telegram_opt_in`) shape — copy 0048.

### The subject-key contract (D1) — the one decision that reaches across an epic
`consentExists(subject_id, type)` is queried by Epic 11b at Sahyog Vivran / In Memoriam **render time**. 11b has a `claim_case_id` (epics.md:2361) → the claim row → `deceasedMemberId`. So recording under `subject_id = deceased_member_id` is what makes 11b's gate resolvable. If a future implementer instead keys on the nominee/filer id or the `claim_case_id`, 11b's `consentExists(pariwarId, deceasedMemberId, …)` returns false forever and no consented memorial ever publishes. Write this convention into the ADR (Task 7) so Epic 11b reads the same key. `claims.deceasedMemberId` is on the claim row (`claims.nominee-bank.handlers.ts:169,325`).

### The no-disbursement-block invariant (D3) — a product + legal hard line
UX-DR2 / UX §12 Stance #1 is unambiguous: default-opt-in is unacceptable and a family that declines the public memorial must still get disbursed. Concretely: the three checkboxes render unchecked; declining (b) `sahyog_vivran_publication` and (c) `in_memoriam_listing` writes NO row for those types and the claim proceeds through `document` → verification → approval → disbursement with zero coupling. Do NOT gate any downstream claim transition on (b)/(c). (a) `claim_time_dpdpa` being required-to-proceed is the recommended reading (you cannot adjudicate a claim while forbidding processing of its PII) — but confirm against any Story 0.13 legal-counsel answer, which wins over this recommendation.

### State posture & the guard (AC5) — record early, revoke anytime
Consent **recording** happens inside the intake wizard (post-`relationship`, pre-`document`), so the claim is in an early pre-adjudication state. Mirror the 6.8 write-path guard: re-read the claim state inside the scope-tx and reject recording on a terminal/settled claim (a typed 409). But **revocation must work at any later state** — the AC3 takedown is by definition post-settlement. So the guard applies to record, NOT to revoke. Confirm the exact allow-set against `schema/claims.ts` — the literal `under_verification` does NOT exist (it's `verification_in_progress` etc.; the 6.8 D2 note). `projectClaimState` is the ONLY legal `current_state` writer (DB trigger + CI gate); the annotation event is `from_state === to_state`.

### Audit boundary (the 3.5 / 6.7 / 6.8 discipline)
Consent recording MUST be audit-or-throw (`audit.withCompensatingAudit`, ADR-0030): the intent line commits FIRST on `servicePool` (its own tx, survives a scope-tx rollback), the consent inserts + claim event run on the scope-tx, and a failure emits a compensating `*_rolled_back` line so the chain reconciles with `events_log` instead of over-counting. NO PII on any audit line — `claim_case_id` + granted-type flags + actor only. `emitAuthAudit` (the secondary NON-PII sink line) is fire-and-forget, last, after `ok = true`.
