# The questions, in short

1. **A follow-up text that gets no report at all** — does the follow-up stop (the family counts as reached) or carry on to the call or
   letter?
2. **The two reminder chases on one claim** — does a text that gets through on one chase end the other chase's follow-up, and does a
   certificate follow-up hold back a correction closure?
3. **A follow-up that would fall after the reminders end, or while they are paused** — is the text still sent?

Until you rule, the system follows **our reading** of each (below), and every one can be changed in one place once you answer.

---

# 1. A follow-up text that gets no report at all

**In one sentence:** when one of the two follow-up texts is accepted by the phone network but no report ever comes back saying whether it
was delivered, should the follow-up end with the family counted as reached, or carry on to its end and the call-or-letter request?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** two of your answers point opposite ways here. You ruled that a text with no report keeps
counting as reaching the person; and that a message to that person that *is* delivered ends the follow-up.

## The one fact that decides it

Reports stop only when something is wrong on our side or the provider's (the report service paused, an account fault) — and then they stop
for every family at once. So this question is really: when we have lost sight of delivery, do we lean toward a phone call and a letter,
or toward treating the family as reached?

| | Option | What changes for a family | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Carry on.** Only a delivered text or a call that reaches the family ends the follow-up. The silent text still counts as reaching them for closure. | A family whose follow-up text went unreported still gets the District Admin's call or letter after about eight days. | A family a silent text may in fact have reached can still be called or sent a letter. While reports are stopped, an alarm is raised — but it reaches the system's logs only, not a named person, until that alarm is connected to someone (a condition before this goes live). |
| **B** | **End as reached** once the follow-up text is two days old with no report. | A family whose follow-up went unreported is treated as reached and gets no call or letter. | A family whose phone stayed off is treated as reached on a text that nothing confirms got through. |

**Our reading:** **A** — because the follow-up exists for families who may not have been reached, and a call or a letter costs far less
than a claim decided for a family that never heard.

# 2. The two reminder chases on one claim

**In one sentence:** a claim can have the correction reminders and the certificate reminders running at the same time, to the same
person — should a text that gets through on one of them end the other one's follow-up, and should a certificate follow-up hold back a
correction closure?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** you ruled that *any* message to the person that is delivered ends the follow-up, and that a
claim waits while a follow-up runs for *any* of its people. Every question we put to you was about one chase at a time; this one was never
asked. Our reading treats each chase on its own, which is narrower than those words.

## The one fact that decides it

Until now the two chases have been kept apart on purpose: a certificate reminder has never counted toward deciding whether a family was
reached before a correction closure. A phone call is the one thing that already crosses: a call that reaches the person ends a follow-up
on either chase, because it reached the person, not a reminder.

| | Option | What changes for a family | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Each chase on its own.** A delivered certificate text does not end a correction follow-up; a certificate follow-up does not hold back a correction closure. (A call still counts for both.) | A family reached by a certificate text still has its correction follow-up run, and its correction closure may wait up to about eight days. | Some extra follow-up texts for a family already reached on the other chase; and a claim can be closed for silence on its corrections while a certificate follow-up for that person is still running. |
| **B** | **A delivered text on either chase ends both follow-ups;** only a correction follow-up holds back a correction closure. | A family reached on either chase is treated as reached on both. | A certificate text then decides whether a family was reached before a correction closure — something kept apart until now; and a claim can still be closed for silence on its corrections while a certificate follow-up for that person is running. |
| **C** | **Both ways:** as B, and a certificate follow-up also holds back a correction closure. | The fullest protection before a correction closure. | A correction closure can wait up to about eight days (more if a report arrives late) for a follow-up on the other chase. |

**Our reading:** **A** — because it keeps the two chases' rules as they were built, and its only cost is a few extra texts; but B or C
follow your words more closely, and either is a small change.

# 3. A follow-up that would fall after the reminders end, or while they are paused

**In one sentence:** if a reminder sent near the end is reported not delivered — a certificate reminder on day 180, or a correction reminder
near day 84 whose report arrives days late — or the certificate reminders are paused while a follow-up runs, should the follow-up texts
still go out?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** you ruled that the correction reminders stop at day 90 and the certificate reminders after 180
days and while they are paused — and that an undelivered reminder is sent again twice. Near the end, both cannot hold.

## The one fact that decides it

The follow-up's timer and its texts can be separated: even when no text is sent, the follow-up can still run its days and end, so the
closure still waits for it (the option you chose said *"at most about a week past day 90"*) and the call-or-letter request still goes out.

| | Option | What changes for a family | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The end wins for texts.** No follow-up text after day 90 / day 180 or during a pause; the follow-up still runs its days, ends, and asks for a call or a letter. | A family whose last reminder failed gets the call-or-letter request about a week later, with one or two fewer texts (mostly on the certificate chase; on the correction chase only when a report arrives days late). | One or two fewer tries by text before the call or letter. |
| **B** | **The follow-up texts go out anyway.** | The family gets its two follow-up texts even after the end or during a pause. | Texts after *"stop at day 90"* / after 180 days / during a pause — against those rulings as written. |
| **C** | **A late follow-up ends at once** at the reminders' end. | The call-or-letter request goes out on the last day. | No second try at all for a reminder that failed on the last day. |

**Our reading:** **A** — because it keeps your stop dates for texts and still gets the family a call or a letter.

# Also given to you as context — decided by us, not asked

These follow from how we build your rulings. None is a question; if you see one differently, tell us and it becomes one.

- **WhatsApp is not sent yet.** The WhatsApp step is for the claimant only (you ruled on 2026-10-18 that no WhatsApp goes to a nominee)
  and needs the claimant's new filing agreement; it is a separate piece of work (row 6-37d). Until then the follow-up goes straight to the second text. Cost: nothing is sent
  between hour 4 and day 2.
- **The days.** The follow-up texts go at 10:00 on day 2 and day 4 after the failed reminder, and the follow-up ends four days later
  (about eight days in all). A report that arrives late moves the whole follow-up later — and the hold on closing the claim with it, which
  can then run past the *"about a week past day 90"* you were told.
- **A follow-up text the network refuses outright** still waits for the follow-up's end date (up to about four days) before the family is
  treated as not reached.
- **A failure reported more than 14 days after the reminder was sent** is not followed up, and that reminder keeps counting as reaching
  the family — reports normally arrive within minutes.
- **A staff request after day 90 or during a certificate pause** (the District Admin asked to call or post a letter) is treated as a
  record for staff, not a "reminder" — so it still happens after the reminders stop.
- **A family concluded "not reached" on a number stays offered the letter route** even if a later text gets through; that text still
  counts as reaching them. Cost: a letter may be posted to a family a later text reached.
- **A nominee is asked for a letter, not a call,** until counsel confirms in writing the basis for phoning a nominee; a nominee who has
  already had their one certificate letter can then not be reached at all.
