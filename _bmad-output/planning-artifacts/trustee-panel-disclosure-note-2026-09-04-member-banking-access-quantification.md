# Appendix A — In plain words

## What you decided

In September you were told about a problem: a **stranger** with a link could see a family's bank
account details on the public website, while a **member of the trust** could not.

You fixed it, and you fixed it the careful way — you took the account details **off the public
website** rather than showing more to more people. ⭐ That was the right instinct and it is being built
first.

In the same decision you also said that **a logged-in member sees the complete banking information.**

## What we did not tell you

We told you about the unfairness. ⛔ **We did not tell you how many people that second sentence gives
the bank details to.**

Applied as written, it means:

> **Every member of a Pariwar can look up the full bank account number of every family that Pariwar
> has ever helped.**

Not just the drives they were asked to pay into. Not just while a drive is running. **All of them,
from now on.**

The trust is being built for **four lakh members**. Each one would see their own Pariwar's families —
so the number who can see any one family's account is the size of that Pariwar. ⚠ We do not have an
agreed figure for that, and we are not going to invent one; for a sense of scale, your own example
last week described **43,000 members** contributing to a single drive.

## The part that is hardest to undo

The public website could always be changed. A page comes down and it is down.

**A phone screen is different.** Once a member has opened a family's account details, that information
is on their device — and it can be photographed, saved and forwarded. Taking the feature away later
does not take back what has already been seen.

⚠ So on almost every measure, the access you granted to members is **wider** than the exposure you
removed from the public. The one thing it is not is **easier to stumble upon** — you have to be a
member, and you have to go looking.

## Why we are telling you rather than quietly doing less

When the engineers reached this decision, their recommendation was narrower: that a member sees the
account details only for the drives **they personally contributed to**.

**That recommendation was put to BigDev and BigDev chose the wider reading.** We have written down
that the narrower option was offered and declined — because the alternative, quietly building
something smaller than you decided and never mentioning it, would have been worse and you would never
have known.

## There is a real case for what you chose

These are the people whose money went to that family. A mutual-aid trust that hides from its own
members where the money went is a strange kind of transparency, and *"only the people who paid into
this particular drive"* is a narrower idea of membership than this trust otherwise uses.

⭐ We are not arguing against your decision. **We are making sure the price of it is visible, because
when you made it, it was not.**

## What happens now

⛔ **Nothing, unless you want something to.**

The work continues either way — the public exposure comes off first, on its own, because that part is
straightforwardly good regardless of anything in this note.

If, having seen the number, you would like the narrower version instead, say so and it is a small
change. If you are content, ⛔ no reply is needed and this note is simply part of the record.
