# Question 1 — may a nominee's phone number be used to keep them out of the five?

**In one sentence:** may the Trust compare a dead member's nominees' phone numbers with members' phone numbers, only to make sure a
nominee who is also a member is never one of the five asked about the death?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it uses a nominee's personal data for a purpose nobody has ruled on, and the basis for
handling someone's data is yours.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ The ground you stated for using a nominee's number covers **contacting** them (*"to reach out … to … prevent fraud"*). This is not
contact: the number is compared inside the system, and nobody is sent anything because of it. We read your ground as covering this
too, but it is a new use, so we ask rather than assume.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes — use the nominee's number for this check.** | A nominee who is also a member, and signs in with the number the member gave for them, is never asked about the death. The comparison keeps no number and sends nothing. If someone already chosen later turns out to match, staff checking the claim see that they are a nominee. | One more use of the nominee's number. Our lawyer has not yet confirmed the legal ground you quoted; nothing goes live until that is done. |
| **B** | **No.** | Nominees are not checked; a nominee who is a colleague can be one of the five. | A person who would receive the money can be asked whether the member has died, and their answer is weighed with the others. |

**Our reading:** A — **because** the check exists to keep the people the Trust asks independent of the payout, which is the same
fraud-prevention purpose you named, and nothing is kept, sent or shown beyond one staff label.

# Question 2 — should staff be warned when the nominees changed shortly before the claim?

**In one sentence:** may we add "the nominees changed in the 90 days before the claim" to the warning staff already see when the
member's workplace or the people they named changed?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** that warning is your ruling; adding to it extends what you ratified.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ Once nominees are left out of the five, changing the nominees before the claim becomes **one more way to change who is asked** — up to
two colleagues, one per nominee. Nominees can be changed until the claim is filed. You accepted that a family can still change who is
asked, with staff warned and the ground inspection always run — but today's warning does not look at nominees.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes — warn staff about nominee changes too.** | When a nominee was declared or changed in the 90 days before the claim, staff checking the claim see the same single warning, now naming nominees. | One more reason for the warning to show — including when someone re-saved the nominees without changing them, or declared them at sign-up within those 90 days. |
| **B** | **No warning for nominee changes.** | Staff are not told when a recent nominee change kept a colleague out of the five. | Staff miss this kind of change; the ground inspection still always runs. |
| **C** | **Do not leave nominees out at all.** | Nominees can be among the five, as today. | A person who would receive the money can be asked about the death (as in Question 1, option B). |

**Our reading:** A — **because** it keeps your accepted cost exactly as you accepted it: a family can still change who is asked, and
staff are told. If the change was dated on or after the day of death, the District Admin already treats it as suspicious and refuses
the claim (your September ruling); the warning covers the changes made before the death.

# In plain English

When a member dies, the Trust asks up to five of their colleagues what they know. The member's nominees receive the money, so if one of
them is also a colleague, their answer is not independent. BigDev has decided they should not be asked. To find them, the system
compares the nominees' phone numbers with members' phone numbers — that is Question 1, because it is a new use of the nominee's number.
And because changing the nominees before the claim would now change who is asked, we suggest the staff warning you already approved
should also mention nominee changes — that is Question 2. We suggest yes to both. Neither holds up the work; both must be answered before
this goes live.
