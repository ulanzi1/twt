# PQ1 — May the Trust ask a family for a list of the relatives the member leaves behind, and where may it appear?

**In one sentence:** when a family writes its memorial, should the app also offer a short list — *wife: Sunita Devi; sons: Amit,
Rahul* — the way a newspaper obituary names the family that mourns, and if so, who may see it?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** the list names **living people**, some of them **children**. Under your ruling on the
addendum, the person sending declares that the adults named have agreed and that they are the guardian of any child named (or have the
guardian's agreement) — but the Trust cannot check it. What the Trust shows about these people, and why it holds their names at all, is
yours.

### The one fact that decides it

The family can **already** name every one of these people in its own words: you ruled in October that living adults named there appear
as written, and today that children may be named and shown there too, on the guardian's declaration. ⇒ A list shows nothing of a **new
kind**. What is new is that the Trust would be **asking**
for names — a row marked *grandson* invites a child's name — and would **hold them as a list**, kept as long as the member's record.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** ⭐ | A list on the **public memorial page only** (⛔ not on the members' screen — as you ruled for the words): relation and name, from these relations of the member who died — spouse, children, parents, brothers and sisters, grandchildren (a son's and a daughter's children, named apart), and sons- and daughters-in-law. **Children may be listed on the guardian's declaration you ruled; every adult listed has agreed, on the same declaration.** The family may tick who on the list is in the photo — for the Trust's review only, as you ruled. The list is used for **nothing else** | A family may show, as an obituary does, *पत्नी · सुनीता देवी, पुत्र · अमित, राहुल, पौत्र · आरव* | The Trust relies on a declaration it cannot check (the cost you accepted for the addendum): a name on a false declaration — a child whose guardian never agreed, an adult never asked — stays up until someone tells the Trust, and then the Trust can only take the **whole** memorial down until a way to remove one name exists (PQ2). Kept for as long as the member's record — for good, unless counsel finds otherwise or the family uses the erasure route promised in October (⛔ not built yet). Counsel has ⛔ not been asked, about the list or the declaration |
| **B** | A list of **relations only**, ⛔ no names (*"wife, two sons, one daughter"*), children included; used for nothing else | The family's shape is shown without anyone's name | Less than families expect of a शोक संदेश; stricter than the words and photo beside it, where you have just allowed names and faces on the declaration; in a small village people can still tell who is meant; kept for good, as A |
| **C** | Relation and name, on the same declaration as A — but shown **only to members** who are signed in, ⛔ not on the public page | Members see the list; the open page does ⛔ not | A new members-only place for the family's own material, which the family's permission would have to name (your October ruling that "members see more than the public" does ⛔ not reach the family's words — so nothing requires this, and nothing forbids it); more to build; members are thousands of people, ⛔ not a closed circle; kept for good, as A |
| **D** | ⛔ No list. The family names whom it wishes in its own words, on the declaration you ruled | Nothing changes beyond your addendum ruling | The design's family line is dropped and the third memorial piece is ⛔ never built; the "who is in the photo" ticks have nothing to point at |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it is the same people, the same declaration, the same review and the same page you have already
ruled on for the words and the photo, and the purpose — this page, and nothing else — written into the ruling itself.

# PQ2 — A relative named on the list asks the Trust to take their own name off

**In one sentence:** if, say, a son named on his father's memorial phones the helpline and asks for his own name to come off, what may
the Trust do?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it is what the Trust owes a named person who did ⛔ not send the memorial.

### The one fact that decides it

Today a request from anyone in the family other than the nominee is ⛔ not a take-down request — the helpline suggests the nominee calls.
You ruled in October that only the nominee who sent a memorial can have it taken down; if that nominee has died or been replaced, ⛔ no
one can; and the Trust can still withdraw its own approval on its four grounds. ⇒ Neither rule was written for a person asking about
**their own name only**. Your ruling on the addendum makes this matter more: this is the only way a person named on a false declaration
— or a child named by a guardian, once grown up — could have their own name taken off.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Only through the nominee: the relative asks the nominee, who sends a new version without them | Nothing — today's rule, applied to one name | A relative on bad terms with the nominee has ⛔ no route, and their name stays up; if the nominee has died or been replaced, ⛔ no one can act; when the Trust approved the memorial exactly as written, the new version means the memorial comes down first and stays off the page while it is reviewed. Counsel has ⛔ not been asked whether a named person has a legal right to have their own name removed — if so, A must change |
| **B** ⭐ | The relative may call the helpline, and the Trust removes **that one name** (⛔ not the memorial), leaving no trace on the page | A named adult — including a child named by a guardian, once grown up — can have their own name removed by phone | **This is a reason for leaving part of the family's memorial out beyond the four you set** — B makes an exception to that rule for a person's own name. The helpline cannot prove the caller is that person, so a family quarrel can play out through it. The removal holds only for the version shown: if the family later sends a new version naming them again, the reviewer must be told. A new helpline step to build (it would come in a later piece of work) |
| **C** | As B, but the Trust first calls the nominee and removes the name only if the nominee agrees | As B, if the nominee agrees | The nominee can keep a relative's name up against that relative's wish; the same exception to the four reasons as B; counsel ⛔ not asked, as A |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** a person's own name on an open page is theirs to withdraw — all the more when it went up on someone
else's declaration — and a list of one name per person makes it a small, exact change.

---

# PC1 (confirm) — the four reasons, and "no trace", apply to the list

We will leave out an entry only for one of the four reasons you set for the words and photo — *it attacks someone named · it accuses
someone of wrongdoing · it is against the law · it names a child the declaration does ⛔ not cover* (as you have just ruled) — and,
**if you choose PQ2 B or C**, at the named person's own request. We tell the family in the app which entry was left out and why, and show ⛔ no mark on the public page where an entry was left
out — as you ruled for the words.
*Cost:* a relative left out leaves no mark; the page shows a shorter family than the family sent.
**A** confirm · **B** something else.

# PC2 (confirm) — if you choose PQ1 A or B, the list stays off the members' screen, as the family's words do

You ruled that "members see more than the public" does ⛔ not reach the family's words. We read the same for the list, which the family's
permission names for the public page only.
*Cost:* a signed-in member sees less of the memorial than the open page shows — the reverse of your rule for the drive's own
information.
**A** confirm · **B** something else. (Under PQ1 C this confirm falls away.)

# In plain English

A newspaper obituary in India names the person, tells their story, and names the family that mourns them — *wife Sunita Devi, sons
Amit and Rahul*. Our memorial page already has the first two. The third is a short list the family could fill in. We have held it back
since October because it means the Trust asking for, and keeping, the names of living people who did not agree to it themselves, some of
whom may be children.

We suggest you allow it on the public memorial page only, used for nothing else (PQ1 A), on the same guardian's declaration you have
just ruled for the words and the photo — so a family can list its grandchildren, and the adults it lists have agreed. We also
suggest that a relative may phone and have their own name taken off, without the whole memorial coming down (PQ2 B) — even though that
adds a reason for leaving something out beyond the four you set. Finally, we ask you to confirm that the four reasons and the "no mark"
rule apply to the list (PC1), and that, like the family's words, it stays off the members' screen (PC2).

If this section and the evidence below ever disagree, the **evidence** is the record.
