## 2. What Went Well

### W-1 — The correct-course caught a constitutional defect that every gate was green through
Story 10.10 enforced suspension via `is_valid: false`. All CI gates green; two full adversarial review
passes over ~7,885 lines. But `is_valid` is **also** the sole pool-assignability predicate; pool
assignment is the only contribution path (fenced by 8.10); R7(A) restoration requires three consecutive
contributions. So **every suspension was a de-facto permanent ban** and Niyamavali §3.3 was
contradicted — while the codebase already honoured §3.3 for lock-in (`VALID_STATES` keeps a locked-in
member on the roster). Six of seven R7 clauses became unreachable.

The **response** was as important as the finding: 10.10 was **not reopened** (its audit / event /
notice / RTBF-scrub / RLS work stands — one derivation line was wrong, corrected by 10.17), rollback
was **rejected** rather than discarding ~7,885 reviewed lines, and the correct-course applied
**three verification corrections against live source that each made the fix cheaper**, plus one
sequencing change against the brief: disclosure (10.16) moved to position 1 as a **hard `[GATE]`** on
the roster unblock, because the moment 10.17 landed, suspended members would receive contribution
requests with no coverage and no explanation.

### W-2 — Governance-commits-precede-implementation became a *verifiable* mechanism
10.13, 10.14, 10.18, 10.19, 10.20, 10.22, 10.29 and 7.11 each ran: routing note → Panel ruling →
`.decision-log.md` entry (`governance:` prefix, **zero `packages/` and `apps/` files, verified by
`git show`**) → then the implementation commit. 10.29's ledger names both commits in order.
`[[feedback_governance_commits_precede_implementation]]` is no longer a convention held in memory — it
is legible from the commit history itself, which is what makes it survive.

Two supporting disciplines held throughout: the Panel's **standing direction** ("*if any AC cannot be
implemented exactly from these rulings, stop and surface the conflict rather than inventing a rule*")
was actually obeyed — 10.22 stopped mid-story when the Panel amended Q3D beyond the offered options
into a three-tier appeal ladder — and amendments were **recorded verbatim as ADDITIONS**, never read
back into the original option (`[[feedback_supersede_never_reinterpret]]`).

### W-3 — A ruled deferral shipped as a *complete* outcome
Story 10.14 routed six questions, **all six blocking**, with Q1 as question zero: *is FR-48 in v1 at
all?* Decision `2026-08-17-126` ruled **(b) defer to v2**. What makes this a win is the precision of
the record:
- **Zero `packages/` and `apps/` files** touched, on both commits, verified.
- Q2–Q6 recorded as **"NOT REACHED"** — explicitly distinct from *"not answered"*; the Panel did not
  decline to rule them, ruling Q1 made them moot. A v2 successor may not treat the entry as having
  settled any of them.
- Both `role_grants` re-examination triggers left **ARMED**, recorded as **"Not addressed"** — not
  discharged, not deferred. `rbac/index.ts:273-283`'s comment left byte-unchanged *because it is
  currently true*; rewriting it would be the stale-reason defect in reverse.
- A new `deferred-to-v2` enum minted **and documented in the STATUS DEFINITIONS block**, with an
  explicit narrowing note that it does **not** discharge Story 0.12's open W-01.
- **No gate run and none owed** — zero source files changed, so `ci:local` would have been vacuous
  over the diff. Recorded as such rather than run and reported as meaningful.

### W-4 — The review layer found what tests structurally cannot, at scale
Beyond the raw counts, the defects worth naming are the ones no DB-free suite could have seen:
- **10.22 / migration `0107`** omitted `decided_at` from its column-level UPDATE grants. The decide path
  sets all six decision columns in one statement, so Postgres refused the whole statement with `42501`
  — **every appeal determination, every time**. §8.8's appeal could be filed and never decided.
  *A column GRANT has no representation in TypeScript.* Caught by the live-DB spec; fixed by **forward**
  migration `0108`, never by re-authoring an applied one. **That is the argument for the live spec
  existing.**
- **10.22** — the mobile in-portal appeal screen never sent `x-turnstile-token`, which
  `requireTurnstile` unconditionally requires. Every real member submission would have 400'd.
- **10.15** — `publish()`'s optimistic-concurrency guard (`eq(surveys.updatedAt, survey.updatedAt)`)
  **failed every call**: `updated_at` is microsecond-precision in Postgres, a JS `Date` holds
  milliseconds, so the echoed value could never match.
- **10.8** — an **inverted fail-safe**: `kyc_manual_fallback.fallbackDefault: true` traced through to
  the manual-fallback CTA being *hidden*, making KYC hard-mandatory on an unevaluable cohort rule —
  while the registry comment, the seam header **and the trustee-attested YAML rationale all asserted
  the opposite**. The attestation itself was false.
- **10.8** — a **rollback deadlock**: a future-dated version made every later flip throw the
  effective-from order guard, *including the audited `rolled_back` flip that was the entire shipped
  rollback mechanism*, and the pending row could be neither amended (append-only trigger) nor deleted
  (no DELETE grant). Scheduling was dropped, recorded as a deliberate narrowing.
- **10.9** — the admin visibility-verdict's competitor filter checked `display_mode` only, never
  audience targetability, so an invisible-to-everyone banner could display as "Current winner" and hide
  a real draft.

### W-5 — Self-correction was recorded, not laundered
Three separate times inside Story 10.8 the review pass's **headline finding was a regression from its
own earlier patch**. Pass 3 retracted Pass 2's "29/29 green" claim outright — that run had
`DATABASE_URL` **unset**, which *skips* integration tests, while the red job was diagnosed by running
the domain package only. Retracted in a Completion Note rather than quietly amended, and named as
*"second time in this story a green-gate claim outran its evidence"*
(`[[feedback_verify_before_committing_governance_claims]]`).

Pass 5 produced the generalizable rule: **a claim appearing in N places must be changed in N places,
and the sweep is PART OF the patch — for prose as much as for code.** It was earned by finding one
gate described four ways across four files, disagreeing with itself.

The same honesty ran through the epic: 10.13 recorded `ci:local` **as observed (29 green / 2 red)** and
noted that a truncated log meant two failing spec names *were never observed and are not reconstructed*;
10.22 ran the full domain suite **alone with its own spec included** (244/244 files) precisely because
"pre-existing flake" and "my load tipped it" look identical from a summary — and wrote the narrower
claim rather than the word *green*.

### W-6 — Revert-sanity became the default evidence form
Nearly every gate and invariant this epic shipped carried a probe that was **run red and restored**:
- **10.18** probed three gates in both directions and found the first version of the domain↔contracts
  ceiling parity assertion **one-directional** — catching the inert-role direction while silently
  passing the privilege-escalation one.
- **6.17**'s D6 fallback probe demonstrated the shape that matters: inserting the forbidden
  "if no tree, gate at district" rule made the test go red with `expected 201 to be 403` — **the

- **7.11**'s AC10 required *writing the distinguishing cases*, because every pre-existing case was

- **1.19** ran three probes with **differing failure sets** (9 / 3 / 4 of 126) — and produced the keeper:
  the `districtsBeneathState ↔ lift` **round-trip test stayed GREEN under a corrupted tree edge**. A
  consistently-corrupted edge is self-consistent in both directions; only the **explicit expected-set

## 3. What Was Hard / What Leaked

### H-1 (headline) — the danger clustered where a SINGLE PREDICATE carried a constitutional meaning → **AI-10-1**
`is_valid` (10.10) is the canonical case, but it repeated inside the correction itself: **10.23**'s
`shouldImpose` re-imposition bar matched on `episode_key`, which folds in `skipsCurrentYear` —
IST-calendar-year scoped, so it moves at the year boundary **and on every further missed cycle,
regardless of member action**. For R7(D)/(E)/(F) members (Escalation 6: no catch-up channel exists) a
further skip is **mechanical, not a choice** — the roster keeps assigning them and they remain unable
to pay. Matching on it reproduced, through skip-count drift, the exact *"de-facto permanent,
machine-imposed coverage removal"* that Decision `2026-08-07-088` clause 3 had been ratified to bar.

The lesson is structural: **DB-free tests and CI gates verify a predicate's mechanics; the harm lives
in its meaning.** Both instances were caught — one by a decision brief, one by review — and neither by
any gate, because no gate can be written for it. → **AI-10-1**.

### H-2 — AI-9-2 was never run, and the per-story tax carried it
Verified: **no `epics.md` commit exists between the Epic-9 retro (`949ac56`) and Story 10.1.** Instead,
10.1's Dev Notes carry an *"Epics-prose drift (AI-9-2 class)"* note, and every subsequent story paid
the tax individually: `apps/member/modules/helpdesk/` does not exist (10.1) · the channels list named
`email` and omitted `telegram` (10.5) · a sibling-file basename misattribution on an AC's own line pin
(10.28) · three citation drifts recorded as routing-note finding F-9 rather than silently corrected
(10.14) · two drifted anchors in minted AC text (1.19) · a `Clause 27(b)` mis-citation repeated 6× across
4 files when the actual provision is `22(b)` (10.22).

It **worked** — nothing shipped wrong, because each story documented the drift rather than coding to
prose. But the *commitment itself* decayed, which is precisely the pattern
`[[feedback_mechanization_split_commitment]]` names, now applied to the retrospective's own action
items. → **AI-10-2**.

### H-3 — AI-9-1 was practised but never extracted, and the exact class recurred → **AI-10-3**

## 4. Key Insights / Lessons

### I-1 — A predicate can be constitutional law
`is_valid: false` is one boolean conjunction. It was also, in effect, a permanent ban contradicting a
ratified instrument. No gate can see that, because a gate verifies the mechanism and the harm lives in
the **meaning**. The countermeasure Epic 10 actually used — and is now institutionalizing — is to write
down, in one sentence, what a member-gating predicate *means to the member*, and check that sentence
against the Niyamavali.

### I-2 — Governance-first works because it is *verifiable*
"Zero `packages/` and `apps/` files, verified by `git show`" is what made the commit-order discipline
real. A convention that can be checked from the history is a convention that survives. The same shape
recurs across the epic: an unrecorded zero discharges nothing (1.19's pre-flight query), a green scan
over an unlisted root proves the root is unlisted, and a gate that has never failed proves nothing.

### I-3 — A count is not a register
Nine successive restatements of "the queue is N" produced an obligation that had never been counted.
The fix — adopt the file as the register, so the eleventh obligation is added to a *list* rather than a
*sentence* — is the same shape as every other Epic-10 lesson. `[[feedback_mechanization_split_commitment]]`
applies to bookkeeping as much as to code, and (H-2) to retrospective action items as much as to either.

### I-4 — After ten epics, the review layer's most valuable findings were about its own prior passes
Three times in 10.8 the headline was a self-inflicted regression; twice a "green" claim outran its
evidence and was retracted rather than amended. The rule that fell out — *a claim appearing in N places
must be changed in N places, and the sweep is part of the patch* — is the cheapest available defence
against the drift class this project spends most of its governance budget fighting.

### I-5 — "Not reached" ≠ "not answered" ≠ "not addressed" ≠ "deferred"
Story 10.14 used **all four** honestly, in one story, and that precision is exactly what let a story
ship **zero lines of code as a complete success**. `[[feedback_closure_language_precision]]` is now
load-bearing rather than stylistic: 10.29 marked its predecessor *"Closed by [implementation]"* and
explicitly **not** *"Resolved via deferral"*; 10.28 closed the `state` arm *"Closed by [edit]"* with **no
successor minted** and the durable reason written into the code; 1.19 recorded role/cohort as *"Not
addressed"* with a concrete re-trigger and minted nothing, because an owner minted for work nobody wants
is exactly the un-gated re-commitment that decays.

### I-6 — A permanent owner born already-discharged is a contradiction
Story 1.18 was forbidden from absorbing 10.28's multi-node report scope *even if it looked cheap after
the ancestry work*, because 10.28 is the **permanent owner** of a concern (cardinality) that is
orthogonal to 1.18's (ancestry). The AC was rewritten from *"revisit"* to *"disposition, never a build"*.
This is the cleanest statement yet of when **not** to fold work into an adjacent story.
