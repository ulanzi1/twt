# The question

**In one sentence:** You ruled that the inspector holds the original death certificate, photographs it and **records whether it matches**
the copy the family uploaded, and that the District Admin checks the certificate on the government's online death register and **records
that they did** — so when either one says **"does not match"**, what happens to the claim (Q1)? And when the family **replaces** the
certificate after the inspector's visit, must an inspector also see the **new** original (Q2)?

**Why they cannot be decided without you:** both decide what stands between a forged certificate and the members' money. You ruled the
checks; what a failed check means — and whether a second certificate must be seen — are rules about paying a family, ⛔ not about building
software.

## The one fact that decides each

- **Q1 — as ruled, a "does not match" changes nothing.** The District Admin could record *"the register does not match"* and approve the
  claim the next minute with ⛔ no reason written — while a family's date of death that is **one day** different from the certificate already
  requires a reason and a note (your FQ12). The strongest sign of a forged certificate would be the one sign that asks nothing of anyone.
- **Q2 — a replaced certificate is exactly where a forged clear date can come in.** A certificate can only be replaced **before** it is
  accepted — when the District Admin turned it back because the date was missing, unclear or in the future, or before anyone reviewed it.
  The inspector often visits **before** that review. So the honest, smudged original is what the inspector held, and the clear-dated
  replacement the District Admin then accepts has been seen by ⛔ nobody.

# What you are choosing between

## Q1 — When the original does not match the copy, or the government register does not match, what happens?

*(You may answer the two checks differently — e.g. "A for the register, B for the inspector".)*

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Shown only** | The District Admin and every later approver see *"does not match"* on the screen. Nobody has to write anything to approve. | ⚠ The strongest sign of forgery asks less of an approver than a one-day date difference does. |
| **B** | **A warning, under your one rule** *(our suggestion)* | Approving while a "does not match" shows needs a chosen reason and a written note — from the District Admin and from every later approver, as for the other warnings. If it appears after the District Admin approved, the final approval waits for the District Admin's reason (your `-277` Q3 B). The claim is ⛔ never refused for it. | ⚠ One more reason to give where the mismatch is innocent (a register not yet updated, a copy that was re-scanned); one more short wait if it appears late. |
| **C** | **The claim cannot be approved while it shows** | The District Admin must turn the certificate back and the family must bring another one that matches. The claim waits until then; it is ⛔ never refused for it. | ⚠ If the register itself is wrong or incomplete, a genuine family can **never** clear it — the claim waits with ⛔ no end. It also needs a new reason for turning a certificate back (today the only reasons are about the date). |

**Our reading: B** — **because** it treats a failed check exactly as you treated every other sign of a false date in FQ12: the approver must
stop and write down why, but a genuine family whose register entry is simply missing is ⛔ not stuck. ⭐ Under every option the District Admin
can still **refuse** a claim they believe is forged, as today.

## Q2 — The family replaces the certificate after the inspector's visit. Must an inspector see the new original?

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes — the same short check as FQ13** *(our suggestion)* | Before approval, an inspector sees, photographs and matches the **new** original — a short visit or an office check, limited to the certificate, as you ruled for the true nominee's refile. Until then the claim waits; it is ⛔ never refused for it. | ⚠ An extra check even when the family simply re-scanned the same paper more clearly; a few more days' wait for those families. |
| **B** | **No — the inspection already done stands** | The claim goes on with the inspection as it was. | ⚠ The certificate the District Admin accepted — the one whose date decides which nominee is paid — has been held by ⛔ nobody from the Trust. |

**Our reading: A** — **because** the date on the accepted certificate decides who is paid (the nominee in place on that date), and FQ11 was
ruled so that someone holds **that** certificate. A replacement is rare (it follows a turned-back certificate), so the cost falls on few
families.

# In plain English

Your rulings added two checks against a forged death certificate: the inspector holds the real certificate and says whether it matches the
copy, and the District Admin looks it up in the government register. But you ruled only that the answers are **written down** — ⛔ not what
happens when the answer is "no". As things stand, a "no" would change nothing at all, which makes the strongest sign of forgery weaker than
a one-day date difference. We suggest treating a "no" like your other warnings: anyone approving must pick a reason and write a note, and
the family is never refused just for it — so an honest family whose register entry is missing is not stuck, but nobody can wave a mismatch
through silently.

Second: when a family's first certificate is turned back (say the date was smudged) and they bring a new one, the inspector who already
visited never saw the new one — yet its date is what decides which nominee is paid. We suggest an inspector also sees the new original, with
the same short check you already ordered for the true nominee's refile.
