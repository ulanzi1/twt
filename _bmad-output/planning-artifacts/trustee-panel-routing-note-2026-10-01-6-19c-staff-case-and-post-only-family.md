# The question

**In one sentence:** Five small points about claims that reach the Super Admin, and about a family we could reach only by letter. They
cover whether such a claim can ever be closed "for no response", who may decide it while it waits, when the family's 90 days start, whether
the Super Admin's power to pay "despite the name problem" survives a change of who must act, and whether a family is told their claim was
closed when we have no working phone for them.

**Why they cannot be decided without you:** each changes how a family's claim can end, who ends it, or what the family hears. Your earlier
answers did not reach these cases.

## The one fact that decides them

⭐ **The Super Admin can send a staff case back to the family.** You let the Super Admin direct the admins (`-256`, widened to staff cases
by `-258`). One direction they can give is *"the family must act after all"*. That single move puts a claim where your rule *"a staff case
is ⛔ never closed for no response"* and your rule *"a claim can be closed only while the family must act"* both seem to apply. Confirms
1a–1d all follow from it. Confirm 2 rests on a second fact: **a family reached only by post has no working phone**, so the closure notice
(a text message) cannot reach them.

the Super Admin. The Super Admin directs that **the family must act after all** and restarts the family's reminders. The family then says

# In plain English

Sometimes a claim is sent back because **staff** made a mistake, not the family. If staff never fix it, after 90 days it goes to the Super
Admin, who can look into it, tell the admins what to do, and decide it. One thing the Super Admin can say is *"actually, the family needs to
give new details"*. That raises some questions you have not been asked before.

First, if the family then says nothing, can the claim be closed for no response? We say **no**: it began as staff's mistake, and you said
such a claim is never closed for no response. The cost is that if the family really has gone quiet, the Super Admin can end it only by
refusing it for some other reason, and the family can appeal that refusal. Second, does the family's 90-day clock start the moment the admins
switch it to "the family must act"? We say **only when the Super Admin restarts the reminders**, because the Super Admin is in charge of the
claim by then. Third, if the claim reached the Super Admin because the Pariwar Admin refused to close it, may the Super Admin still pay
it despite the name problem after it is switched to "staff must fix it"? We say **yes**, but a careful **no** would also be reasonable.
Fourth, while the Super Admin holds a claim, can the Pariwar Admin still decide it? We say **no**: one person decides at a time.

Last: when a claim is closed for no response, we send the family a short text. But for some people our only working route was a posted
letter, because their phone number is dead. They would never hear that their claim was closed. **We suggest a posted letter** telling them,
and how to file again; until you say so, they receive nothing.
