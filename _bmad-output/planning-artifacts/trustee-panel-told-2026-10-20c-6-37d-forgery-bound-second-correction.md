## Four things our earlier notes today did not say correctly

**1. "A person in both at once could get one WhatsApp for each" — it is up to two of each.** The limit of two copies applies to each
follow-up. A person can have two follow-ups at once on one claim — one for the bank-details correction and one for the death
certificate — so they could get up to two copies of each reminder, four in all. The limit is also per claim: someone named on two
live claims could get more.

**2. Retrying does not fully protect the one WhatsApp reminder.** We said we retry so that a short Meta outage would not use up the
claimant's only WhatsApp reminder. We retry once, about a minute later. If Meta is still failing then, the reminder is used up and no
WhatsApp is sent for that follow-up. The text messages, the phone call and the letter still follow as before.

**3. "It cannot go where the claimant has replied STOP" — almost always.** If our system fails at the moment it records a STOP (for
example, a brief outage of the service that protects phone numbers), that STOP is not kept, and WhatsApp could still go to that number
until someone records it by hand. Since today such a failure raises an alarm for staff. It is rare, but it is possible.

**4. "About four hours later" — except when the "not delivered" report itself arrives very late.** If the phone company's report that
a text was not delivered reaches us more than eight hours late, the WhatsApp waits for the next morning's 10:00 run instead.

**What we did.** Nothing about who is contacted, or why, has changed. The records (Decision `2026-10-20-372`, ADR-0042) now say exactly
this.
