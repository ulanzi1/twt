# Question 1 — send the member's full name; shorten only a very long one

**In one sentence:** instead of "Ramesh S." whenever the name does not fit, we would send the member's FULL name in every text — up to
60 letters — and only for a longer name drop titles and then turn the first words into initials, never touching the last word; do you
agree?

**Why it is yours:** it replaces the shorter form you chose on 2026-10-18.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ The SMS rules allow one name box ("variable") of at most 30 letters (one rule says 40; we plan for 30). The shorter form you
chose — first word plus the first letter of the last word — turned out to say very little for some names:

| The full name | What option B sends |
|---|---|
| K. Venkata Satya Narayana Murthy Subrahmanyam | **K. S.** |
| Shri Rajendra Prasad Narayan Singh Srivastava | **Shri S.** |

⭐ A text may carry TWO name boxes side by side, which together hold **60 letters** — enough for almost every real name in full.

## The rule we propose

1. **Send the full name** whenever it fits 60 letters.
2. **Only if it is longer:** remove titles and suffixes — **Shri / Sri, Smt, Shrimati, Sushri, Dr, Late, Mr / Mrs / Ms, Ji, श्री,
   श्रीमती, सुश्री, डॉ, स्व., स्वर्गीय, जी** (whole words only; Kumari, Devi, Kumar and Bai are parts of names and stay).
3. **If still longer:** turn words into their first letter, starting from the FIRST word, one word at a time, until it fits — and
   ⛔ never the last word (the family name people recognise):

   | The full name (65 letters) | What is sent (55 letters) |
   |---|---|
   | Kumar Venkata Satya Narayana Murthy Subrahmanyam Swaminathan Iyer | **K V Satya Narayana Murthy Subrahmanyam Swaminathan Iyer** |

4. **If it still does not fit** (we know of ⛔ such name): ⛔ text, as today — the family's text raises an alarm.

⭐ **Names are in English.** The member's name comes from Aadhaar through DigiLocker, which gives the English name. Where a member
types it instead, the app will ask for it in English — your own ruling (`-227`: *"Please use English Name everywhere"*). The Hindi
texts carry the same English name.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The full name, shortened only above 60 as above** (our suggestion). | The family and the neighbours see the member's real name; only a very long name loses titles or becomes "K V Satya …". | Each text needs two name boxes — so four texts must drop the helpline number to stay within the SMS rules (Question 2). |
| **B** | **Keep what you ruled on 2026-10-18** — "Ramesh S." when the name does not fit 30 (or 40). | Long names arrive as "K. S." or "Shri S.". | A family or neighbour may not recognise the member at all. |
| **C** | **The full name only; ⛔ shortening.** | Names up to 60 letters in full; a longer one gets ⛔ text. | A very long name loses the text altogether — for the refused person, the only notice of the last date to appeal. |

**Our reading:** A — **because** it sends the name the family knows in nearly every case, and where it must shorten it keeps the family
name and as much of the rest as fits.

# Question 2 — the helpline number in four texts

**In one sentence:** to make room for the second name box, the appeal-date text would say just *"Please call the helpline."* and
the neighbours' text would end *"…, or call us."* — both without the number; do you agree?

**Why it is yours:** you ratified the neighbours' text with *"[link to the app] — or call the helpline [number]"* (`-303`).

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ The SMS rules allow **at most three** boxes in a text. The helpline number is a box, because each Pariwar has its own number.

| Text | Boxes today | With two name boxes |
|---|---|---|
| "Could not go ahead" and "has been closed" (to the family) | name, helpline | name ×2, helpline = 3 — **fits; the number stays** |
| "Can be appealed until [date]" (to the refused person) | name, date, helpline | name ×2, date, helpline = **4 — too many** |
| The text to the five neighbours | name, link, helpline | name ×2, link, helpline = **4 — too many** |

The two texts that would be over the limit become:

| Text | Today | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Appeal date | *"The claim for [member] could not go ahead. It can be appealed until [date]. Please call the helpline [number]."* | *"The claim for [member] could not go ahead. It can be appealed until [date]. Please call the helpline."* |
| Neighbours | *"We have been told that [member] has died. If you knew them, please answer a few short questions: [link] — or call the helpline [number]."* | *"We have been told that [member] has died. If you knew them, please answer a few short questions: [link], or call us."* |

(The Hindi texts change the same way; the Hindi reviewers check the words.) ⭐ For the appeal-date text this RETURNS to your own
words — *"Please call the helpline."*, with no number (`-293`); the number was added when we built it. ⭐ In the neighbours' text the
dash becomes a comma: a dash is not a standard SMS character and turns the whole English text into the costlier Unicode kind —
about 3–4 parts instead of 2.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Drop the number from these two texts** (our suggestion). | The refused person reads your own words, *"Please call the helpline."*; the neighbours read *"or call us"*. | A refused person who does not use the app has no number in the text. The neighbours are members who use the app and know the helpline; those who chose WhatsApp also get the number in the WhatsApp reminder two days later. |
| **B** | **Keep the number; these two texts keep ONE name box** (30 letters, the same rule shortened to fit). | The number stays. | In these two texts a long name is shortened much harder — e.g. "K V S N M S Swaminathan Iyer" — and the four texts name the member differently. |

**Our reading:** A — **because** the name is what tells a person what the text is about; the appeal-date text keeps your own words,
and the neighbours are members who already know the helpline.
