# The question

**In one sentence:** When a neighbour answers *"Yes"* to *"May the District Admin phone you about this?"*, who may see that neighbour's
phone number? Today the District Admin's screen shows it — to the District Admin, to the district's verifiers who work that same
screen, and to the Super Admin, who can open every screen. Should the Pariwar Admin, who approves the claim later, see it too (Q1)?
And is today's set right (Q2)?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** you ratified the question's words, and they name ONE person. Every other person who sees
the number goes beyond those words. Today's set was our own call when the screen was built, and it was ⛔ never put to you. The only
basis offered is our reading of the member's signup consent, which counsel has ⛔ not confirmed.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **The neighbour was asked about the District Admin, by name, and nobody else.** You agreed that later approvers see *"every answer
for the claim"* — so they see that the neighbour said *"Yes"* — but a phone number is ⛔ not an answer.

**Our reading:** **Q1 A and Q2 A** — **because** the people on the District Admin's screen act for the District Admin's office in
checking the claim, which is what the neighbour agreed to help with, while the Pariwar Admin approves later and can ask that office to
call. If later approvers really need to phone neighbours, **Q1 C** gets the neighbour's agreement honestly, where **Q1 B** relies on a
reading of the signup terms nobody has checked. ⚠ Our Q2 A reading is the weaker of the two: the Super Admin is ⛔ not the District
Admin's office, and **Q2 B** is a reasonable answer.

# In plain English

When someone dies, the Trust texts a few of their colleagues — or, failing that, people the member named — and asks a few questions.
One question asks whether the District Admin may phone them. If they say yes, the District Admin's screen shows their number, and so
far that screen is also used by the district's verifiers and can be opened by the Super Admin. Later, the Pariwar Admin must also
approve the family's claim, and will now see the neighbours' answers so they can judge them.

We suggest the Pariwar Admin should ⛔ not also see the phone number: the neighbour agreed to be phoned by the District Admin, not by
everyone. If the Pariwar Admin needs to talk to a neighbour, they can ask the District Admin's office — or you can change the question's
words so future neighbours agree to it knowingly. We also ask you to confirm, or narrow, who already sees it on the District Admin's
screen. If this summary and the evidence below ever disagree, the evidence is the record.
