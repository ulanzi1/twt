# Question 1 — the WhatsApp reminder: about four hours after a failed text, or at the next 10:00?

**In one sentence:** when a reminder text to the person who made the claim is reported **not delivered**, should the WhatsApp copy go
**about four hours after the text was sent, in the daytime**, or wait for **the next morning at 10:00**?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** you ruled both halves. For the bank-details reminders you confirmed *"one message a day, at
10:00 in the morning"*. Later you ruled that the Trust sends the message on WhatsApp *"when the SMS has not been delivered 4 hours after
it was sent"*. A WhatsApp four hours after a 10:00 text lands at about 14:00 — a second message that day. Which rule gives way is
yours.

## The one fact that decides it

**The 10:00 text did not arrive.** The phone network has reported it undelivered. So the 14:00 WhatsApp is the **first** reminder the
family actually receives that day, not a second one. The "one message a day" rule protects a family from being messaged twice;
here they have been reached once at most.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **About four hours after the failed text, between 9 am and 8 pm** (a later failure waits for 9 am). | The family gets the reminder on WhatsApp the same afternoon, while the text is still undelivered. | Two messages are SENT that day (one never arrived). A WhatsApp that is delivered also ends the follow-up texts, so the claim may be closed for silence sooner. |
| **B** | **At the next 10:00 run.** | The WhatsApp goes about a day after the failed text, with the next day's messages. | About a day later. In the first week, when a reminder already goes every day, the WhatsApp arrives beside that day's text. |
| **C** | **About four hours later, but only when no other reminder is due the next morning.** | Same afternoon on some days, next morning on others. | A rule a family cannot predict, and staff cannot easily explain. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** your later ruling names the four hours specifically for a text that was not delivered, and the
family has not been reached at all that day. While you decide, the system is built to A; nothing is sent to anyone until the
claimant's WhatsApp goes live, and that does not happen before your ruling.

# Question 2 — remove "or any other means of communication that may appear in future" from the claimant's agreement?

**In one sentence:** the claimant's filing agreement is to name text message, WhatsApp, phone call and post; you also ratified the
words *"or any other means of communication that may appear in future"* — may we **remove** them, so the agreement names only those
four?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** those words are part of your ratified ruling, and you accepted their stated cost when you
ruled (*"an agreement to channels not yet named may not count as explicit consent"*). Our earlier proposal allowed them to be removed
without a new ruling only **if counsel advised it**; counsel has not been asked. So removing them now needs you.

## The one fact that decides it

**India's data-protection law asks for consent that is "specific".** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act requires consent to be
*"free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous"* (in force from 2027 — the date is our calculation from the 2025 Rules). A
person cannot know which future means they are agreeing to, so those words weaken the whole agreement rather than add to it.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Remove the words.** The agreement names text message, WhatsApp, phone call and post only. | The claimant knows exactly how the Trust may contact them. | If the Trust later wants a new way to reach claimants (for example an automated call), claims filed before then must be asked again before that way is used for them. |
| **B** | **Keep the words, as ratified.** | No change from your ruling. | The agreement is less specific; counsel may still advise removing them later, and a claimant contacted by a "future means" may not have truly agreed to it — the cost you accepted. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** an agreement a person can understand in full is stronger than one that reaches further, and the
Trust can always ask again when a new way is needed. While you decide, the new agreement is prepared without the words; it does not
go live before your ruling, and if you keep them they are put back.

# In plain English

When a reminder text to the person who made a claim does not get through, the Trust plans to send the same reminder on WhatsApp — only
to that person, only because they agreed to it when they filed, and never at night. We ask two things. First: may that WhatsApp go
the same afternoon (about four hours after the failed text), even though you also said "one message a day, at 10:00"? We suggest yes,
because the morning text never arrived. Second: may we take the words "or any other means of communication that may appear in
future" out of the agreement, so it names only text, WhatsApp, phone call and post? We suggest yes, because a person should know
exactly what they are agreeing to. And for your information: the agreement's sentence now says "contact me" rather than "contact the
claimant named here". If this summary and the evidence below ever disagree, the evidence is the record.
