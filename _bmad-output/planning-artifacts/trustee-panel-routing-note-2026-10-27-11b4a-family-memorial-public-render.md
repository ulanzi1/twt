# T1 — For your information: only the nominee can have a memorial taken down

**What was decided.** On 2026-10-23, after you agreed that a family may ask the helpline to take a published memorial down, BigDev
decided that **only the nominee** — the person whose code sent the memorial — can make that request. Anyone else in the family who
calls is listened to and asked to have the nominee call.

**Why we are telling you.** When you chose the soft measures that discourage take-downs, *"requiring the nominee to make the
request"* was on the list and was ⛔ **not** chosen as one of them. That left open who may ask. BigDev then answered it: the nominee
gave the permission when the memorial was sent, so the nominee is the one who takes it back. BigDev's decision recommended showing it
to you with the next note. This is that note.

**What you need to do.** Nothing, unless you disagree. ⚠ If you would rather **any family member** could ask, say so: PQ1 then
falls away, and the build changes how the helpline checks the caller.

# PQ1 — When the nominee who sent it has died or been replaced

**In one sentence:** if the nominee who sent a memorial later dies, or the family records a different nominee, who can ask the
helpline to take the memorial down?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** under T1 only *that* nominee can ask. Built literally, a family whose nominee has died
or been replaced can ⛔ never have the memorial taken down. Whether the Trust owes them a way out is a question of what the Trust owes
a family.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **The family can already put a new memorial in place of the old one in one case, and the new nominee is the one who does it.**
Sending a memorial needs a code sent to the **current** nominee's phone. If the Trust left a part out of the memorial on the page,
the current nominee can send a new version, and once the Trust approves it, it replaces the old one. But if the Trust approved the
memorial **exactly as written**, no new version can be sent. Then the only way it comes down is a take-down — and under T1 the
current nominee cannot ask for one. So the gap is narrow but real: a memorial approved exactly as written, whose nominee is gone.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Only the nominee who sent it**, as built. If that nominee has died or been replaced, ⛔ no one can have it taken down. The Trust can still withdraw its own approval on its four grounds. | Clear and simple: one person, the one who gave the permission. | A family can be stuck with a memorial they now regret, with ⛔ no way to remove it. The permission they agreed to told them only the nominee could ask, so they were warned — but it may be years later, and the nominee may be the reason. |
| **B** | **The nominee who sent it, or — if that nominee has died or been replaced — the nominee recorded for the member now.** The helpline calls back the current nominee on the number recorded for them. *"Cannot be reached"* alone is ⛔ not enough: the original nominee stays the only one who can ask while they are alive and still the nominee. | The family always has one person who can ask — the same person who could already send a new version. | Two nominees could disagree; the build follows whoever is the nominee now. A family that changes its nominee in order to get the memorial down can do so. The permission text says *"Only the nominee can ask"*, which a family may have read as meaning the original nominee. |
| **C** | **Any adult family member** who calls, once the helpline has checked who they are. | Anyone close to the member can ask. | There is ⛔ no way today to check who a caller is beyond the nominee's recorded number; new checking would be needed. Different family members may want different things, and the helpline would have to judge between them. It also reverses T1. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** it closes the gap without widening who speaks for the family: the person who can ask is the
same person who can already send a new memorial, and the helpline can check them in the same way. ⚠ The build does **A** until you
answer.

# PQ2 — The family's words name the member; the page does not yet

**In one sentence:** the family's words will usually name the member who died, and the photo shows them — but the page itself
does ⛔ not show the member's name yet. Should the words and photo still appear exactly as the Trust approved them?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it is about what the Trust discloses about a person. The Trust has its own rule for when
the member's name may appear on this page, and the family's words would put it there anyway.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **You answered this situation's outline already, knowing the page names no one.** You ruled that the member's name *may* appear
on this page, but only once a written clause in the member's own terms allows it. That clause has ⛔ not been written, so today no
drive's page names the member. The note you answered on 2026-10-23 said so — *"today no drive's page does"* — and you chose that the
family's words and photo appear **as the Trust approved them**. What that note did ⛔ not ask in so many words is whether the family's
words may name **the member who died** while the page itself does not.

## What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **As approved** — the words and photo appear as the Trust approved them, including the member's name if the family wrote it. | The family's memorial reads as they wrote it. | Anyone with the page's link learns who died, from the family's words, before the Trust's own clause for showing the name exists. The member cannot be asked. |
| **B** | **The reviewer leaves the member's name out** of the words (and leaves the photo out) until the page may show the name. | The page stays consistent: nothing on it names the member. | It adds a fifth reason for leaving something out, beyond the four you ruled. Most memorials would lose their photo and be marked with gaps where the name was, for as long as the clause is unwritten — today, indefinitely. |
| **C** | **Hold the whole memorial back** until the page may show the member's name. | Nothing appears until then. | Today that means no memorial appears at all, with no date. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** you chose it on 2026-10-23 knowing the page named no one; the family is the one disclosing, on
its own permission, given when it sent the memorial; and B and C would each take away most of what the family wrote. ⚠ The build
does **A**.

## PC3 — When the approver has left, is anyone else told?

When a memorial is taken down, an email goes to **every person who approved a version of it that came down** (usually one person).
It says only that a memorial they approved was taken down at the nominee's request — ⛔ no names, ⛔ no words.

| | Option | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** *(built)* | If an approver has left the Trust, they are ⛔ not emailed and ⛔ no one else is told in their place. The take-down still happens and is recorded. | ⛔ No one learns of the take-down by email; it is visible only on the Trust's own screens. |
| **B** | Someone else is told instead — for example the Pariwar's administrator. | A person who did ⛔ not approve the memorial learns it existed and was taken down. You chose the approver because they had already seen it. |

**Our reading: A.**

## PC4 — Does the family's description of the photo appear with it?

When a family adds a photo, they also write a short description of it, which screen readers read aloud for people who cannot see
it. Your 2026-10-23 answer covered *"the words and photo"*; it did ⛔ not mention the description.

| | Option | Cost |

| **B** | The photo appears without the family's description. | People using screen readers hear only "photo", with nothing about who is in it. |

**Our reading: A.**

## PC5 — Does "a member must see more than the public" reach the family's words?

Your rule is that a member must see **more** than the public, never less. It was later settled that this binds the drive's own
information — the drive record and the nominee's bank details. The family's memorial appears on the public page, but ⛔ not in the
member app, because the family's permission names only the public page.

| | Option | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** *(built)* | The rule does ⛔ not reach the family's words: they are the family's writing, not the drive's information. | ⚠ On the screen, the memorial sits inside the drive's section of the page, so a reader could take it as part of the drive. |
| **B** | The rule does reach them. | The memorial would also have to appear in the member app — but the family gave permission for one place only, so the permission text would need to change, and families who already sent memorials agreed to the old text. |

**Our reading: A.**
