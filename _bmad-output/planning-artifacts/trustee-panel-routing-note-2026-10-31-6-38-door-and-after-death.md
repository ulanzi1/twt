# Q1 — "had not finished joining": where does it end?

You ruled that a claim is refused at the door for someone who, at their death, *"had not finished joining (no identity check)"*. Your
rulebook says a person becomes a member only once they have registered, had their identity verified, and paid the joining fee. Two kinds of
people sit between "only signed up" and "full member", and your words do not say which side they fall on. (Someone in both — typed-in
identity AND fee unpaid — is refused if either answer says refuse.)

**Untouched by this question, by your own words:** members in their first days after joining, behind on contributions, or suspended — the
existing rules decide them, as you said.

## Q1 (a) — an identity nobody has checked

**In one sentence:** should a claim be refused for someone whose identity was never checked — they typed their own details into the app
instead of verifying through DigiLocker, and nobody at the Trust has verified them since?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it decides whether these families are helped — and your words *"(no identity check)"* and
*"first days after joining … untouched"* pull in opposite directions for exactly these people.

### The one fact that decides it

**Today nothing ever checks a typed-in identity, and these members never leave their "first days after joining".** There are two ways to
give your identity when joining: through DigiLocker (the government's Aadhaar service sends the Trust the person's details), or — when
DigiLocker is switched off for a group, or is failing — by typing them in. The rules say a typed-in identity is to be verified by a trustee
when the first days after joining end. ⚠ **Neither of those two steps is built:** nothing ends the first days after joining, and there is
no screen on which anyone verifies a typed-in identity. So a member who typed their details in and paid stays, in the system, "in their
first days after joining" — which your ruling left untouched — for as long as they are a member, with an identity no one has checked.
(Members who used DigiLocker stay in that period too; their identity WAS checked.) When you ruled, the example in front of you was the
person who signed up with only a phone number and has no name on record at all.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A claim is filed as usual** (what is being built until you answer). The claim's normal checks then apply. | The family is helped like any other. | A drive may open in the name of someone whose identity nobody ever checked — today, every member who joined by typing their details in. |
| **B** | **Refused at the door** when the identity was never checked, like someone who only signed up. | The family is told the claim cannot be filed; no record, no appeal (your Q3 B). | A family is turned away because the Trust never built its own check — after the member did everything asked of them and paid. It also reaches members your ruling said were untouched. |
| **C** | **Filed, but the identity is checked first** — by staff, from the death documents and the details on record — before any drive opens (our suggestion). | The family is helped once staff confirm who the person was. | A new step for staff that does not exist today; it must be built before members' claims go live. We have ⛔ found an existing step that compares the dead person's details with the member record (the automatic certificate reader returns nothing yet). |

**Our reading:** **C** — **because** it keeps money from being raised in an unchecked name without turning a family away for a check the
Trust itself never made. Until it is built, A stands.

## Q1 (b) — identity details given, but the joining fee never paid

**In one sentence:** should a claim be refused for someone who gave their identity details but never paid the joining fee?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** your rulebook says such a person is not a member; your ruling did not mention the fee.

### The one fact that decides it

**Your rulebook makes the fee a condition of membership** — *"A person becomes a member only on all of: (a) registration; (b) identity
verification (KYC); (c) payment of the Vyawastha Shulk; and (d) admission under these rules."* Nobody in this group ever paid to join.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A claim is filed as usual** (what is being built until you answer). | The family is helped like a member's. | A drive may raise money in the name of someone who never paid to join — against your rulebook's own definition of a member. |
| **B** | **Refused at the door** (our suggestion), with the same treatment as the other refused cases. | The family is told the claim cannot be filed; no record, no appeal. | A family is turned away because the person had not yet paid when they died — even if they died days after giving their details. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** it matches your rulebook and the reason behind your ruling: no money is raised in the name of someone
who was not a member.

# Q2 — after a death is reported, what may the dead member's account still do?

Once a claim is filed, the dead member's account in the app is used by the person they nominated (after a code is sent to that person's
phone). You ruled that this person may **see** the record and **ask for corrections**, but not erase it. Two other things the account can
still do today were not asked.

## Q2 (a) — withdraw the dead member's membership

**In one sentence:** after a death is reported, may anyone still withdraw the dead member's membership from their account?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** your reason for Q1 (a) — *"no one can withdraw it on their behalf afterwards"* — points this way,
but your ruling covered erasure only; refusing a withdrawal would go further than you ruled.

### The one fact that decides it

**A single withdrawal after death takes away the "see" you granted.** Once a membership is withdrawn, nobody can sign in to that account
again, and both ways of getting a copy of the record — the download in the app and the copy staff arrange — refuse a withdrawn member. The
family keeps only the right to ask staff for a correction. (The claim itself appears to carry on — see "How much to trust this note".)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Withdrawal refused once a death is reported** (our suggestion), lifted on the same terms as erasure (the report closed because the member is alive). | The family keeps their access to see the record. | A living member wrongly reported dead cannot withdraw until the report is cleared — and the "cleared" process is not built yet, so for now they could not withdraw at all. |
| **B** | **As today — withdrawal allowed** (what is being built until you answer). | No change. | One action by whoever holds the account withdraws, on the dead member's behalf, the agreement you said no one can withdraw — and ends the family's access to see the record. |
| **C** | **Withdrawal allowed, but the family can still see the record** afterwards. | The family keeps a copy even after a withdrawal. | Still a withdrawal on the dead member's behalf; and it must be built. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it follows your own reason for Q1 (a), and it protects the right to see that you granted.

## Q2 (b) — change the record directly

**In one sentence:** after a death is reported, may the account still change the record directly — address, school posting, the medical
declaration — or only ask staff for a correction?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** your ruling said "ask for a correction"; the app today lets these be changed directly.

### The one fact that decides it

**Today the account can change these without anyone checking** (the medical declaration asks for a code; the address does not). Your words
— see the record, ask for a correction — describe a request that staff act on, not a direct change.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Only ask** (our suggestion): direct changes are refused once a death is reported; the family asks staff, as you ruled. | Every change goes through staff. | More work for staff and a slower fix for the family. |
| **B** | **As today — direct changes allowed** (what is being built until you answer). | The family can fix details themselves. | The record the claim rests on can be changed by whoever holds the account, with nobody reviewing it. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it is what your Q2 words say, and the record now underpins a claim and a drive.

# Q3 — may the family be told WHY a claim cannot be filed?

**In one sentence:** when a claim is refused at the door, may the helpline (and the app) tell the caller the reason — or only that it cannot
be filed?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** the reason is a fact about a person's membership history, told to whoever is calling.

### The one fact that decides it

**The caller already hears the member's name before filing** — the operator reads it out and asks the caller to confirm it. The reason
would add one more thing: that the person had left the Trust, had been removed from it, had their record erased at their request, or had
never finished joining. "Removed" is a sanction. The operator does see the reason on their screen (so they can re-check a mistyped number
before the call ends — the way we are reducing the cost you accepted in Q3 B).

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Only "a claim cannot be filed"** (what is being built until you answer). | The caller learns nothing about the person's membership. | The family does not learn why; they may call again and again, or believe it is a mistake they cannot fix. |
| **B** | **One general reason for every case:** "the person was not a member of the Trust when they died" (our suggestion). | The family understands the answer without learning the details. | Tells the caller that the person was not a member — a small disclosure, the same for every case. |
| **C** | **The specific reason** — left, removed, erased, or never finished joining. | The family learns exactly why. | Tells whoever is calling about the person's membership history, including a sanction. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** it gives the family an honest, final answer — the truth of your ruling, in your own words — without
disclosing which of the four it was.

# In plain English

Your ruling on 30 October is being built. Three edges need your word. **First**, between "only signed up" and "full member" are two groups:
people who typed in their own identity details, which nobody has ever checked (the Trust's checking step is not built), and people who gave
their details but never paid. We suggest that for the first, a claim is filed but staff check who the person was before any drive opens
(that check must be built); and that the second is refused (they never joined, by your own rulebook). **Second**, after a death
is reported, the dead member's account can still withdraw the membership — which would end the family's access to the record you said they
may see — and can still change details directly instead of asking. We suggest stopping both, as your own reasons point. **Third**, when a
claim is refused, we suggest telling the family one general, true reason — the person was not a member when they died — rather than nothing,
or the specific (and sometimes sensitive) reason. Until you answer, the build takes the narrowest path on each, and nothing is live.
