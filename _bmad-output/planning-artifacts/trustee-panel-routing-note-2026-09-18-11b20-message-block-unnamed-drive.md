# The question

**In one sentence:** when the public drive page does not show the name of the teacher who died, should
the closing message still appear, using the no-name wording you supplied (*"The family received
contributions of ₹X from colleagues."*), or should it not appear at all?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** you supplied the no-name wording for a family that has
**withheld** the name. But the page leaves the name out for other reasons as well, and today it leaves it
out for **every** drive, because the legal clause that permits publishing it has not arrived from
counsel. Using your wording in those cases would extend it beyond the case you wrote it for. That
decision is about what the Trust says in public about a family, so it is yours to make, not ours.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **The page can never tell why a name is missing, and this is deliberate.** Showing the reason would
reveal which members have or have not agreed to be named. So all the reasons end up in the same place:
the clause not arriving yet, a member not having agreed, a record that cannot be read, or a one-word
name the Pariwar's naming style cannot display. The page cannot tell them apart.

⇒ The choice is therefore **not** *"no-name wording now, full wording once counsel's clause arrives."*
If the no-name wording may only appear where a family chose it, then the message **cannot appear on any
drive without a name, ever**, even after counsel's clause arrives, because the page will still be unable
to tell which unnamed drives are unnamed by choice.

# What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Use the no-name wording **whenever** the name is not shown, whatever the reason | Every drive that has received money shows the full closing message now, with the no-name headline. Once counsel's clause arrives, drives that may be named get the named headline automatically. | Until the clause arrives, **no** family is thanked by name on the public page, including families who would have wanted it. A visitor who has read, on the drive list, that *"a family may choose whether their relative is named"* might conclude that this family chose not to be named. |
| **B** | Show the message **only** on drives that display a name | Nothing appears on any drive today. After counsel's clause arrives, the message appears only on named drives. | Some families will **never** see the closing message on their public page, including families who **did** agree but whose name the system cannot display (for example a one-word name). The public page would also behave differently from the member app and the drive list, which both already use the no-name wording. |
| **C** | Wait for counsel's clause, **then** do A | Nothing appears until the clause arrives. After that, the same as A. | The message stays hidden for an open-ended period. The legal item has been overdue since 2026-09-07 and has no date. Waiting only means the first public headlines will be named ones. It does not stop the no-name headline appearing later, because A still applies for every drive the clause does not cover. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** the no-name sentence is true of every drive it would appear on (the
family did receive the contributions), it says nothing about why the name is absent, and it is exactly
what the member app and the public drive list already do. The inference a visitor might draw about the
family's choice is a real cost. It comes from the drive list's existing consent sentence, though, not
from this message, and it is already present on that list today.
⚠ This reading is separable: rejecting it leaves the rest of the note usable.

# In plain English

When a teacher dies and colleagues contribute, each drive gets a public page. You approved a closing
message for that page: *"Late [name]'s family received contributions of ₹X from colleagues,"* followed by
four short paragraphs of thanks. You also approved a version without the name, for families who do not
want their relative named.

At the moment the page never shows the name, for any family. The legal clause that allows it has not
arrived yet. Even after it arrives, some names will still be missing for reasons that have nothing to do
with what the family wanted, and the page cannot tell those cases apart from a family's choice, by
design.

So the real choice is between showing your no-name message whenever there is no name, or showing the
message only where a name appears. The second means many families, today every family, get no closing
message at all. We suggest the first: the sentence is true, it takes no side on why the name is missing,
and it is what the member app already shows.
