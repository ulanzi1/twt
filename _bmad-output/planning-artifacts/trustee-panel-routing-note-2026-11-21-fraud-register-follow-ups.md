# The question

**In one sentence:** your two answers of 21 November settle the fraud register's shape, and eight edges they leave need your word —
two before anything more is built.

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** each edge changes whether a family is paid or waits, what a person is told, what the Trust
keeps, or who can be barred. Choosing any of them ourselves would decide it for you.

## The one fact that decides the urgent part

**A flag on a claim now stops approval until a finding — but a finding only exists for a member who is alive.** When the helpline
flags a claim and the member really did die, there is nobody to find innocent or guilty, so as answered the family's claim waits for
ever (F1). And the register's identity numbers wait for a lawful way to hold an Aadhaar-based number, which may never come (F2).

# Part 1 — before anything more is built

## F1 — A flagged claim where the member really died: how does it go on? (from your Q7 A, 21 November)

You chose that a flagged claim waits at both approvals until the District Admin records a finding. The note said the cost: a flag on a
death that **did** happen never gets a finding, *"unless you add a 'flag closed: the death is real' step, which you would need to
confirm is not a third outcome"*.

**The one fact:** "innocent" and "guilty" are findings about a **living** member. When the member is dead, neither fits — so without a
way to close the flag, the family waits for ever.

| | Option | What changes for a family | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The District Admin closes the flag: "the death is real"**, with a note, once the certificate is accepted and the ground inspection is done (our suggestion). The investigation ends **without** a finding — no one is found innocent or guilty. | The claim goes on to approval as normal. | One more staff step. A District Admin who closes a flag wrongly lets a false claim through (the approvals still see the flag's history). |
| **B** | **The flag lapses** after a fixed time (say 30 days) with no finding. | The claim waits up to 30 days, then goes on. | A false claim the District Admin has not reached in time goes on to approval. |
| **C** | **Only the Super Admin can release it.** | The claim waits until the Super Admin acts. | Every real family flagged by mistake depends on one person. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** the person who would record a finding is the one best placed to say there is nothing to find, and
ending without a finding adds no third outcome to *"innocent or guilty"*.

## F2 — Does the PAN half of the register wait for Aadhaar? (from your Q5 C, 21 November)

You chose to keep *"PAN and Aadhaar"* and wait for a lawful route for Aadhaar. The option's cost read: *"The register cannot be built
until one exists."*

**The one fact:** today the Trust has no lawful way we know of to hold an Aadhaar-based number (our understanding — counsel is asked to
confirm), so "wait" may mean **never**. Your 21 November answer on mobile numbers also said no bar until PAN **or** Aadhaar is
collected — so while both wait, a member found guilty can rejoin, and a person who faked a claim can file again.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Build the PAN half once counsel clears it; add Aadhaar if and when a lawful route exists** (our suggestion). | Families are asked for PAN (as you ruled); a guilty person can be barred by PAN. | Until Aadhaar comes, someone who gives no Aadhaar is caught only by PAN or by the person checking name matches. |
| **B** | **Wait for both** — build nothing of the identity numbers until Aadhaar can be held. | Nothing changes for families now. | The register may never be built; a person found to have faked a claim can file again, on any number. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it builds what the law allows now without giving up the Aadhaar half you chose to keep.

# Part 2 — before the parts they name are built

## F3 — The mobile-number flag: who is the "membership approver", and what do they see? (from your Q3, 21 November)

You said: no bar by mobile number, *"but flag the mobile number so that membership approver would know"*.

**The one fact:** **nobody approves a membership today** — a person joins by themselves (phone code, identity check, fee). The nearest
staff act is the District Admin's membership check when a claim is made. And a mobile number may since have been given to a stranger.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **When someone joins with a flagged number, the District Admin of their district is told** — information only; the joining goes ahead (our suggestion). | Nothing for the person joining. | The District Admin learns that the number once belonged to someone found guilty — even when the new holder is a stranger. A new notice to build. |
| **B** | **The flag is shown only at the membership check, when a claim is made** for that member. | Nothing until a claim. | The guilty person can join and contribute unnoticed until a claim. |
| **C** | **A new approval step at joining, for flagged numbers only.** | A person with a flagged number waits for staff. | It is the "held for a person to check" option you did not choose last time; a stranger with a recycled number waits. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it lets the person you meant know at the moment of joining, without stopping anyone. Please also
say what they are told: *"this mobile number belonged to a member found guilty of a false death claim"* is our suggested line.

## F4 — May a member found guilty appeal the finding? (asked with your Q1, 21 November — not answered)

**The one fact:** your September ruling makes the **ban** not appealable, and the rulebook makes a **termination** appealable to a Panel
member who did not take part. A guilty finding leads to both — but neither is built yet, so today a member wrongly found guilty stays
locked with no way to ask anyone.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **No separate appeal of the finding; the termination's appeal is the route** once the ban is built (our suggestion). | A wrongly found member appeals the termination, as the rulebook allows. | Until the ban and termination are built, a wrongly found member has no route at all. |
| **B** | **The finding itself is appealable within 90 days** to a Panel member who did not take part. | A wrongly found member can appeal at once. | A new appeal path to build. With two trustees, the one who imposes a ban (your Q5 A, 21 November) cannot hear the appeal, so every appeal falls to the other. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it keeps one appeal for one act, as the rulebook does.

## F5 — When a ban ends a membership in another Pariwar, is that Pariwar told? (asked with your Q6, 21 November — not answered)

| | Option | Who learns what | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Nobody is told**; the membership simply ends and the records show why. | Only staff who look. | That Pariwar's admins may not know why a member left. |
| **B** | **That Pariwar's Pariwar Admin is told** (our suggestion). | One admin per Pariwar. | One more person learns of a fraud finding made elsewhere. |
| **C** | **That Pariwar's members are told.** | Everyone in it. | A fraud finding becomes widely known; the person is named to people who were not involved. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** the person who runs that Pariwar should know why a member is gone, and no one else needs to.

## F6 — The words staff see for a claim closed because the member is alive (asked with your Q8, 21 November — not confirmed)

Helpline staff will see such a claim as ***"Closed — the member was found alive"***. What staff tell a caller is a disclosure, and you
chose the staff wording for the other kind of closure in October.

| | Option | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Confirm these words** (our suggestion). | A caller who asks is told plainly that the member is alive — which may be the filer who lied. |
| **B** | **"Closed — please refer to a District Admin"** | Staff cannot answer an honest family's question themselves. |

## F7 — After a real death, is the person who filed investigated separately? (asked with your Q4, 21 November — the second half)

You chose that the helpline operator or the District Admin raises it and a different person finds. The note also asked whether the
finding — *"the person who filed took part / did not take part"* — is a **separate investigation** of that person, ⛔ a third outcome
added to the finding about the member.

| | Option | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes — a separate investigation of the person who filed**, with two findings: took part / did not (our suggestion). | A second kind of investigation to build. |
| **B** | **A third outcome on the member's investigation.** | It breaks your September rule *"two outcomes only"*, and records the finding against the member who died. |

## F8 — Staff notes about a member found INNOCENT: deleted with the family's details? (asked with your Q3, 21 November — not answered)

**The one fact:** the investigation's notes may describe the member and the person who filed. The finding itself ("innocent") is what
frees the member.

| | Option | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The notes are deleted after counsel's period, like the family's details; the finding is kept** (our suggestion). | If the same person is reported again, staff cannot read what was found the first time — only that the member was found innocent. |
| **B** | **The notes are kept for as long as claim records are kept.** | Notes about an innocent member, and perhaps a non-member, are kept for years — against *"only the guilty are kept"*. |

**Our reading on F6–F8:** **A** each — **because** each keeps your earlier rulings whole.
