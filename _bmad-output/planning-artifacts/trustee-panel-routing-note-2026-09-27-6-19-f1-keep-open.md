# The question

**In one sentence:** When a claim reaches the Super Admin, must the Super Admin **decide it** — close, refuse or approve — or may they
also **keep it open** and give the family more time?

## First — which claims reach the Super Admin? (the clarification asked for)

⭐ **Only one kind, and it is rare.** ⚠ These claims go to the **Super Admin**, ⛔ **not** to the Trustee Panel — the Panel is not in
this chain at all. A claim gets there only if **every one** of these happened, in this order:

| Step | What happened | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A member died, a claim was filed, and the death was checked. | The family / the helpline |
| 2 | The **District Admin approved** the claim. | District Admin |
| 3 | The **Pariwar Admin did ⛔ not approve** it and **sent it back** with a note — almost always because **the name on the bank account does not match the nominee's name**, and that is ⛔ not a small clerical difference. | Pariwar Admin |
| 4 | For **90 days** the family was reminded (daily, then twice a week, then weekly), and **posted a letter** if their phone was dead — and **never sent the corrected bank details.** | The system and the District Admin |
| 5 | The **District Admin asked to close** the claim for no response. | District Admin |
| 6 | The **Pariwar Admin said no** — declined to close it, with a note — and it went to the Super Admin. | Pariwar Admin |

⭐ **So the Super Admin is looking at a death the Trust has already accepted as real, and a claim held up by one thing: the bank
account the money would go to.** The family is probably owed the money; the doubt is **where to pay it**, and the family has gone
silent. And the Pariwar Admin — who knows the district — has said *"do not close this."*

**What the Super Admin can already do** (your answers `-251`, `-255`):
- **Close** it — the claim ends, ⛔ not appealable.
- **Refuse it for another reason** — appealable once.
- **Approve it despite the name problem** — the money goes to the account on file, with ⛔ no further check.
Each with a written note.

**While the claim sits with the Super Admin, nothing chases the family** — the reminders stopped at day 90.

## The one fact that decides it

**Because the Super Admin can already approve and pay, "keep open" is only for one situation: the Super Admin believes the family is
owed the money but does ⛔ not trust the account on file, and hopes the family will still come back with the right one.** The question
is whether that hope is allowed — and, if so, for how long.

# What you are choosing between

| | Option | What happens to the family | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **No keep-open.** The Super Admin must close, refuse or approve. | The claim **ends** at the Super Admin — paid, refused or closed. | If the Super Admin doubts the account, they must choose between **paying a doubtful account** and **closing a genuine claim**. There is no "wait". |
| **B** | **Keep open, with a new 90 days.** The reminders and letters start again from day 1. At the end, the District Admin may ask to close again — and it may come back to the Super Admin again. | The family gets **another full 90 days** of reminders. | It **can repeat without end**: every "keep open" starts another 90 days. A claim nobody wants to close could wait for **years**. |
| **C** | **Keep open, with nothing more.** No new period, no reminders. The claim waits until the family sends the correction. | Nothing happens unless the family comes back **on their own**. | **Nobody chases the family**, and the claim can wait **forever** — the outcome you ruled against when you set the 90 days. |
| **D** *(new)* | **Keep open ONCE.** One more 90 days, with reminders and letters. At its end the claim returns to the Super Admin, who **must then decide** — close, refuse or approve; ⛔ no second keep-open. | The family gets **one more full chance**, and the claim still **ends** — at most about **180 days** after it was first sent back, plus the time the admins take. | Longer than A — about three more months before the family is paid or closed. More reminders, and possibly a second letter. |

**Our reading: D** — **because** it is the only option that both lets the Super Admin give a genuine family one more chance **and**
still guarantees the claim ends. A forces a hard choice at once; B can loop for years; C leaves the family unchased, which your 90-day
ruling was made to prevent. **Cost, honestly:** up to about three more months of waiting for a family who is in fact owed the money.

⚠ Our first note suggested **A**. We now suggest **D**, because the first note did not show why a Super Admin would want to wait at
all — and once that is clear (a genuine claim, a doubtful account), a single, time-limited wait is the fairest answer.

# In plain English

Only one kind of claim reaches the Super Admin: a death the Trust has already accepted, where the Pariwar Admin sent the claim back
because the bank account's name did not match the nominee's, the family then stayed silent for 90 days despite reminders and letters,
the District Admin asked to close it, and the Pariwar Admin refused. So the Super Admin is looking at a family who is probably owed the
money, but whose bank account is in doubt.

The Super Admin can already close it, refuse it, or pay it anyway. "Keep open" would be for the case where they want to pay, but not to
that account, and hope the family comes back. We see four ways: **no waiting** (a hard choice at once); **another 90 days, as often as
needed** (it can go on for years); **wait with nobody chasing** (it can go on forever); or **one more 90 days, then a final decision**.

We suggest the last: **one more chance, then an end.** If this paragraph and the evidence below ever disagree, **the evidence is the
record.**
