# The question

**In one sentence:** When a claim is sent back for a correction, **who exactly do we contact — and are we allowed to send messages
and letters to a family member who has not agreed to be contacted by us?**

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** Your rulings say the family is reminded, that a letter goes by post when the phone is dead,
and that the claimant's name, phone and address are collected at filing. They did not say **whose agreement** covers this. That is a
question about what the Trust may do with a person's details and whether it may contact them — not a question for the people writing
the software.

## The one fact that decides it

**The person who signs the consent form at filing is the person filing — and a claimant who is neither the filer nor the nominee has
agreed to nothing.** The form the filer ticks covers *"the deceased member's, my, and the nominees' personal information"*; it does not
name a separate claimant, and the legal rule behind it is still waiting for counsel's determination. And the plan the Trust already
approved **does not allow** text messages to anyone who has not joined the app and opted in to WhatsApp — so for a family member
outside the app, **there is no "standard channel" to use.**

---

# What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Message the family anyway** — by text or WhatsApp, on the strength of the consent form the filer already signed. | The nominee and the claimant get reminders, as you ruled. | The claimant **never agreed**; the plan **must be widened** to allow a new kind of text message (a change to the approved scope, and a new registration with the telecom regulator); and counsel has **not** confirmed the filer can agree for others. If counsel says no, we would have contacted people we may not have been allowed to. |
| **B** | **Remind only the District Admin, who telephones the family;** the system sends letters by post when the phone is dead. | The family hears from **a person**, not a machine. Nobody is contacted without a person deciding to. | It does **not** do what you ruled (*"both"*). The District Admin already handles 15–30 files a day, and the only record of contact with the family is what they type in. |
| **C** | **Ask for the agreement at filing.** The filer confirms — in the form — that the claimant and the nominee may be contacted, and their details are captured; then reminders go to those who are covered. | One more confirmation at a time of grief. The family is told up front that we will contact them. | Whether **one person can agree on behalf of another** is precisely the legal question, and it is unanswered. It also adds a step to a form the family finds hard. |

**Our reading: B for now, with C's wording taken to counsel** — **because** it is the only path where **no one is contacted without
a person having decided to**, and the loop still works: a claim that is sent back is chased, the family is reached by the District
Admin, and a dead phone still gets a letter. **Cost, honestly:** the family gets no automatic reminder in the meantime, which is less
than you ruled. ⚠ **This is separable:** if counsel confirms the basis, C then switches on the family reminders without redoing the
work.

### Two smaller points that ride on the same fact

1. **When the claimant is not the nominee — who is contacted, and to whose address?** *Our default:* reminders to **both** people who
   can act; the **letter to the claimant's address** (the person who filed), or the nominee's address when they are the same person.
2. **When two nominees are declared (a 75/25 split) — is an address needed for each?** *Our default:* **yes, one address for each
   declared nominee;** the claimant's details once.

# In plain English

You have ruled that a family whose claim has been sent back should be reminded regularly, and written to by post if their phone is
dead — and that we should now collect the claimant's name, phone and address at filing. What nobody has decided is **whose
permission** we are working under. The form a family ticks names the filer and the nominees, not a separate claimant, and the legal
rule behind it is still with counsel. And the messaging plan we already approved does not allow us to text people who have not joined
the app.

We see three ways forward. **Only the District Admin contacts the family** (safest, but less than you asked for). **Message the family
straight away** (does what you asked, but rests on a permission we cannot show, and needs the approved plan widened). Or **ask for the
permission on the form** (the right long-term answer, but it depends on counsel confirming that one person can agree for another).

We suggest **starting with the District Admin, and taking the wording of the form to counsel**, so nobody is contacted without a
person deciding to, and the family reminders switch on once counsel says yes. If this paragraph and the evidence below ever disagree,
**the evidence is the record.**

- **Which channel reaches a nominee who is not an app member.** `-230` says "standard channel"; the approved plan has none for this person.

## E3 — What the code actually does

⚠ A code comment is a claim, not evidence — each point was traced.
- **No claimant personal data exists.** A claim carries only `claimantActorId` (null on the helpline path; defaulting to null on the member path — the latter inferred) and a relationship label. (E4.7, E4.8)
- **A nominee's address is optional** today (`address: z.string()…optional()`; the column is nullable). Claim filing asks for none. (E4.8)
- **A member declares 1 or 2 nominees** (`nominees: z.array(NomineeDeclareEntry).min(1).max(2)`, a 75/25 split). (E4.9)
- **The alert system addresses a member id** (`Alert.member_id` is a required identifier) and the delivery resolvers are member-only — so a family outside the app has no delivery path. (E4.10)
