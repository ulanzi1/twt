# The questions

**In one sentence:** after today's note was sent, BigDev decided that a person who cannot use DigiLocker should upload a photo of their
PAN card, a photo of their Aadhaar showing only the last four digits, and a selfie — so staff could check them without a meeting; is
that acceptable proof, and how long may the Trust keep the photos?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** today's note asked what staff must see, and every option said nothing is copied or kept. This
option keeps copies. Whatever you accept here is still all the identity checking that member's family will get after the death.

## The one fact that decides it

**This would be the first time the Trust keeps a photo of a living member's identity documents.** Today the only identity picture it
holds is the photo DigiLocker sends. Keeping PAN and Aadhaar copies makes the Trust responsible for protecting them, and the law forbids
it ever to keep a full Aadhaar number — so an Aadhaar photo is acceptable only if the first eight digits are hidden, and the app cannot
yet promise that every photo will be (we are testing whether it can).

# Q1 — adding a fifth option: E

The four options of today's note (A: photo ID shown in person or on a video call; B: in person only; C: plus an employer's letter;
D: DigiLocker only) are unchanged. This adds:

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E** | **Uploaded documents and a selfie.** The person photographs their PAN card and an Aadhaar showing only the last four digits, and takes a selfie, all in the app. A District Admin (members in their district) or the Pariwar Admin (any member) checks that the name and date of birth match across both documents and what the person entered, and that the selfie matches the photos. ⛔ meeting. If anything does not match, staff record "could not confirm" and may ask for a meeting (option A) (our suggestion). | The member never has to meet anyone or arrange a call; it can be done the same day they join. | A borrowed or forged card and a selfie of a photo are easier to pass than a face-to-face check; the Trust holds copies of ID documents (a breach would expose them); an Aadhaar photo that still shows all twelve digits would be unlawful to keep the moment it arrives, so staff must be able to destroy it at once; a member without a PAN card cannot use E. |

**Our reading:** **E, with A as the fallback** — **because** it is the check most members can complete at once, without staff travel, and
the meeting stays available whenever the documents do not settle it. Its cost is that it is weaker than seeing the person, and that the
Trust then holds copies (Q4). You can reject this reading and still use both tables. **Built until you rule: A**, as in today's note — the
upload screens may be built, but ⛔ copy is collected until you rule.

# Q4 — if E is allowed, how long does the Trust keep the photos?

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Only until staff have checked them**, then deleted; the record keeps only what staff saw (the kind of document, the result). | The Trust holds no copy after the check. | If the identity is questioned later — a suspected fraud, or after the death — there is nothing to look at again; the member may be asked to send them again. |
| **B** | **While the person is a member**, deleted when they leave or ask for their data to be erased — except that once a death is reported, nothing is erased (your ruling of 30 October) (our suggestion). | The member's documents are there if staff, the fraud process or the after-death check need them. | The Trust holds ID copies for many members for years; the longer they are kept, the more a breach exposes. |
| **C** | **A fixed period** (for example two years after the check), then deleted whatever happens. | A clear limit the member can be told. | The copy may be gone exactly when it is needed — a death many years after joining. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** a confirmation in life replaces the identity check after the death, so the copies are what staff
would look at if a claim raises a doubt; and your 30 October ruling already keeps a member's record once their death is reported. Its
cost is the holding itself. **Built until you rule:** ⛔ copy is kept (Q1 is built on A).

# In plain English

We first asked you what staff must see before they confirm a typed-in identity, assuming they would meet the member or see them on a
call and keep nothing. BigDev now wants the member to send photos instead: their PAN card, their Aadhaar with only the last four digits
showing, and a selfie, checked by the District Admin or the Pariwar Admin. We suggest you allow it, with a meeting as the fallback when
the photos do not settle it (Q1 E), and that the Trust keep the photos while the person is a member (Q4 B). It is weaker than meeting
the person and it means the Trust holds ID copies, which it must protect.

# EVIDENCE — for the record, ⛔ not for the meeting

## E1 — What is already ratified or binding, verbatim

> **Aadhaar Act 2016 (as amended), s.8A(4)(b)** (law, ⛔ a ruling): an offline verification-seeking entity shall not *"collect, use, or
> store an Aadhaar number or biometric information of any individual for any purpose"*. · s.8A(3)(c): it must inform the person of
> *"alternatives to submission of information requested for, if any."*

> **`2026-10-30-423` Q1 (a) A** (Trustee-ratified): once a member's death is reported, their personal data is never erased — the
> entry's title: *"ONCE A MEMBER'S DEATH IS REPORTED, THEIR PERSONAL DATA IS ⛔ NEVER ERASED (Q1 (a) A — THE CONSENT WAS GIVEN WHILE
> THEY WERE ALIVE …)"*. Q4 B relies on it.

> **`2026-10-31-424` Q1 (a) C** (Trustee-ratified): *"A claim for someone who typed in their own identity details, never verified
> since, is filed — but the identity is checked first, by staff, from the death documents and the details on record, before any drive
> opens."*

## E2 — What is NOT ratified

- The upload route itself — BigDev's author-commit `2026-11-21-482` decision 1, ⛔ a ruling.
- Storing ID copies at all, and for how long — ⛔ ruling (Q4).
- The regulation read: Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Regulations 2021, reg 16A(3) — an offline verification-seeking
  entity may store offline Aadhaar data with consent, securely (session reading, 2026-11-03; ⛔ counsel's).

## E3 — What the code actually does

- ⛔ document upload for identity: the typed-in route is `POST /api/v1/member/kyc/manual` (name, date of birth, optional photo).
- The only identity picture held is the DigiLocker photo, stored encrypted (`packages/domain/src/schema/member_kyc_profiles.ts`,
  `photo_ciphertext`).
- Upload storage exists for other purposes (claim documents, help-desk attachments, payment screenshots, bank statements —
  `packages/platform-adapters/src/*-storage/`); an identity-document store would be new (row `3-16`).
- Row `3-16-document-upload-identity-when-digilocker-unavailable` (backlog) is the build; its first task tests the digit masking.
