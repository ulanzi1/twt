# The question

**In one sentence:** When a claim reaches the Super Admin, must the Super Admin **decide it at once** — close, refuse or approve — or
may they **hold it while they look into it**, meet the Pariwar Admin and the District Admin, and ask them to do things first?

## First — which claims reach the Super Admin? (the clarification asked for)

⭐ **Only one kind, and it is rare.** ⚠ These claims go to the **Super Admin** (the Trust's senior administrator in the system),
⛔ **not** to the Trustee Panel. A claim gets there only if **every one** of these happened, in this order:

| Step | What happened | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A member died, a claim was filed, and the death was checked. | The family / the helpline |
| 2 | The **District Admin approved** the claim. | District Admin |
| 3 | The **Pariwar Admin did ⛔ not approve** it and **sent it back** with a note — almost always because **the name on the bank account does not match the nominee's name**, and that is ⛔ not a small clerical difference. | Pariwar Admin |
| 4 | For **90 days** the family was reminded (daily, then twice a week, then weekly), and **posted a letter** if their phone was dead — and **never sent the corrected bank details.** | The system and the District Admin |
| 5 | The **District Admin asked to close** the claim for no response. | District Admin |
| 6 | The **Pariwar Admin said no** — declined to close it, with a note — and it went to the Super Admin. | Pariwar Admin |

⭐ **So the Super Admin is looking at a death the Trust has already accepted as real, and a claim held up by one thing: the bank
account the money would go to.** The family is probably owed the money; the doubt is **where to pay it**, and the family has gone
silent. And the **two admins disagree** — the District Admin wants it closed, the Pariwar Admin does not.

**What the Super Admin can already do** (your answers `-251`, `-255`): **close** it (⛔ not appealable), **refuse it for another reason**
(appealable once), or **approve it despite the name problem** (paid to the account on file, ⛔ no further check) — each with a written
note.

**While the claim sits with the Super Admin, nothing chases the family** — the reminders stopped at day 90.

## The one fact that decides it

**A Super Admin cannot responsibly decide a claim like this the moment it arrives.** The two admins disagree, the family is silent,
and the account is in doubt. Before deciding, the Super Admin will usually need to **meet the Pariwar Admin and the District Admin**, hear
why they disagree, and often **ask one of them to do something first** — visit the family, try another number, check the account with the
bank. ⭐ The software today has **no way to hold a claim "under review"**, and **no way to record** what the Super Admin asked the admins
to do or what they did. That is what F1 must settle.

# What you are choosing between

| | Option | What happens to the family | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Decide at once.** The Super Admin must close, refuse or approve; ⛔ no holding. | The claim ends quickly. | ⚠ **Unworkable in practice**: it leaves no time to meet the admins or ask for anything. A Super Admin would either decide blind or leave the claim sitting with ⛔ no record of why. |
| **B** | **Keep open, with a new 90 days** of reminders to the family; it may come back to the Super Admin again. | Another full 90 days of reminders. | It **can repeat without end**, and it is about waiting for the **family** — it gives the Super Admin ⛔ no way to ask the admins for anything. |
| **C** | **Keep open, with nothing more.** | Nothing happens unless the family comes back on their own. | **Nobody chases anyone**, and nothing is recorded — the claim can wait **forever**. |
| **D** | **Keep open ONCE:** one more 90 days of reminders, then the Super Admin must decide. | One more full chance, and the claim still ends. | Again only about waiting for the **family**; ⛔ no way to ask the admins to act, and a fixed 90 days whether or not the review needs it. |
| **E** *(new — recommended)* | **A review period.** The Super Admin places the claim **under review**, with a note. While under review: nothing is paid, closed or refused; the Super Admin may **meet the admins** and **record directions** to the Pariwar Admin or the District Admin (for example *"visit the family"*, *"confirm the account with the bank"*, *"restart the reminders"*), and each admin **records what they did**. The review ends only with a decision — close, refuse or approve — with a note. ⛔ No fixed deadline, but the Super Admin is **reminded every 30 days** while the claim is under review. | The claim is looked into properly; the family may be contacted again if the Super Admin directs it. Every step is recorded, so the family's history shows what the Trust did. | ⚠ **No guaranteed end date** — a review can run long if the Super Admin does not act; the 30-day reminder is the only prompt. More work for the admins, each direction recorded. |

**Our reading: E** — **because** it matches how such a claim would really be handled: the Super Admin needs time, needs to hear both
admins, and may need them to act before deciding — and E lets all of that happen **on the record**. ⭐ E also covers what D was for: if
the Super Admin wants the family chased again, they **direct** it. **Cost, honestly:** there is ⛔ no hard deadline; a claim under review
ends only when the Super Admin decides.

### Two details of E, if you choose it

1. **A deadline?** *Our default:* ⛔ **no hard deadline**, but a **reminder to the Super Admin every 30 days** while a claim is under
   review. *If you would rather:* a deadline (say 90 days), after which — what? There is no one above the Super Admin in the system to
   escalate to, so a deadline would need you to name who is told.
2. **Must the admins do what the Super Admin directs?** *Our default:* **yes** — a direction is recorded against the admin it names,
   they are reminded of it, and they record what they did. ⚠ **This gives the Super Admin a new power: to direct the District Admin and
   the Pariwar Admin on a claim.** Your earlier rulings gave the Super Admin a decision, ⛔ not a power to direct.

# In plain English

Only one kind of claim reaches the Super Admin: a death the Trust has already accepted, where the Pariwar Admin sent the claim back
because the bank account's name did not match the nominee's, the family then stayed silent for 90 days despite reminders and letters,
the District Admin asked to close it, and the Pariwar Admin refused. So the Super Admin is looking at a family who is probably owed the
money, a bank account in doubt, and two admins who disagree.

Nobody can decide that on the spot. The Super Admin will want to talk to both admins and may ask one of them to do something first —
visit the family, try another number, check the account. Today the system has no way to hold a claim while that happens, or to record
it.

We suggest a **review period**: the Super Admin marks the claim as under review, records what they ask each admin to do, the admins
record what they did, and the review ends only with a decision — close, refuse or pay — with a note. There is no fixed deadline, but the
Super Admin is reminded every 30 days. Two things we ask you to confirm: that there is no hard deadline, and that the Super Admin may
direct the two admins in this way. If this paragraph and the evidence below ever disagree, **the evidence is the record.**
