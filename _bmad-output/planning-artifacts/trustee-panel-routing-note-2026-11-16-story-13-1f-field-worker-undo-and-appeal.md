# Question 1 — the time a worker was wrongly ended: are teachers who joined then credited?

**In one sentence:** when a wrongly ended worker is restored, are the teachers who joined with their code WHILE they were ended credited to
them?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it is what the Trust owes a worker, and your 2026-11-07 ruling returned it to you with the work
that builds the undo.

## The one fact that decides it

**Nothing credits any worker for any teacher yet** — the part of the system that will record credit has not been built. So today the
question costs nobody anything; it decides what that later work builds. As built, the record of a teacher who joined in the ended window
says the worker was ended then — not credited.

## What you are choosing between (Question 1)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Not credited.** The code was off while the worker was ended. *(As built today.)* | A worker ended by someone else's mistake loses the credit for that window. | The worker bears the cost of an officer's mistake. |
| **B** | **Held, and credited if the ending is undone** — by mistake or on appeal. *Our suggestion*, by analogy to your ruling for a worker suspended under review ("held, and credited if cleared"), which you did not extend to endings. | The worker loses nothing for an officer's mistake. | The credit work must hold credits for every ended worker's code for as long as the ending could be undone (with no time limit, for ever); and it narrows the product rule "new attributions on a deactivated code are rejected" for an ended worker (the rule would be annotated, not rewritten). |
| **C** | **Held, and credited only if undone within a set time** (for example 90 days). | As B, inside the time. | A worker restored after the cut-off loses the window through no fault of their own; the holding is still built; it narrows the same rule. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** a slip should cost the worker nothing, and it matches what you ruled for a worker under review. But
nothing depends on it today: as built the answer is A until the credit work builds yours.

# Question 2 — the appeal's rules: a time limit, and how many appeals

**In one sentence:** may a worker appeal at any time, and only once for each ending?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it limits what a worker may ask for; your ruling left the time limit open.

## The one fact that decides it

**A worker may not learn of an ending at once** — they have no login and the system sends them nothing — so any time limit runs from a date
the worker may not know. Members, under the Niyamavali, may appeal again after a refusal (one open at a time) with no deadline.

## What you are choosing between (Question 2)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **No time limit; one appeal per ending** (a withdrawn appeal does not count). *Our suggestion; as built.* | A worker can appeal whenever they learn of the ending, once. | Narrower than members get: a worker refused once is restored only if a Pariwar Admin later finds the ending was a mistake; an appeal can arrive years later. |
| **B** | **A time limit** (for example 30 days from the ending) **and one appeal.** | Appeals come while the facts are fresh. | A worker who learns of the ending late loses the appeal; a date rule to build. |
| **C** | **No limit, and a refused appeal may be brought again** (as for members). | The worker can always ask again. | The same ending can be argued again and again. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** a worker who is not told cannot be held to a deadline, and one decision per ending keeps the record
clear. Changing to B or C before going live is a small change.

# Question 3 — what the worker is told, and by whom

**In one sentence:** (a) who tells the worker of an undo or of an appeal's outcome; and (b) when a worker is ended, are they told that they
may appeal, and to whom?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** your ruling said "what the worker is told" is yours.

## The one fact that decides it

**The system has no way to reach a field worker today.** They have no app login, and a text message needs a wording approved by the
telecom regulator first (the code text is still waiting for that approval). You ruled for move requests (2026-11-08) that the officer tells
the worker, reminded by the screen.

## What you are choosing between (Question 3 (a) — an undo or an outcome)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The officer tells the worker**: the officer who recorded the appeal tells them its outcome; after a mistake is undone, the officer who undid it. The screen reminds them: "Tell the worker." *Our suggestion; as built.* | The worker hears from an officer. | It rests on officers; nothing records that the worker was told. |
| **B** | **Nothing is required, and no reminder.** | — | A worker may never learn that they were restored. |
| **C** | **A text message.** | The worker hears even if the officer forgets. | A new wording to approve first, and its delay. |

## What you are choosing between (Question 3 (b) — at an ending)

| **C** | **A text message at the ending.** | As A, by the system. | As (a) C; and it reaches a worker the Trust has just ended. |

**Our reading:** **A** for both — **because** it is the shape you ruled for move requests, and nothing else can reach a worker today.

# Question 4 — who hears, in four cases your ruling did not name

**In one sentence:** who hears an appeal when (a) the officer who ended the worker was the Pariwar's only Pariwar Admin, or also the
worker's District Admin; (b) a super admin ended the worker; (c) may a super admin hear an appeal that the Pariwar Admin hears; and (d) every
trustee with an account in the Pariwar is the officer who ended the worker?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** who judges a decision about a person. You named the plain cases only.

## The one fact that decides it

**Your ruling sends a Pariwar Admin's ending to you so that nobody hears an appeal against their own act.** Each case below asks how far that
reason reaches. The system fixes the hearer at the moment of the ending, so a later change in someone's role does not move it.

## What you are choosing between (Question 4)

| | Case | Option A — *our suggestion, as built* | Cost of A | Option B | Cost of B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | The ender was the Pariwar's ONLY Pariwar Admin, or held both the Pariwar Admin and the District Admin role. (The system records such an officer as a District Admin; we count them as a Pariwar Admin.) | **You hear it** — confirm. Your ruling left the only-Pariwar-Admin case to us to propose. | You hear these appeals too. | The Pariwar Admin hears it. | An officer hears the appeal against their own ending. |
| **(b)** | A super admin ended the worker. | **You hear it** — a super admin stands above the Pariwar Admin. | It departs from your ruling's words ("the Pariwar Admin hears it" covers every ender who is not a Pariwar Admin), and you hear these too. | The Pariwar Admin hears it, as the words read. | An officer judges a higher officer's act. |

**Our reading:** **A** in all four — **because** each keeps an appeal away from the officer who made the ending, and (b) and (c) were built
that way ahead of your answer; going live waits for it.

# Question 5 — four permissions, and how you record

**In one sentence:** do you confirm four new permissions, held as below, and that one trustee's own console account records the Panel's
decision on an appeal you hear?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** your earlier decision says field-worker permissions are confirmed by you when their feature ships.

## The one fact that decides it

**You ruled who does each of these; the permissions turn it into switches.** And your ruling's accepted cost says an appeal is "recorded by
the officer who hears it" — for an appeal you hear, that is a trustee, signed in as themselves. The Trustee Panel role already acts this
way in the console for members' appeals and restorations; it was set up for members and is itself provisional. Reading the appeals shows
the workers' names to trustees, with every look recorded.

| Permission | Held by |
|---|---|
| Undo an ending | the Pariwar Admin only |
| Hear an appeal the Pariwar Admin hears | the Pariwar Admin |
| Hear an appeal the Panel hears | the Trustee Panel only (no other role without your decision) |
| Read the appeals | the District Admin of the worker's district, the Pariwar Admin, the Trustee Panel |

A super admin holds all four by the rule that it holds every permission (as you confirmed for Story 13.1c's five) — Question 4 (c) depends
on this; but a super admin never acts as the Panel.

## What you are choosing between (Question 5)

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Confirm, as built.** *Our suggestion.* | The record shows which hearer acted, and a trustee records your decision. | One trustee's console act stands for the Panel's decision; each Pariwar needs a trustee with an account who did not end the worker. |
| **B** | **Confirm the permissions, but your decision is recorded only with the date you ruled.** | The record carries the ruling date too. | One more field, and a rule the system cannot check. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it is your ruling turned into permissions, and the record tells which hearer acted.
