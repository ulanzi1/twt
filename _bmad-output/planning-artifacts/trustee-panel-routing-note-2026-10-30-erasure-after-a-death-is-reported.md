# Q1 — once a member's death is reported, may their personal data be erased?

## Q1 (a) — the rule

**In one sentence:** when someone reports that a member has died (a claim is filed for their death), should the member's personal data
stop being erasable from that moment?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** you have kept death certificates even against an erasure request, and accepted that a deceased
member's record is kept — but you have never ruled whether the member's record itself may be erased after death. Our own earlier record says
it may.

## The one fact that decides it

**Today the system lets it happen, and the claim then runs blind.** Nothing checks for a reported death before erasing. If a member's
record is erased while a claim for their death is open, the claim is ⛔ closed: it stalls at the step that decides who is paid (the date of
death is gone), while the donation drive for the family stays open and keeps collecting — under no name. A donation text could even show the
placeholder word the system uses for an erased name.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Never erased once a death is reported** (our suggestion). From the moment a claim is filed for the member's death, an erasure request is refused, for good — unless the report is closed as false (Q1 (b)). The reason: the member agreed to how their data is used while they were alive; that agreement is what the claim, the drive and the donors rely on, and no one can withdraw it on their behalf afterwards. | The family's claim, the drive and the donors' record always stay tied to a named member. | Nobody — not the nominee, not the family — can have a dead member's record erased; it is kept indefinitely. Whether the law allows the Trust to refuse is counsel's to confirm (see Q2). |
| **B** | **Erasure allowed; the claim carries on** (what the system does today). | The record is erased; the claim and the drive continue without the member's details. | The claim cannot be completed (who is paid can no longer be decided), yet the drive keeps raising money for a person no one can name. |
| **C** | **Erasure allowed, and it closes the claim.** | Erasing the record ends the family's claim. | The family loses the Trust's help because of an erasure request — possibly made by someone acting in the dead member's name. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** an erasure request after death can only come from someone other than the member, and the claim and the
donors depend on the record. A also replaces an earlier record of ours (never put to you) that said a deceased member's record may be erased.

## Q1 (b) — a false report

**In one sentence:** if a death was reported falsely (the member is alive), may the member ask for erasure again once the report is closed?

**The one fact that decides it:** under Q1 (a) A, anyone filing a false death report would otherwise take away a living member's right to
erasure for ever.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Yes — once the report is closed because the member is alive** (our suggestion), erasure is possible again. | A living member is never stuck with someone else's false report. | While the false report is open, the member cannot have their data erased. |
| **B** | **No — never again, once any death report was filed.** | Simplest rule. | A living member loses a right permanently because of someone else's lie. |

**Our reading:** **A.**

# Q2 — what may the person the member nominated do with the record after death?

**In one sentence:** the Trust's draft privacy policy tells members they may *"nominate a person to exercise your rights in the event of
death"*; if you choose Q1 (a) A, which of those rights should that person have?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** the promise is in the member's privacy policy, and Q1 (a) A takes erasure out of it.

**The one fact that decides it:** this nomination is a right the data-protection law gives members; how far the Trust may limit it is
counsel's to say. What the Trust WANTS is yours.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The nominated person may see the record and ask for a correction, but ⛔ erase it** (our suggestion, matching Q1 (a) A); the privacy policy is changed to say so before members agree to it. | The family can check and correct what the Trust holds; the record stays. | If counsel says the law gives the nominated person a right to erase, A must change. |
| **B** | **The nominated person may also erase — but only after the claim is fully closed** (paid, or finally refused). | The family can have the record erased once the Trust's help is over. | The drive and the donors' history then lose the member's name; the certificates stay anyway (your September ruling). |
| **C** | **The nominated person may erase at any time.** | Fullest control for the family. | Undoes Q1 (a) A. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it is the only option consistent with Q1 (a) A; counsel then checks it.

# Q3 — whose death may a claim be filed for?

**In one sentence:** should the Trust refuse to open a claim for someone who, at their death, was not a member — someone who never finished
joining, who had left, who had been removed, or whose record had been erased?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it decides whose families the Trust helps.

## The one fact that decides it

**Today a claim can be filed for any of these people, and a drive could then open.** The helpline checks only that the person's member
number exists. Someone who signed up with their phone but never completed the identity check has no name on record at all — the system shows
a blank where their name should be.

⛔ **This question does ⛔ touch** members who are full members but cannot claim for a while — in their first days after joining, behind on
contributions, or suspended. Your existing rules decide their eligibility; nothing here changes them.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Recorded and refused, with the reason, before any drive opens** (our suggestion): a claim for someone who at death had not finished joining (⛔ identity check), had left the Trust, had been removed, or had been erased is kept on record, refused, and the reason given. | The family gets a clear answer and the usual route to appeal; no drive opens. | More work for staff on each such case. |
| **B** | **Refused at the door — ⛔ recorded.** | The helpline tells the caller the claim cannot be filed. | No record and no appeal; a mistyped member number turns a real family away silently. |
| **C** | **As today — the claim runs.** | No change. | A drive can raise money for someone who was not a member — or who has no name on record. |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** the family still gets an answer they can challenge, and no money is raised in a non-member's name.

# In plain English

When a member dies and their family asks the Trust for help, the claim, the donation drive and every donor's record rest on that member's
details. Today the system would let those details be erased in the middle of a claim — the claim then stalls while the drive keeps collecting
for a nameless person. We suggest a simple rule: once a death is reported, the member's record is never erased, because the member agreed to
its use while alive and no one can take that back for them — except where the report turns out to be false and the member is alive. The
person the member nominated can still see and correct the record. Separately, the Trust should not open a claim for someone who was not a
member when they died: the claim should be recorded and refused with a reason, before any drive opens. Counsel then confirms the law.
