# R1 — "member also sees the memorial page": what should the members' app show?

**In one sentence:** you added that members should also see the memorial without logging out; should the members' app show the memorial
itself — the family's words, photo and list — or should it link to the public memorial page?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it decides where a family's words, photo and children's names appear, and next to what.

### The one fact that decides it

The public memorial page has ⛔ no sign-in at all — anyone with the link opens it, member or not, so ⛔ nothing today makes a member log
out to see it. What a member could ⛔ not do is see the memorial **inside the app**. ⇒ Your words fit two different builds, and they
differ in one thing: **the app's drive screen shows the member's own name**, and the public page does ⛔ not yet name the member at all.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** ⭐ | **The app shows the memorial itself.** The drive screen in the members' app shows the family's words, photo and family list, beside the member's name — only for memorials sent with the new permission (which tells the family it appears in the app) | A member reads the memorial where they already are | Children's names and faces sit beside the dead member's name, which the public page does ⛔ not do; one more place the family's material appears, kept in step with the public page; memorials sent earlier — whose families agreed to the public page only — never appear in the app |
| **B** | **The app links to the public page.** The drive screen gets a button that opens the public memorial page; ⛔ nothing of the memorial is shown inside the app | A member opens the memorial with one tap | The member leaves the app to read it; nothing new is disclosed; the new permission at Send says *"and to members in the app"*, so its wording changes before launch |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it is how BigDev, who relayed your ruling, understood it: members see the memorial *page*, in the
app, without going elsewhere. If you meant B, we change one sentence of the permission and build a link instead.

# R2 — Does the declaration cover the adults a family names in its own words, or only those on the list?

**In one sentence:** the declaration you ruled has two halves — the guardian half for children, and *"every adult I have named has agreed
to be named"*; does that adult half reach the people a family mentions in its own words, or only the people on the family list?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it decides what a grieving family declares when it presses Send.

### The one fact that decides it

In October you ruled that living adults named in the family's words *"appear as written"* — ⛔ nobody's agreement asked. Your addendum
ruling extended the declaration to the words for **children**; it did ⛔ not say whether the adult half goes with it.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Everywhere:** the family declares that every adult it names — in the list **or** in its words — has agreed | One rule for the whole page | A family thanking a doctor, a teacher or the village head in its words is declaring something it has ⛔ not asked — so it must ask them, leave them out, or declare falsely; the reviewer cannot tell which names in free writing agreed |
| **B** ⭐ | **The list only:** adults on the family list have agreed; adults named in the words appear as written, as you ruled in October | The family's words stay as free as they are today | Two rules on one page — the list's adults were asked, the words' adults were ⛔ not; the permission's sentence changes to *"every adult you have named in the family list"* |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** your October ruling on the words stands, the addendum asked only about children in the words, and a
family should ⛔ not be asked to declare something about everyone it thanks.

# R3 — When the family has given the declaration, when may the reviewer still leave out a child?

**In one sentence:** after the declaration, may the Trust's reviewer still leave out a child's name or photo — and if so, on what?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it decides when a child's name or face is withheld from an open page on the Trust's judgment
rather than the family's word.

### The one fact that decides it

The declaration covers *"every child I have named"*, and the reviewer cannot check it. So the only question is whether, and on what,
the reviewer may still leave a child out once it is given.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** ⭐ | **Only on a concrete reason:** the reviewer leaves out a child only where something in the memorial itself shows the declaration cannot cover that child, and the family is told why | A child's name stays unless the memorial itself contradicts the declaration | A judgment — two reviewers may decide differently; the family may disagree and send again |
| **B** | **Never:** the declaration is taken as given; the child reason applies only to memorials sent without one | Families get exactly what they declared | A plainly impossible declaration (a neighbour's child, named without a word of agreement) stays up until someone tells the Trust |
| **C** | **Ask first:** where the reviewer doubts it, the Trust asks the family through the app before deciding | The family explains before anything is left out | A new step and more waiting in a grief flow; more to build |

**Our reading:** **A** — **because** it keeps the family's word as the rule and the Trust's judgment for the plain exceptions only.
