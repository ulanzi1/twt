# The question

**In one sentence:** Until the Government formally classes the Trust as a large data holder, who is the one named person responsible
when members' data is exposed — and does the Trust appoint a Data Protection Officer now, or only when the law requires one?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** it puts a duty with a legal deadline on a named individual, and it decides whose name and
contact the Trust publishes to members as the person who answers about their data. Only the Panel can name that person.

## The one fact that decides it

The law requires a **Data Protection Officer only of organisations the Government specifically names** as large or sensitive data
holders — and it has named none so far, as far as we could find. But it requires **every** organisation, the Trust included, to do two
things from the day the law starts to apply (May 2027): **report every exposure of personal data** — to each affected person without
delay, and to the regulator with a detailed report inside 72 hours — and **publish the contact of a person who can answer** members'
questions about their data.

So a Data Protection Officer is optional for the Trust today; a named, reachable person is not. **Today the Trust has named no one**:
the published-contact person has never been designated, and our draft privacy policy gives every one of these duties to "the Data
Protection Officer" — a post nobody holds.

# What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Appoint a Data Protection Officer before the app goes live, although the law does not yet require one. | A member sees one named officer in the privacy policy from day one, exactly as the draft is written. That officer runs the response if data is exposed. | The Trust must find, now, an individual based in India who answers to the Board of Trustees and has the time and independence for the role. No candidate has been identified. The Trust takes on the role's weight years before the law may ever ask for it. |
| **B** | Name ONE person now as both the published contact and the person responsible for an exposure. Appoint a Data Protection Officer only if the Government names the Trust (or earlier, if counsel advises it). | A member sees a named contact person (not called "Data Protection Officer") in the privacy policy. If data is exposed, that same person tells the members and the regulator. | Two acts by the Panel now: naming that person as the contact, and making them responsible for an exposure. Every place the draft privacy policy says "Data Protection Officer" must be reworded before members agree to it. If the Government later names the Trust, a 12-month clock for a formal data audit starts with no Data Protection Officer yet in post. |
| **C** | The Panel's chair is responsible for an exposure until the Government names the Trust. | If data is exposed, the chair tells the members and the regulator. The published contact for everyday questions may be a different person. | A trustee personally carries a 72-hour legal duty that can arrive on any day. Members may meet two different names — one for questions, one after an exposure. Whether a trustee may hold this role at all is a question we are putting to counsel; it is not answered. |

Under every option a contact must be published by the time the law applies.

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** the Trust needs a named, reachable person in any case, the law does not ask the Trust for a Data
Protection Officer today, and one person for both duties is the simplest thing a member can be told. It is separable: if you prefer A or
C, the register, the clock, the drafts and the procedures being built work unchanged — they leave the person's name blank until you
rule.

# In plain English

If a member's personal details are ever exposed — a wrong person sees them, a file goes astray, a system is broken into — the law will
require the Trust to tell that member quickly and plainly, and to report to the Government's data regulator within three days. This
applies to every organisation, however small.

The law also has a special role, a "Data Protection Officer", but only organisations the Government singles out must have one. The
Trust has not been singled out, and may never be.

What the Trust does need, in every case, is **one named person**: someone members can contact about their data, and someone who takes
charge if data is exposed. Nobody holds that role today, and our draft privacy policy promises an officer who does not exist.

We suggest the Panel names one person for both duties now, and appoints a formal Data Protection Officer only if the Government
requires it or counsel advises it (option B). It is the least the law asks, it gives members one clear name, and it does not load the
Trust with a formal office it may never need. The cost is that the Panel must choose that person, and the privacy policy must be
reworded before members agree to it.

Nothing is waiting on your answer except going live.
