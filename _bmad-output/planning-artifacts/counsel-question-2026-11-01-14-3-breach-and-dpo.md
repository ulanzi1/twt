## The situation, in brief

**Nothing below is live yet** — the Trust's app is not in production and holds no real member's data.

The Trust is a public charitable trust running a teachers' mutual-aid scheme. Its app will hold, for each member: identity and contact
details, KYC details derived from Aadhaar (never the full Aadhaar number), a nominee's details, payment history, and — when a member
dies — a death certificate and the claim's papers. It also holds a name and mobile number for each field worker.

We have read the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)) in the
Gazette. Our own understanding — **not legal advice** — is:

- **Reporting a breach binds every Data Fiduciary**, not only a Significant one: section 8(6) of the Act and Rule 7. Each affected
  person is told "without delay"; the Board is told "without delay" and given a detailed report "within seventy-two hours of becoming
  aware of the breach". There is no threshold of seriousness — Rule 7 speaks of "any personal data breach".
- **A Data Protection Officer is required only of a Significant Data Fiduciary** (sections 2(l) and 10(2)(a)); every Data Fiduciary
  must publish the business contact of "a Data Protection Officer, if applicable, or a person who is able to answer" (section 8(9)).
- **Significant Data Fiduciary status is by Government notification**, of one fiduciary or a class (section 10(1)); it has no numeric
  threshold. We found no such notification (secondary sources only).
- **These duties start eighteen months after the Rules were published** (Rule 1(4); G.S.R. 843(E) clause (c) for the Act's sections).
  The Gazette issue is dated 13 November 2025 and carries an e-publication number stamped 14 November 2025 — so the date is 13 or 14
  May 2027. We found no amendment to that period (secondary sources only).
- We could not find that the Data Protection Board has appointed members or a way to file an intimation yet.

The Trust is building a register to record a breach, a 72-hour clock, and drafts of the three notices Rule 7 describes; staff send the
notices themselves.

**The Trustee Panel has since ruled** (Decision `2026-11-20-468`): the Trust does not appoint a Data Protection Officer before the
law requires one; ONE named person is both the published contact (section 8(9)) and the person responsible for a breach; a Data
Protection Officer is appointed if the Trust is notified as significant, or earlier on your advice. The Panel named the person; the
capacity they act in is not recorded here — `[BigDev to add before sending: who the named person is to the Trust — a trustee, staff
or an outside professional]`.

## The questions

1. **Does the breach duty bind the Trust, and from when?** (a) Do section 8(6) and Rule 7 bind the Trust as it is constituted — a
   public charitable trust that is neither a company nor notified as significant? (b) Do the eighteen months end on 13 May 2027 or
   14 May 2027? (c) Is there any registration duty for an ordinary Data Fiduciary? (Our planning records assume a "Data Fiduciary
   registration"; we read the Act as registering Consent Managers only.)
2. **CERT-In.** Do the CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 (report within 6 hours of noticing; logs kept 180 days in India) apply to
   the Trust? Their addressees are "service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporate and Government organisations". If
   they apply, is that report additional to the one to the Board? Until you answer, our procedure treats the 6-hour report as owed.
3. **How an affected member may be told.** Rule 7(1) says "through her user account or any mode of communication registered by her".
   Does a notice inside the app (the member's own account) together with a WhatsApp message or a phone call to the number the member
   registered satisfy it? Is an SMS required? May one notice be addressed to all affected members of a Pariwar, or must each be
   addressed individually?
4. **Significant Data Fiduciary exposure, and a voluntary officer.** Given what the Trust holds (Aadhaar-derived KYC, death
   certificates, payment history, nominees), how likely is a notification to reach the Trust or its class, and do you advise
   appointing a Data Protection Officer before the law requires one?
5. **Who may hold the role.** May the person the Panel has named hold both duties — the published contact and the breach owner —
   in the capacity described above? More generally, may a trustee be the Data Protection Officer, or that named person? What independence or conflict limits apply? (The Act asks that the officer be an individual based
   in India and "responsible to the Board of Directors or similar governing body".)
