# The question

**In one sentence:** When a claim is refused because the nominee was changed after the death, and the true nominee files her own claim,
you ruled that her claim waits at final approval *"until the appeal is decided"* — but if the person who was refused **never appeals**,
nothing is ever decided, so how long should she wait?

**Why it cannot be decided without you:** the Trust removed every time limit on appealing a refused claim, so that a grieving family is
never rushed. That promise and your FQ5 ruling now pull against each other: keeping the promise for the refused claimant keeps the true
nominee waiting for ever. Only you can say which gives, and by how much.

## The one fact that decides it

⭐ **A refused claim can be appealed at any time — there is ⛔ no deadline, by design — so "not appealed yet" never turns into "decided".**
If the refused person simply does nothing, the true nominee's claim, under FQ5 as worded, can ⛔ never be approved.

⚠ And the person who does nothing is, in the case FQ5 was written for, the person who changed the nominee after the death — the one
who gains by the true nominee waiting.

# What you are choosing between

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **A time limit to appeal THIS kind of refusal only** — e.g. 90 days from the refusal. After it, the refusal can ⛔ no longer be appealed, and the true nominee's claim may be approved. An appeal filed in time is heard and decided as today. | The true nominee waits at most the time limit (plus any appeal filed in it). The refused person has a fixed time to appeal, and is told the refusal can be appealed. | The **only** refusal with a deadline. Someone refused in good faith (for example, a date of death recorded wrongly) who misses the limit loses their appeal — though their own claim was never the one that would pay them: an allowed appeal still pays whoever was the nominee at the death. You set the number. |
| **B** | **She waits only while an appeal is actually open.** If none has been filed when her claim reaches final approval, it is approved; from then on, the refusal can ⛔ no longer be appealed. | The true nominee waits only for an appeal someone actually made. | The refused person's right to appeal ends — possibly within weeks, and without warning — the moment her claim is approved. This is close to the option you turned down in FQ5 (*"approving the new claim ends the appeal"*). |
| **C** | **She waits until an appeal is decided, however long that is** (FQ5 read as written). | ⛔ No change to anyone's appeal right. | If the refused person never appeals, the true nominee is ⛔ never paid through her claim. Only staff action outside the system could end it. |

**Our reading:** **A**, with **90 days** from the refusal — **because** it keeps your FQ5 ruling whole (her claim still waits for any
appeal actually made, and is closed if that appeal succeeds), keeps the refused person's appeal real and known, and puts an end on the
wait that the refused person cannot extend by doing nothing. 90 days is the period you already gave a family to respond to a correction
before their claim can be closed (`-229`, as amended by `-230` / `-231`); you may prefer another number.

⚠ The Panel can reject this reading and still use the table — each option is built differently but ⛔ none needs another question.

# The second question (Q2)

**In one sentence:** When the refused person's appeal **succeeds** and the true nominee's own claim is therefore **closed** (FQ5), should
the Trust **send her a text** saying so, or is it enough that her claim's status says *"closed — please call the helpline"*?

**The one fact that decides it:** ⭐ a nominee who filed **through the helpline** never sees an app status — without a text she learns her
claim was closed only if she calls. ⚠ And closed is ⛔ not lost: the appeal can only succeed by showing the change came before the death,
and the earlier claim then pays whoever was the nominee at the death — possibly her.

| | Option | What changes for a person | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | ⛔ No text. Her claim shows *"This claim has been closed. Please call the helpline."* in the app and to the helpline. | She finds out when she looks or calls. | A helpline filer may wait without knowing. |
| **B** | A text, once: *"Your claim for [member] has been closed. Please call the helpline."* — accusing no one, naming ⛔ no other person. | She is told promptly and pointed to the helpline. | A second new text: registered first (DLT), and the same counsel question as FQ7 (she never agreed to be contacted); her number may be out of date. |

**Our reading:** **B** — **because** it is FQ7's own shape and reasoning (you chose to text her when the first claim was refused; the
closing of her own claim is the moment she most needs the helpline), and it rides FQ7's counsel check rather than adding a new one. ⭐ Until
you answer, the system builds A (⛔ no text) — nothing is sent that you have not ruled.

# In plain English

**Q1.** When someone files a claim after quietly changing the nominee after the death, the District Admin refuses it. The real nominee then
files her own claim. You said her claim must wait at the very last step until the refused person's appeal is decided — so that the Trust
never pays twice for one death, and never pays the wrong person. But the refused person can appeal at any time, with no deadline, so if
they simply never appeal, the real nominee waits for ever — and the person causing the wait is the one who tried to take the money.

We suggest giving this one kind of refusal a time limit to appeal — 90 days from the refusal. An appeal made in time is heard as usual,
and the real nominee keeps waiting for it; after 90 days with no appeal, her claim can be approved. Nothing changes for any other refused
claim.

**Q2.** If the refused person's appeal succeeds, the real nominee's own claim is closed (the earlier claim goes ahead instead). We suggest
texting her once that her claim was closed and asking her to call the helpline — the same kind of text you already chose when the first
claim was refused — so that someone who filed by phone is not left waiting without knowing.
